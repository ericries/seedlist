## Background

Jean Tardy-Joubert is a Partner at Highland Europe, the London- and Geneva-based growth-stage technology fund that spun out of Highland Capital Partners in 2012 [^1][^2]. He joined Highland Europe in March 2018 as an advisory partner, went full-time, and was promoted to Partner in December 2020 alongside Gajan Rajanathan and Ronan Shally when the firm closed its fourth fund at €700M [^3][^4][^5].

Tardy-Joubert spent roughly 25 years in technology investment banking before moving to venture. He began his career at Deutsche Bank in the early 1990s [^6], later led European technology investment banking at Credit Suisse First Boston and Merrill Lynch, and in 2009 founded and ran the European practice of Frank Quattrone's tech M&A boutique Qatalyst Partners [^6][^7][^1]. Across those roles he advised on landmark European tech transactions including Autonomy's $11.7B sale to HP, Skyscanner's $1.8B sale to Ctrip, SwiftKey's sale to Microsoft, and MatchesFashion's sale to Apax Partners [^3][^7]. He was a director of Qatalyst Partners Limited (UK) from September 2014 until January 2020 [^8].

He was born in Paris in November 1969, is a French national resident in England, and is based in London [^8][^1]. He holds a Magistère Banque Finance from Université Panthéon-Assas (Paris II), completed 1988–1993 [^9].

## Stated Thesis

Tardy-Joubert publicly frames his investing around European growth-stage software with global potential. On joining Highland Europe he said the firm's "people, brand, platform and operating ethos" were the reason he moved from banking to investing, and that European tech leaders would emerge from continental strengths in areas like luxury, design, travel, and automotive [^3].

Highland Europe itself describes its mandate as backing "European-rooted, growth-stage software and consumer internet companies" with more than €10M in annualized revenue, writing cheques between €10M and €70M [^10][^11]. Tardy-Joubert operates inside that mandate rather than publishing a separate personal thesis.

Recurring themes across his portfolio commentary include: (a) software for frontline / deskless / non-corporate workforces [^12][^13], (b) vertical software that embeds a workflow rather than sitting on top of it [^14], and (c) sustainability data infrastructure — particularly around real-estate emissions [^15].

## Inferred Thesis

Sample size is small: only four rounds since his 2018 arrival can be independently tied to Tardy-Joubert personally through direct quotes or verified board appointments (Yoobic, Snapshift/Combo, Deepki, Wordsmith) [^12][^13][^15][^14]. Percentages would be misleading at that size, so patterns are described qualitatively.

- **Stage**: Growth rounds, predominantly Series B and Series C. The four verified deals range from a $45M Series A (Snapshift/Combo, 2022) [^13] to a €150M Series C (Deepki, 2022) [^15]. Median announced round size is roughly $50M–$70M.
- **Geography**: Strongly Europe-rooted. Three of four verified deals are French-headquartered companies (Yoobic, Deepki, Snapshift/Combo) [^12][^15][^13]; the fourth (Wordsmith) is Edinburgh-based [^14]. No US-headquartered lead deals for him personally are verified, though Highland Europe as a firm has co-led rounds in US companies such as Unframe [^16].
- **Sectors**: Two clear clusters — (1) software for frontline/deskless/non-corporate workforces (Yoobic for retail associates, Snapshift/Combo for hospitality shift management) [^12][^13], and (2) vertical B2B software with a compliance or infrastructure angle (Deepki for real-estate ESG data, Wordsmith for in-house legal, and historically Condeco for workplace scheduling) [^15][^14][^17].
- **Co-investor patterns**: Highland Europe frequently rounds out its lead cheques with continental European and specialist investors. Deepki was co-led with One Peak, with participation from Bpifrance, Revaia, Hi Inov, and Statkraft Ventures [^15]. Wordsmith was co-led with Index Ventures [^14]. Yoobic's Series C included Felix Capital and Insight Partners as existing backers [^12]. Snapshift included Bpifrance and UL Invest [^13].
- **Board involvement**: Tardy-Joubert takes formal board seats on lead deals. UK Companies House confirms he was appointed director of Yoobic Limited on 13 July 2021 (immediately following the Series C) and served as director of Eptura International Limited (formerly Condeco Group) from 24 January 2019 until 30 July 2021, exiting shortly before Thoma Bravo's growth investment closed [^8].
- **Notable gap between stated and inferred**: Highland Europe's marketing emphasises "consumer internet" alongside software; Tardy-Joubert's own verified deals are entirely B2B software.

## In Their Own Words

On Snapshift's opportunity in deskless workforce software, at the time of the $45M Series A [^13]:

> "We spoke to countless businesses in the broad hospitality and service industries that are struggling with the exact pain point that Snapshift is solving."

On the shift from deskbound to distributed work, at the time of Yoobic's $50M Series C [^12]:

> "While companies have historically focused on digital investments for deskbound employees, the world is becoming distributed and decentralized. We anticipate a seismic shift that will see huge resources, technology, and capital shifted toward frontline teams."

On ESG data as a wedge into real estate, at the time of Deepki's €150M Series C [^15]:

> "What gets measured, gets managed and this is key to reducing emissions from the real estate sector, which currently is responsible for 40% of the Earth's carbon emissions."

On why he backed Wordsmith's approach to in-house legal work [^14]:

> "What is most exciting about Wordsmith is that this is a tool built for companies, rightfully involving all employees in legal affairs, in coordination with the in-house legal team. By taking a vertical approach, Ross and the Wordsmith team have established themselves at the forefront of the sector, with demonstrable market traction, impressive growth and more than 500 satisfied customers."

On moving from banking to venture, at the time he joined Highland Europe in March 2018 [^3]:

> "I am a big believer in people, brand, platform and operating ethos, and on all four counts, Highland Europe feels like home."

> "I have had first-hand experience of working with the Highland team and their cohesive, productive and fun culture made my decision to join very easy."
