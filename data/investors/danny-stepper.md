## Background

Danny Stepper is Co-Founder and Managing Partner of Taste Tomorrow Ventures (TTV), a Los Angeles-based venture capital firm launched in 2025 that invests in early-stage food and beverage brands [^1][^2]. He is simultaneously Co-Founder and CEO of LA Libations, the emerging-beverage incubator he founded with Dino Sarti in the mid-2010s, which functions as an "emerging beverage category captain" for major U.S. retailers and served as the operating platform out of which TTV was spun [^3][^2].

Stepper's operating career began in 1994 stocking shelves for Coca-Cola at a Food 4 Less in Pacoima; he was promoted 14 times over nine years within the Coca-Cola system, eventually running the Costco relationship that would later generate over $1B in business for his own companies [^3]. He took a four-year Hollywood detour producing films — brokering a $45M Adidas brand-equity deal for the *Goal!* trilogy, then co-founding Madvine, an entertainment-marketing venture inside Relativity Media — before returning to beverages to launch LA Libations [^3]. Coca-Cola served as LA Libations' anchor customer until it withdrew its services agreement in 2019, at which point Molson Coors rescued the company [^3]. LA Libations reports having incubated or scaled brands including BODYARMOR (grown from $100M to $1.4B in revenue before Coca-Cola's $8B acquisition), Core Water (sold to Keurig Dr Pepper for $526M), ZOA Energy (with Dwayne Johnson), HAPPY Coffee (with Robert Downey Jr.), BERO (with Tom Holland), and PLEZI (with Michelle Obama and Stephen Curry) [^3]. In 2024, Stepper acquired and relocated The Beverage Forum industry conference to Manhattan Beach; the April 2026 edition attracted 1,300+ industry leaders [^3].

TTV launched publicly in late May 2025 with a $30M debut fund (TTV Fund I), backed by strategic LPs including Ball Corporation (packaging) and Advantage Solutions (retail services), with a final close expected in late summer 2025 [^4][^5]. Stepper co-founded TTV with LA Libations co-founder Dino Sarti and former PepsiCo and Disney executive Scott Guthrie; the three principals bring more than 80 combined years of operating experience in consumer brands [^4]. TTV writes up to $2M per investment into companies with under $20M in revenue [^6].

## Stated Thesis

Taste Tomorrow Ventures publicly describes its focus as backing "mission-driven consumer brands disrupting established categories," with an early-stage-to-Series-A remit across functional beverages, better-for-you snacks, sustainable packaging solutions, and health-forward consumer brands [^4][^7]. On the fund's launch, Stepper framed TTV's diligence lens around three attributes — purpose, momentum, and leadership:
> "We couldn't be more excited to partner with Seth Goldman and Just Ice Tea as our first investment — it's a brand that embodies the purpose, innovation, and momentum we want to partner with." [^7]

He has separately positioned TTV as the institutionalization of LA Libations' longstanding brand-picking work, telling Easy Reader News that the fund and its associated $330M debt facility for purchase-order financing together represent a full-stack capital layer around the LA Libations retail-access platform [^3].

## Inferred Thesis

Analysis is based on **8 disclosed TTV Fund I investments** cited in press between May 2025 and May 2026 [^4][^7][^8][^9][^10][^11]. TTV is one year old at time of research, so the sample is small and sector patterns are early — but the pattern is unusually coherent for a first-year fund.

**Sector distribution (8 verified investments):**
- **Ready-to-drink beverages**: 4 of 8 (50%) — Just Ice Tea (organic iced tea), Happy Products (canned coffee), Chamberlain Coffee (RTD coffee), Cove Soda (functional soda) [^7][^8][^9].
- **Better-for-you snacks & candy**: 3 of 8 (38%) — Better Sour (plant-based sour gummy candy), Harken Sweets (date-sweetened chocolate), Don't Quit (better-for-you snack) [^10][^11][^9].
- **Adult beverages / spirits alternatives**: 1 of 8 (13%) — Bero (Tom Holland non-alcoholic beer) [^9].

**Stage distribution:** Predominantly seed-to-Series-A. Better Sour (2023-founded), Harken Sweets (2023-founded), and Just Ice Tea are seed-stage bets on 2-3 year old brands; Chamberlain Coffee and Happy Products are more established brands where TTV is entering at growth [^7][^8][^10][^11]. Per TTV's stated criteria, portfolio companies have <$20M revenue at time of investment [^6].

**Founder profile patterns:** TTV consistently backs celebrity-adjacent or category-veteran founders whose brand equity accelerates retail placement — Seth Goldman (co-founder Honest Tea) at Just Ice Tea, Robert Downey Jr. and Craig Dubitsky at Happy, Emma Chamberlain at Chamberlain Coffee, Tom Holland at Bero [^7][^8][^9]. Better Sour (Bella Hughes & Semira Nikou) and Harken Sweets (Katie Lefkowitz) are the operator-founder exceptions [^10][^11]. This is consistent with LA Libations' pre-TTV pattern (BODYARMOR/Kobe Bryant, ZOA/Dwayne Johnson, PLEZI/Michelle Obama, BERO/Tom Holland) [^3].

**Distinctive edge:** Unlike a typical VC, TTV pairs capital with LA Libations' retail-placement channel into Kroger, Costco, Walmart, Whole Foods, Sprouts, and other national retailers [^3][^11]. Portfolio companies are described as gaining access to LA Libations' vertically integrated marketing, merchandising, and distribution services rather than just a check [^3].

**Geographic focus:** U.S.-only in disclosed portfolio. All eight investments are U.S.-headquartered brands.

**Co-investor patterns:** Small sample, but observed co-investors include Robert Trone (Total Wine & More co-founder) on Just Ice Tea's later $9M round, Polar Strategic Ventures, and — on Harken Sweets — Selva and GRTSHT [^12][^11]. TTV appears to prefer participating-with-strategics or leading small seed rounds rather than syndicating with generalist consumer VCs.

**Notable gaps:** No alt-protein (Impossible/Beyond-style), no restaurant/hospitality tech, no food-service SaaS, no dark-kitchen or delivery infrastructure, and no ag-tech in the disclosed portfolio. TTV is a **brand-level CPG fund**, not a food-tech or food-service-platform fund.
