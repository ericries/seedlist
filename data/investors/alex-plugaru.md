## Background

Alex Plugaru is a software engineer and entrepreneur based in New York City [^1]. He co-founded Gorgias, a customer service helpdesk and conversational AI platform built for ecommerce (originally Shopify-focused), with Romain Lapeyre in 2015 [^2]. He served as Co-Founder and CTO of Gorgias for 11 years, stepping down from the CTO role in May 2026 to start Elench, an automated manufacturing company [^3]. Gorgias is a Series C company that has raised roughly $104M to date [^2].

Plugaru describes himself as a software engineer with "20+ years of experience" and maintains a personal blog covering startups, software engineering, and management [^1]. He is active on X/Twitter under the handle `@humanfromearth` [^4].

## Stated Thesis

Plugaru has not published a formal investing thesis. His angel activity to date has clustered around developer infrastructure / webhook tooling adjacent to the ecommerce and SaaS stacks he knows from Gorgias [^5][^6]. ## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size is too small for reliable percentages.** Only one portfolio company (Hookdeck) is publicly verifiable across two consecutive rounds [^5][^6][^7]. Qualitative observations from that single data point:

- **Sector**: Developer infrastructure / webhook tooling — directly adjacent to Plugaru's CTO domain at Gorgias (ecommerce APIs, integrations).
- **Stage**: Pre-seed and seed.
- **Geography**: The single confirmed portfolio company (Hookdeck) is Canadian, consistent with Tracxn's "primarily Canada" characterization [^7].
- **Co-investor pattern**: Has co-invested with his Gorgias co-founder Romain Lapeyre, Chris Nguyen (LogDNA co-founder), Farhan Thawar (Shopify VP Engineering), Ott Kaukver (Checkout CTO), Francois Arbour (Designstripe), Arbour & Renaud Capital, and Matrix Partners [^5][^6]. The co-investor mix skews heavily toward operator angels.

Given the lack of breadth, the most defensible inference is that Plugaru's angel activity to date is opportunistic — backing infrastructure used by ecommerce/SaaS teams alongside other operator-investors — rather than a systematic program.

## In Their Own Words

On leaving Gorgias and starting Elench:

> "After 11 years at Gorgias I've stepped down from my CTO role and started Elench." [^3]

> "The mission is the same: automate all the things! This time around it's more about atoms than bits." [^3]

> "The honest endgame is more boring and more interesting than singularity stuff." [^3]

On engineering management (Swarmia podcast):

> "the engineering managers, no matter which level, need to know how to code and need to be technical." [^9]

On team size and headcount:

> "I don't really subscribe to the success based on the headcount." [^9]

> "if you can do it with a smaller team, absolutely 100% of the time you should." [^9]

On CTO presence with engineers:

> "if the CTO starts commenting on PRs and you don't have a very close relationship with them, it can be triggering" [^9]

> "maintain the contact, do continue doing skip-levels, and don't stay in your ivory tower" [^9]
