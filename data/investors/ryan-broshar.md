## Background

Ryan Broshar is Founder and General Partner of Matchstick Ventures, a pre-seed and seed-stage venture firm he co-founded in 2013 with Natty Zola [^1][^2]. Based in Minneapolis with a second office in Boulder, Colorado, Matchstick invests in software-oriented companies with ties to the "North and the Rockies" — underserved startup ecosystems outside the Bay Area and NYC [^2][^3]. Broshar earned an undergraduate degree from the University of Minnesota, where he started a college-publication business (University Guide) that he sold two years out of college, then completed an MBA at CU-Boulder [^1][^3]. Before Matchstick he co-founded Beta.MN (Minnesota's demo-day organization), Twin Cities Startup Week, and the Techstars Retail Accelerator with Target — where he served as Managing Director for three programs [^1][^2][^3]. Matchstick has raised three funds — a $5M Fund I, a $30M Fund II, and a $55M Fund III — totaling $90M in AUM across 100+ portfolio companies [^4].

## Stated Thesis

Broshar publicly frames Matchstick as backing "underdogs" from underrepresented geographies and non-traditional backgrounds. On the firm's site he says: "We like to support founders that are underdogs. That come from untraditional backgrounds" [^5]. He has said the fund looks for "diverse, humble founders who have a chip on their shoulder and identify as the 'underdogs' who assemble humble, hard-working teams obsessed with the problems they are solving" [^1]. Matchstick's stated geographic focus is the Rockies (Colorado) and the North (Minneapolis and adjacent Upper Midwest/Central US markets) [^4].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on public disclosures and 95+ recorded investments [^3][^6]:

- **Stage**: Pre-seed and seed — investment range $250K–$650K with a $500K sweet spot per Signal by NFX [^6].
- **Geography**: Portfolio skews to Minneapolis, Boulder/Denver, and other secondary-market North American ecosystems including Toronto (Frate) — consistent with the stated "North and Rockies" thesis [^7][^2].
- **Sector breadth**: Recent 2025-2026 investments span vertical SaaS (Dalton Mills for the trades; Locate.ai for CRE brokerage), healthcare AI/ops (Saile clinical credentialing; Automate.Clinic), enterprise AI (Type, Great Sky AI hardware), fintech (Cast Finance AI accounting, Paperstack, Pageport), and dev/data tools (Freeplay, Liminal AI data security) [^7][^8].
- **Cheque behavior**: Broshar frequently leads or co-leads seed rounds and often re-participates in follow-on rounds (Two Boxes, Tab Commerce, Liminal) [^8].
- **Sample size caveat**: Portfolio breakdown is qualitative based on 30+ named recent investments; full sector percentage decomposition is not available.

The delta between stated and inferred: The "underdog" framing accurately describes the geographic bet, but the actual sector mix has drifted heavily toward AI-native enterprise tooling since 2024 (Type, Great Sky, Liminal, Freeplay, Automate.Clinic) even while the "chip-on-the-shoulder founder" language stays consistent [^7][^8].
