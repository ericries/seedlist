## Background

Olivia Moore is a partner on the investing team at Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), focused on AI and consumer investing [^1][^2]. She joined a16z in 2021 alongside her twin sister Justine Moore, after both had previously worked as early-stage investors at CRV [^1][^2][^3]. Prior to CRV, she worked at Goldman Sachs on the Alternative Investments and Manager Selection team [^1][^2].

Moore holds a B.S. in Economics (with Honors and Distinction) from Stanford University and an MBA from the Stanford Graduate School of Business [^1][^2]. While at Stanford she co-founded Cardinal Ventures, the university's on-campus startup incubator, which has since supported hundreds of student ventures [^3].

At a16z Moore publishes widely on consumer AI — including her recurring "Top 100 Gen AI Consumer Apps" rankings — and tracks which AI apps are gaining traction with everyday users [^1].

## Stated Thesis

Moore says publicly that her focus at a16z is on consumer AI apps and AI-native workflows for small and mid-market businesses [^1]. She has framed the current wave as a repricing of vertical software: many "AI-native" companies she backs replace older SaaS workflows with agents that place, receive, or reason over calls, messages, and documents [^4][^5].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size warning:** The a16z-led/co-led investments where Moore is publicly named as sponsor number roughly 5 disclosed rounds since 2024, plus prior CRV-era investments. Percentages are not computed given the small sample; qualitative patterns only.

- **Vertical AI agents replacing operational SaaS.** All disclosed a16z investments where Moore is credited (HappyRobot, Toma, Salient, Krea, Scribenote) build AI agents that handle a specific operational workflow — freight brokerage calls, dealership service scheduling, lender-borrower conversations, image and video generation, veterinary scribing [^4][^5][^6].
- **Voice AI concentration.** Three of five publicly named a16z investments (HappyRobot, Toma, Salient) center on AI-driven voice agents — a distinct concentration on real-time voice interfaces over chat-based agents [^4][^5][^6].
- **SMB / vertical-market bias.** The dealer-services, freight brokerage, and lending sub-verticals point to a preference for tightly-scoped, mid-market operational buyers rather than horizontal enterprise tooling [^4][^5][^6].
- **Consumer-tech roots.** CRV-era deals (Kapwing Series A, Brigit Series A, Harper Wilde seed, Uppercase seed) skewed to consumer creator tools, consumer fintech, and D2C commerce, foreshadowing her present consumer-AI focus [^3].

## In Their Own Words

On why a16z backed HappyRobot's Series A:

> "We're thrilled to announce we are leading HappyRobot's Series A." — Olivia Moore, Anish Acharya, and Seema Amble, a16z announcement, December 4, 2024 [^4]

On the design of Toma's AI dealership agent:

> "Toma accurately captures detailed customer information and vehicle data (including VIN, part numbers, repair orders, and vehicle models), schedules service appointments, processes parts orders, and efficiently books test drives in real time." — a16z Toma announcement, June 5, 2025 [^5]
