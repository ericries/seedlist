## Background

Pat Hanrahan (born May 8, 1955, in Milwaukee, Wisconsin) is an American computer graphics researcher and the Canon USA Professor Emeritus of Computer Science and Electrical Engineering at Stanford University [^1][^2]. He earned a B.S. in Nuclear Engineering (1977) and a Ph.D. in Biophysics (1985) from the University of Wisconsin–Madison [^1][^2].

Hanrahan was a founding employee of Pixar Animation Studios in the 1980s, where he served as a principal architect of the RenderMan Interface Specification and the RenderMan Shading Language — the rendering system used to produce *Toy Story*, *Tin Toy*, and numerous other Pixar films [^1][^2][^3]. He joined Princeton University's computer science faculty in 1989 and moved to Stanford in 1995 [^1][^2].

In 2003, Hanrahan co-founded Tableau Software alongside his Stanford Ph.D. student Chris Stolte and Stanford Business School graduate Christian Chabot, spinning the company out of Stanford research on visualizing large relational databases [^3][^4]. Tableau went public on the NYSE in May 2013 at $31 per share, raising $254 million [^3]. Salesforce acquired Tableau in June 2019 in an all-stock deal valued at approximately $15.3 billion, making Hanrahan a billionaire [^4]. He served as Chief Scientist at Tableau throughout its history and dedicated approximately 20% of his time to the company as a part-time employee while continuing at Stanford [^3][^4].

Beyond Tableau, Hanrahan co-founded PeakStream in January 2005 with Matt Papakipos and Asher Waldfogel; the company developed a software platform for programming multi-core and parallel processors (including GPUs) and was acquired by Google in June 2007 [^5][^6]. He has also served on the technical advisory boards of NVIDIA, Exluna, Neoptica, VSee, Procedural, and SkyTree [^7].

In 2019, Hanrahan received the ACM A.M. Turing Award jointly with Ed Catmull "for fundamental contributions to 3-D computer graphics, and the revolutionary impact of these techniques on computer-generated imagery (CGI) in filmmaking and other applications" [^8]. Additional honors include three Academy Awards for Technical Achievement (1993, 2004, 2014), the SIGGRAPH Computer Graphics Achievement Award, the SIGGRAPH Steven A. Coons Award (2003), the IEEE Visualization Career Award (2006), election to the National Academy of Engineering (1999), and Fellowship of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2007) and the ACM (2008) [^1][^2][^7].

Hanrahan is also Co-Founder and Director of the Maxwell/Hanrahan Foundation, a philanthropic organization supporting Craft, Diversity & Inclusion in Art/Design/Computing, Earth Science, Field Biology, Food, Public School Teachers, and Resilient Ecosystems [^2].

## Stated Thesis

Hanrahan has not published a formal angel investment thesis. His public profile describes his investment focus as "rendering algorithms, graphics processing units, scientific illustration, and visualization" [^7] — a mapping of his academic research domains onto his angel activity rather than a distinct market-facing thesis. He is listed as a pre-seed and seed-stage angel [^7].

## Inferred Thesis

Only one angel investment by Hanrahan has been independently verified with a public source: a September 11, 2014 seed round in Apropose, a data-driven web-design analytics startup spun out of Stanford's computer science department [^9][^10]. This sample is too small to compute meaningful sector percentages or stage distributions.

**What can be said from the available evidence:**

- **Stanford-adjacent deal flow.** The one verified angel investment (Apropose) was a Stanford CS Department spinout, and Hanrahan participated alongside NEA and Andreessen Horowitz — both firms with deep Stanford ties [^9][^10].
- **Domain overlap with research areas.** Apropose's technology — data-driven analysis of visual design at scale — sits at the intersection of Hanrahan's areas of academic focus (visualization, data-driven analysis).
- **Advisory role beyond capital.** Hanrahan has served on the technical advisory boards of NVIDIA, Exluna, Neoptica, VSee, Procedural, and SkyTree [^7]. These are advisory (typically compensated in equity), not personal angel investments in the traditional sense, but they indicate an ongoing pattern of engaging with startups in graphics, GPU computing, video communication, procedural generation, and machine learning tooling.
- **Founder / operator archetype.** Hanrahan's own two founded companies — Tableau (data visualization) and PeakStream (GPU/parallel-computing developer tools) — both target technical, developer-facing markets built on his research; his engagement with startups follows the same pattern.

**Explicit gap:** Given his stature (Turing Award, Pixar founding employee, Tableau billionaire, Stanford full professor for 25+ years, adviser to NVIDIA), Hanrahan's publicly documented angel portfolio is unusually thin. It is possible he makes investments through Tableau-era operating vehicles or private LP commitments that are not publicly reported. Founders seeking his backing should treat him as an infrequent, high-conviction angel who invests through his personal network — not a systematic seed check-writer.
