## Background

Will Quist is a Partner at Slow Ventures, which he joined in November 2015 [^1][^2]. Slow Ventures lists his title as "Excitable GP" on its team page, reflecting the firm's informal branding [^1]. Before Slow, Quist spent more than eight years at Industry Ventures, and earlier in his career he worked in finance at Banc of America [^2][^3]. He is a former collegiate water polo player who has described his path into venture in podcast interviews [^4].

Slow Ventures itself is a generalist early-stage firm founded in 2011 by former Facebook employees Dave Morin, Kevin Colleran, and Sam Lessin; today the fund is run principally by Colleran, Lessin, and Quist [^5]. As of December 2024, Slow was raising a $165M Fund VI and a $110M opportunity fund, totalling $275M across two vehicles [^5]. Quist is one of three named partners on Slow's core investing team, alongside Kevin Colleran (Managing Director) and Sam Lessin (GP) [^1].

## Stated Thesis

Quist publicly frames his approach around a program he designed at Slow called the "Slow PhD," an engagement pattern meant for founders with subject-matter expertise and high opportunity cost. The stated premise: "Important companies and successful businesses can't be hacked, forced, or faked" [^6]. In a September 2025 Snailmail essay, he distilled the philosophy as:

> "You need slow conviction, not fast momentum." [^7]

Signal by NFX lists his declared focus areas as "Banking, Future of Work, Health Care Services," with strong preferences for Web3/crypto, consumer internet, SaaS, and health IT, and stated aversion to marketing technology, content platforms, and lending [^2].

Slow itself markets a broad, sector-agnostic seed thesis spanning security, fintech, buyouts/rollups, SaaS, crypto, consumer, healthcare, and creator economy [^1].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 8 unique verified portfolio companies (9 rounds) documented below, Quist's investment behavior differs meaningfully from a generic seed generalist. The sample is small, so numerical percentages are omitted in favor of qualitative patterns.

**Sector patterns.** Quist's verified deal history skews toward "picks-and-shovels for old-world systems being rewired" — food R&D (Starday), sustainable timber (Lumber Manufactory), prize-linked fintech (Yotta), Web3 infrastructure (Portal), music creator payments (Breakr), patient communication (Karuna Health), and AI agent tooling (AgentAI). Only Yotta and Portal sit clearly in the "banking / crypto" lanes Signal shows as his top declared interests; the majority are consumer/industrial hybrids.

**Stage.** Predominantly seed. Two of the nine verified rounds were Series A (Yotta 2021, Starday 2025); the remaining seven were seed or seed-extension rounds.

**Check sizes.** Signal reports a $500K–$2M range with a ~$1M sweet spot at seed [^2]. In Series A leads, the personal-attributed check has been materially larger — e.g., Slow led Starday's $8M Series A equity tranche alongside Equal Ventures [^8].

**Deal role.** Quist's verified rounds skew toward *participation alongside another lead* (Portal, Yotta, Lumber Manufactory, Breakr) rather than sole leadership. The exception is Starday, where Slow Ventures was named co-lead with Equal Ventures on the April 2025 Series A [^8][^9].

**Co-investors.** Recurring co-investors across his verified rounds include Equal Ventures (Starday), Haun Ventures and Acrew Capital (Portal), Base10 Partners and Y Combinator (Yotta), and Precursor Ventures (Breakr) [^8][^10][^11][^12].

**Founder pattern.** Multiple investments back founders re-engineering physical/regulated industries (food formulation, sawmills, wallet infrastructure, patient outreach), consistent with the "Slow PhD" framing of long-cycle building over hype-driven momentum.

**Sample-size caveat.** Signal.nfx lists 9 individually attributed deals; Slow Ventures has funded 500+ companies as a firm, so Quist's true attribution is likely larger than what public records surface. The inferred thesis above is grounded only in what is independently verifiable.

## In Their Own Words

**On starting versus finishing (Snailmail, September 2025):**
> "'Can I start this?' when the much more important question is, 'Should I?'" [^7]

**On opportunity cost for experienced founders:**
> "When your alternative is $500K+ a year in cash and RSUs, sprinting toward incorporation becomes expensive." [^7]

**On walking away as a founder:**
> "Sometimes, after all that work, the best outcome is deciding not to start. That's not failure—it's efficiency." [^7]

**On time as an asset:**
> "Your time is your most expensive asset—don't spend it lightly." [^7]

**On Yotta's prize-linked savings model (Crunchbase News, October 2020):**
> "We think the power of random rewards is underrated in the U.S. as a way to drive significant behavior change, and we were ideating on when that would get properly applied to financial services." [^13]

**On Starday's food-innovation model (Business Wire, April 2025):**
> "What excites us about Starday is how they're rethinking the entire system for how food brands are created and scaled." [^9]
