## Background

Willem Delbare is a Belgian serial SaaS founder turned operator-angel based in Ghent. He earned a Master of Science in Computer Science from Ghent University (2007–2012) [^1]. In 2012 he co-founded Teamleader — a CRM, project-management and invoicing platform for SMBs — alongside Jeroen De Wit and Mathias De Loore, and served as CTO until 2016 [^2][^3]. Teamleader grew into one of Belgium's most-cited SaaS successes (eventually acquired by Visma) and produced an alumni network of operator-angels that Delbare remains connected to [^3].

After Teamleader, Delbare co-founded Officient (HR/payroll SaaS, 2017–2022) and was associated with other Belgian SaaS ventures including FutureproofedCities [^3][^4]. In 2022 he co-founded Aikido Security in Ghent with Roeland Delrue (COO), Felix Garriau (CMO) and Madeline Lawrence (CGO), positioning the company as a "no-BS" developer-first security platform for SMBs [^4][^5][^6]. Aikido raised a €2M pre-seed in January 2023 [^5], a $5M seed in late 2023, and a $17M Series A led by Singular in May 2024 with participation from Notion Capital and Connect Ventures [^6][^7]. As of 2026 Aikido reports 250+ employees and ~$85M total raised, protecting 100,000+ teams [^4].

His angel investing runs in parallel with operating Aikido. Public databases record a small but growing portfolio of typically European (and especially Belgian) seed-stage SaaS bets, with check sizes ranging $5K–$50K and a sweet-spot check around $25K [^8].

## Stated Thesis

Delbare has not published a formal investment thesis. His public commentary frames investing as an extension of his operator perspective: he backs developer-facing or SMB-facing software where he has direct lived experience as a buyer or builder. On the journy.io seed announcement, describing the customer-data integration problem his portfolio company tackles, he said: "it is a lot of hard work to build, to secure, and to maintain…and certainly costly, too." [^9]

His self-description across founder interviews emphasises building "software that didn't make people want to throw their laptops out the window" [^10], a sensibility that maps closely to the kinds of practical, B2B SaaS tools he angel-invests in.

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size is small — 7 publicly verified angel investments — so percentages would be misleading. Below is a qualitative read of the pattern.** [^8]

- **Stage:** Almost exclusively pre-seed and seed. Verified rounds range from a $410K seed (Salesflare, 2018) to a $20M Series A (Prelude, 2026 — likely a small follow-on or early angel position) [^8].
- **Geography:** Heavily Belgian / Benelux-skewed. journy.io, StoryChief, and Salesflare are Belgian SaaS companies [^9][^11]; this matches his role inside the Ghent / Teamleader / Showpad operator network.
- **Sector:** B2B SaaS dominates — marketing/sales tooling (StoryChief, Salesflare, journy.io), HR/people tools (Donna), and developer/security-adjacent products. Recent 2024–2026 rounds (Donna, Belfort, Holmes, Prelude) suggest a broader geographic and sectoral mix as his network has expanded post-Aikido [^8].
- **Check size:** Aggregator data shows a $5K–$50K range with a $25K sweet spot — i.e., consistent friend-of-founder operator checks rather than lead positions [^8].
- **Co-investor pattern:** Frequently co-invests with Belgian-Dutch funds and operator-angels: PMV (Flemish public-investment vehicle) and Peak Capital on journy.io [^9]; the Aikido pre-seed itself was backed by Belgian operator-angels including Louis Jonckheere and Pieterjan Bouten (Showpad), Christophe Morbee (Besox), and Mathias Geeroms (OTA Insight), all of whom form the wider Ghent/Antwerp operator-angel cluster Delbare moves in [^5].
- **Founder profile:** Verified portfolio companies are overwhelmingly led by repeat or technical SaaS founders out of Belgium/Netherlands.

**Notable gap:** Despite running a security company, security startups do not dominate his angel portfolio — the bets reflect "SaaS founder helping SaaS founders" more than vertical security expertise.

## In Their Own Words

On the original problem behind Aikido — the operator pain that shaped his current view of security tooling:

> "Software security tools have made my life as a CTO harder…They overload you with false positives, spam you with notifications, and make triaging hard." — Willem Delbare, Aikido pre-seed announcement, January 2023 [^5]

> "I wasted countless hours on irrelevant security alerts as a CTO. These legacy security platforms eat up developer time." — Willem Delbare, quoted in TechCrunch on Aikido's Series A, May 2024 [^7]

> "Developers are so swamped with complexity and false alerts, that they stop checking them all together." — Willem Delbare, TechCrunch, May 2024 [^7]

On market structure and who buys security:

> "Security, like compliance, used to be for the big guys. Now security is a growing requirement for all companies." — Willem Delbare, TechCrunch, May 2024 [^7]

On building software more broadly:

> "building software that didn't make people want to throw their laptops out the window" — Willem Delbare, Unite.ai interview, 2024 [^10]

On the journy.io thesis (a CRM-integration angel bet):

> "it is a lot of hard work to build, to secure, and to maintain…and certainly costly, too." — Willem Delbare, Peak Capital announcement, February 2021 [^9]
