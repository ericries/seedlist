## Background

Chris Choi Downer is a General Partner at Brewer Lane Ventures, a Boston-based venture capital firm focused on early-stage fintech, insurtech, and healthtech [^1][^2]. He was promoted to General Partner in March 2022 alongside Rohan Malhotra [^3].

Before joining Brewer Lane, Downer held Principal-level roles at two insurance-focused investment groups — Sorenson Ventures and XL Innovate (the corporate venture arm of AXA XL) — where he focused on early-stage analytics and insurtech investments across North America, Europe, and Asia [^4]. He began his career at Goldman Sachs in the Alternative Investments and Manager Selection Group, working out of both New York and London [^4].

Downer graduated with a BA magna cum laude from Dartmouth College, where he played rugby and was later inducted into the Dartmouth Athletics Hall of Fame ("Wearers of the Green") [^4]. He is a minority owner of the New England Free Jacks of Major League Rugby [^4].

Brewer Lane Ventures was founded in 2019 by John Kim and hired insurtech veteran Martha Notaras as Managing Partner in January 2020 [^5]. The firm's core mandate is Series A and Series B fintech and insurtech investing, and Chris Downer is one of its three general partners [^2][^3].

## Stated Thesis

Downer publicly frames Brewer Lane's insurance-tech thesis around companies that combine software with distribution or workflow depth in insurance and financial services. On Novella's Series A, he stated: "Novella is the first wholesaler to effectively blend AI and talent. Wholesalers spend years building relationships. Novella makes them better at it — that's why top brokers are joining the team, and how they're changing specialty insurance." [^6][^7]

On Ennabl's Series A, he stated: "Their platform is revolutionizing the way insurance brokers leverage data, and we see tremendous promise in Ennabl's ability to further impact the market." [^8]

Brewer Lane publicly describes its focus as "early-stage financial, insurance, and healthcare technology companies" typically at Series A and Series B [^2].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample caveat: four rounds are directly attributed to Downer as the deal partner in public sources [^9]. Additional Brewer Lane portfolio companies (see below) reflect the firm's overall activity, not necessarily deals Downer personally led. Percentages are not computed given the small verified sample.**

Observed patterns from personally-attributed deals (Novella, Sixfold, Dispatch, Ennabl, Stonestep):

- **Stage: predominantly Series A and B.** Four of five verified personal deals are Series A [^9][^6][^8]; Sixfold was a Series B lead in January 2026 [^10]. No verified pre-seed activity — consistent with Brewer Lane's Series A/B mandate [^2].
- **Sector concentration: insurance-adjacent software.** All five verified deals are insurance-tech, insurance analytics, or financial services data platforms: Novella (AI wholesale insurance broker) [^7], Sixfold (AI underwriting) [^10], Ennabl (broker data platform) [^8], Dispatch (financial advisory data) [^11], Stonestep (microinsurance analytics) [^9]. This is a tighter thesis than the broader "fintech + insurtech + healthtech" firm mandate.
- **Check size: $1M–$10M with a $5M sweet spot** [^9] — consistent with leading Series A rounds of $8M–$21M in the verified set [^6][^8].
- **Lead-investor stance.** Brewer Lane led at least three of Downer's personally-attributed deals (Novella, Ennabl, Sixfold) [^7][^8][^10], suggesting he sources and leads rather than follows into competitive rounds.
- **Co-investor patterns.** Verified co-investors on Downer-attributed rounds include Guidewire Software (strategic), Bessemer Venture Partners, Salesforce Ventures (Sixfold) [^10]; Box Group, SV Angel, Avid Ventures, Crystal Venture Partners, Verissimo Ventures, Blank Ventures, and Arch (Novella) [^7]; Altai Ventures (Ennabl) [^8].
- **Distribution and incumbent-adjacent bets.** The Novella and Sixfold theses both center on augmenting incumbent insurance workflows (wholesale broking, underwriting) with AI, rather than disrupting incumbents — a distinct pattern versus consumer-insurance disruption.
