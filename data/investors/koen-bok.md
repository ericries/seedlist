## Background

Koen Bok is co-founder and CEO of Framer, the Amsterdam-based no-code website builder. He describes himself on his personal site as "half designer, half programmer and a little bit of an entrepreneur" [^1].

Bok began his career in 2006 as co-founder and general director of Sofa B.V., a 15-person Amsterdam design studio that won two consecutive Apple Design Awards — one for Checkout and one for Versions — and worked with clients including TomTom, Mozilla, and Nike [^2]. Facebook acquired Sofa in 2011, and Bok spent more than two years there as a product designer working on Messages, Ads, Video, and Photos [^2] [^3].

Bok co-founded Framer with Jorn van Dijk in 2014 after leaving Facebook [^3]. Framer initially shipped as a prototyping tool for designers before pivoting in 2021 to become a full website builder [^4]. The company crossed $50M ARR in 2025 and raised a $100M Series D at a $2B valuation in August 2025, led by existing investors Meritech and Atomico with Accel returning [^5] [^6]. Bok co-runs Festina, an early-stage investment fund, alongside Jorn van Dijk [^1] [^7].

## Stated Thesis

Festina, the fund Bok runs with Jorn van Dijk, publicly describes its focus as "software companies with world-class user experience design" [^7]. The fund's motto — "Festina Lente," or "make haste slowly" — is also displayed on the site [^7].

On his personal website, Bok invites entrepreneurs to reach out directly: "If you have one and you think I can help, drop me a line" [^1]. Beyond this and the Festina one-liner, Bok has not published a formal investment thesis document.

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 8 verified angel investments (of ~20 reported by NFX Signal [^8]); this analysis covers approximately 40% of his tracked activity. Because the verified sample is small and biased toward larger, well-publicized rounds, sector percentages are omitted in favor of qualitative description.

**Sector pattern:** Verified investments cluster tightly around software tools for designers, developers, and product teams — Pitch (presentation software) [^9], Linear (project management for software teams) [^10], CodeSandbox (in-browser development environment) [^11], Softr (no-code app builder) [^12], VoidZero (open-source JavaScript toolchain) [^13], and Spline (3D design tool, via Festina) [^7]. Revue (newsletter tool, acquired by Twitter in 2021) fits the same design/tools pattern [^14]. This is a narrow-lane portfolio: every verified investment is a productivity, design, or developer tool.

**Stage pattern:** Verified rounds span pre-seed through Series A. NFX Signal's tracking of 20 investments shows the same pattern extending through 2026, with recent participation in pre-seed and seed rounds for Upstream ($3M pre-seed, June 2026), Bounti ($5M seed, March 2026), Modem ($4M pre-seed, February 2026), and Lemni ($4M pre-seed, February 2025) [^8].

**Check size:** NFX Signal reports a range of $5K-$50K with a sweet spot of $25K [^8].

**Geographic pattern:** Portfolio spans US (Linear, Pitch, VoidZero), Europe (Softr in Berlin, Mollie in Amsterdam, Spline), consistent with Bok being based between Amsterdam and Barcelona.

**Co-investor pattern:** Bok frequently appears in rounds alongside other product-builder angels — most notably his Framer co-founder Jorn van Dijk (Linear, VoidZero, and via Festina), Christian Reber (Pitch founder, at Softr alongside Bok) [^12], and Guillermo Rauch (Vercel; also invested in CodeSandbox) [^11]. His investments consistently sit in rounds led by top-tier funds (Index Ventures at Pitch, Accel at Linear Series B and VoidZero, FirstMark at Softr).

**Notable exits:** CodeSandbox (acquired by Together AI in December 2024) [^15] and Revue (acquired by Twitter in 2021) [^14].

## In Their Own Words

On product-market fit, from a Designer Founders interview: "If you don't have product market fit, then nothing seems to work and everybody will start pointing at each other" [^3].

Also from Designer Founders: "The most important thing is building something that people want" [^3].

On Framer's Series D and strategy, quoted by TechCrunch (August 28, 2025): "With this funding, we're doubling down on enterprise growth and AI, so any company can confidently run their entire website on Framer" [^16].

On Framer's product model, quoted in Forbes (September 28, 2023): "You design the website in Framer and then at the end you click publish and we turn it into a website that we also host" [^17].

On the design/build workflow gap that motivated Framer's pivot, also quoted by Forbes: "We discovered a really big juicy problem because traditional web design happens in one tool like Figma, then actual building is done elsewhere" [^17].

On his own identity, from his personal site: "I'm half designer, half programmer and a little bit of an entrepreneur" [^1].
