## Background

Kanwaljit Singh is the Founder and Managing Partner of Fireside Ventures, a Bengaluru-based venture firm dedicated to early-stage Indian consumer brands. He launched Fireside in 2018 after more than two decades spanning consumer marketing and venture investing in India [^1][^2].

Singh spent over 13 years in consumer marketing roles, primarily at Hindustan Unilever and Intel India, before moving into venture capital [^1]. He served as a Director at Carlyle Group India and subsequently co-founded Helion Venture Partners, where he was Senior Managing Director [^1]. During his Helion years and as an angel, he backed early Indian consumer brands including Paper Boat, ID Fresh, Epigamia, and Licious — investments that shaped the consumer-focused thesis that became Fireside Ventures [^1][^2].

Fireside Ventures closed its first fund of ₹340 crore in March 2017, Fund II at ₹863 crore ($118M) in 2021, and Fund IV at ₹2,265 crore ($253M) on December 5, 2024 [^2][^3][^4]. Fireside's portfolio has produced three unicorns (boAt, Licious, Cult.fit) and at least one public listing (Mamaearth/Honasa Consumer, listed on BSE and NSE in November 2023) [^5].

## Stated Thesis

Singh publicly describes Fireside as backing early-stage Indian consumer brands with deep "consumer playbook" understanding, hands-on founder support across marketing, HR, and platform partnerships (Meta, Amazon, Flipkart), and a portfolio-construction approach rather than a power-law bet [^4][^5].

In a 2024 Outlook Business interview, Singh described the firm's check sizes and ownership model directly: initial checks of "$1-2 million," with $8-10 million deployed across a company's lifetime, and a target ownership of approximately 20% — up from a 10% target in Fund I [^4]. He has also stated, "We have 70-80%, sometimes even 90% success, because we understand the playbook," contrasting Fireside's consumer-portfolio model with traditional tech-VC outlier dependence [^4].

Singh emphasizes wellness as a category driver and Indian consumption depth as the macro tailwind, noting that the post-COVID digital shift expanded Fireside's addressable consumer base beyond "the urban elite" to "the entire gamut of consumers across the country" [^6].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on a verified portfolio sample of 17 companies (see Portfolio below):

- **Sector breakdown (17 verified investments):** Personal care / beauty: 5 (Mamaearth, Bombay Shaving Company, Azani, Traya, The Ayurveda Experience) — 29%. Food & beverage: 6 (Yoga Bar, Samosa Singh, Slurrp Farm, Vahdam, Kapiva, Goodness Beverages) — 35%. Consumer electronics/lifestyle: 2 (boAt, The Sleep Company) — 12%. Wellness/health: 2 (Gynoveda, Sarva) — 12%. Fashion/accessories: 2 (Fablestreet, Pipa Bella) — 12%. Heavy concentration in personal care + food & beverage (64% combined) consistent with Singh's Hindustan Unilever background.
- **Stage distribution:** Predominantly seed and Series A — Fireside is "first institutional investor" in many portfolio companies including Mamaearth, boAt, and Yoga Bar [^7].
- **Geographic focus:** India-centric. Sample shows portfolio companies across Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, and Hyderabad — no international consumer brands found in the verified sample.
- **Check size:** Singh has publicly stated $1-2M initial, $8-10M lifetime, targeting ~20% ownership [^4].
- **Founder profile:** Singh has stated approximately 50% of portfolio is women-founded, and that Fireside has backed "15-20 founder couples" across roughly 60 portfolio companies [^4]. This is unusually high for Indian VC and consistent with consumer-category founder demographics.
- **Portfolio approach (not power law):** Singh explicitly differentiates Fireside from tech VC, citing 70-80% portfolio success rates rather than outlier dependence [^4]. Consumer brands with steady unit economics, not winner-take-all dynamics.
- **Notable gap vs. stated thesis:** Stated thesis emphasizes "consumer brands" broadly; inferred portfolio is heavily skewed to ingestible/personal-care categories (food, beverage, beauty, wellness = 76% of sample) with relatively limited exposure to consumer hardware (boAt, Sleep Company are the main exceptions).

Sample size caveat: 17 verified investments out of approximately 60 portfolio companies (~28%); patterns above should be interpreted as directional.
