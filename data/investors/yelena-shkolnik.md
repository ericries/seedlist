## Background

Yelena Shkolnik is a Partner at Jump Capital, a thesis-driven venture firm making Series A investments in fintech, IT/data infrastructure, and vertical B2B SaaS [^1][^2]. She is based in Los Angeles, where she was raised, and joined Jump in 2015 [^1][^2].

Her pre-VC career started in investment banking at UBS, then film finance at DreamWorks, and then venture at Chicago-based MK Capital before Jump [^3]. She was named to Crain's Chicago Business "Rising Stars in Tech" list in 2020 [^4].

Jump Capital is headquartered in Chicago and describes itself as focused on Series A rounds with typical check sizes of $8–$10M, leading approximately 70% of the rounds it participates in [^1]. Per NFX Signal, Shkolnik's personal check range spans $100K–$10M with a sweet spot around $1M [^2].

## Stated Thesis

Publicly, Shkolnik describes her focus as "the convergence of fintech, content, and commerce," particularly through streaming platforms, social media, and creator-economy platforms enabling both entertainment and financial transactions [^1]. Her formal areas at Jump are enterprise software, media, fintech, and crypto [^1][^5].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 6 publicly verified Series A / Seed investments (small sample — qualitative only, no percentages [^2]):

- **Stage:** Predominantly Series A ($8–$14M rounds), with occasional Seed follow-through into Series A (Obsess). This matches Jump's stated Series-A specialization.
- **Sector:** All 6 verified investments cluster in enterprise software with a commerce/media tilt — Vinyl Equity (music-royalty fintech), Highline (payments / earned-wage payroll), Recurate (resale/circular commerce infrastructure), Toolio (retail merchandising SaaS), Obsess (immersive/AR commerce), TVision (TV attention analytics). The through-line is exactly the "convergence of fintech, content, and commerce" she publicly claims.
- **Geography:** US-centric (New York, LA, Chicago).
- **Co-investor pattern:** Repeats include Foundation Capital, Founder Collective, Summit Partners, Gradient Ventures, XRC Ventures, and RevTech Ventures [^2]. These are consistent with Series-A commerce-tech syndicates.
- **Ownership behavior:** Jump leads roughly 70% of the rounds it participates in per firm materials [^1]; Shkolnik's TVision role — quoted in the round announcement as the Jump principal — is consistent with that lead-heavy posture [^6].
- **Notable gap:** Despite her stated crypto interest [^1], no verified crypto investments appear on her personal NFX Signal profile as of this pass. Jump has a separate $350M crypto fund [^7] but Shkolnik's personal named deals to date are non-crypto commerce/enterprise.

## In Their Own Words

On working in crypto:

> "The crypto community is an active and engaged one. So I think it is easier to get publicity for the work we do in crypto. But in crypto, we're not just investors, we're also builders." — Yelena Shkolnik, dot.LA interview [^1]

On creator-economy tokenization and ownership:

> "Is it easier for me to sort of incentivize you by saying, 'Hey, you're gonna have some fractional equity ownership in Nike?' Or is it easier for me to incentivize you with a social token? I don't know." — Yelena Shkolnik, dot.LA interview [^1]

On the current entrepreneurial moment:

> "I think that this general motif...is just the opportunity for everyone to be an entrepreneur and kind of determine their own destiny." — Yelena Shkolnik, dot.LA interview [^1]
