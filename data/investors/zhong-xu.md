## Background

Zhong Xu is the co-founder and CEO of Deliverect, a Ghent-based SaaS platform that connects delivery marketplaces such as Uber Eats, Deliveroo and DoorDash with restaurant point-of-sale systems [^1][^2]. Born in Shanghai, he moved to Belgium as a child when his father pursued a PhD [^3]. He began building websites for Chinese restaurants at age 16 and taught himself C++ to build a point-of-sale system in his teens [^3].

Xu earned a bachelor's degree in computer science (2004–2007) and a master's degree in software engineering (2007–2009), both from Ghent University [^1]. He worked as a senior engineer at Belgian broadcast-tech company EVS, developing mobile and cloud applications used at the London Olympics, UEFA, and the BBC [^1]. In 2012 he co-founded POSIOS, a cloud- and iPad-based POS system for hospitality; POSIOS was acquired by Lightspeed Commerce in 2014, after which Xu served as Global Director of Hospitality at Lightspeed until 2018 [^1][^4].

He co-founded Deliverect with Jan Hollez, Jerome Laredo and Jelte Vrijhoef in September 2018 [^2][^4]. Deliverect reached unicorn status in January 2022 following a $150M Series D and has since publicly reported processing more than one billion orders across 68,000+ restaurant locations [^2][^5]. Xu is a member of the Forbes Technology Council [^6].

## Stated Thesis

Xu does not run a fund and has not published a formal investment thesis. Publicly listed aggregator data describes him as an angel investor focused on food-and-beverage, SaaS and consumer technology in the Netherlands and Belgium, with a small-check profile in the $5K–$50K range and a $25K sweet spot [^7]. Note that this range is self-reported to NFX Signal and has not been independently verified against primary press disclosures.

His public commentary about founders and building companies — captured in interviews rather than as an investment thesis — emphasizes execution and speed. Asked about advice to founders, he has said: "Speed beats perfection – don't force yourself to make the best product, but rather try to launch quicker than later to get feedback from customers" [^8], and "The 'idea' is really not that important, it's all about execution" [^8].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size caveat:** Only two publicly confirmed angel investments could be independently verified from primary sources (Orderchamp seed 2020, Casper Series A 2022). Aggregator listings mention two additional deals (Vendorvue, Mealhero) attributed to Xu, but neither round's primary press coverage names him individually [^9][^10][^11]. The analysis below is qualitative.

**Sector focus:** All verified deals sit in food-and-beverage or restaurant-adjacent commerce — Orderchamp is a B2B wholesale marketplace for independent retailers [^12], Casper is a Belgian ghost-kitchen operator [^13]. This mirrors Xu's operating experience in restaurant POS and delivery integration.

**Stage:** Seed and Series A. Orderchamp participation was in a seed round with Dutch VC henQ and other angels [^12]; Casper was a €5M Series A led by Slingshot Ventures [^13]. Both round sizes fall well below institutional-lead thresholds — consistent with an angel-check role.

**Geographic focus:** Benelux. Both confirmed deals are Ghent- or Amsterdam-based [^12][^13].

**Co-investor patterns:** In the Orderchamp seed, Xu invested alongside fellow operator-angels Stefan van den Berg (former CEO of Afterpay) and Thomas Plantenga (CEO of Vinted) [^12]. In Casper, he participated alongside his Deliverect co-founder Jan Hollez and Belgian serial entrepreneur Jürgen Ingels (Smartfin) [^13]. Both patterns suggest he prefers syndicates of operating founders over pure-financial investors.

**Important caveat on Casper:** Xu is publicly described as a co-founder of Casper, though "he has never been operationally involved" per Casper CEO Matthias Laga [^14]. The Series A participation is therefore best characterized as insider-plus-angel rather than a purely arm's-length angel check.

## In Their Own Words

On execution vs. ideas, from an interview with Digital Web Solutions:

> "The 'idea' is really not that important, it's all about execution." [^8]

On speed:

> "Speed beats perfection – don't force yourself to make the best product, but rather try to launch quicker than later to get feedback from customers." [^8]

On the founder mindset:

> "Just do it, be naïve and hungry. Don't overthink it and just take the first step." [^8]

On risk and starting POSIOS, his first company:

> "Starting POSIOS in 2010 was probably the best but also the riskiest and craziest decision." [^8]

> "You should always just start and don't regret things in life." [^8]

On what drives him:

> "I like being outside of my comfort zone, so it means that I like the unknown and the challenging." [^8]

> "If something seems impossible, I always want to take a crack at it and make the impossible possible." [^8]

On endurance in company-building, reflecting on closing Deliverect's Series C during the Covid lockdowns:

> "It was about grit, resourcefulness, and refusing to quit even when everything looked fragile. You don't need a fancy boardroom to close a round sometimes the most important deals of your life get signed on an ironing plank in a basement." [^15]

> "Behind every 'unicorn story' are endless sacrifices, doubts, and sleepless nights. The truth is, building a company is often more about endurance than genius." [^15]

> "Entrepreneurship is glorified, but the reality is lonely, gritty, and often messy." [^15]
