## Background

Vijay Shekhar Sharma (born June 7, 1978, in Aligarh, Uttar Pradesh) is the founder and CEO of One97 Communications Limited and its consumer brand Paytm [^1]. He completed a B.Tech from Delhi College of Engineering (now Delhi Technological University) at age 19 [^1]. His first venture, indiasite.net, was founded in 1997 while he was still in college and sold for approximately $1 million two years later; he then founded One97 Communications in 2000, initially offering mobile value-added services and content [^1]. Paytm was launched under One97 in 2010 as a mobile recharge platform and grew into one of India's largest digital payments companies [^1].

In November 2021, Sharma led Paytm's IPO, which raised approximately $2.5 billion at a valuation of roughly $19 billion — India's largest initial public offering at that time [^1]. Sharma stepped down from the Paytm Payments Bank board in 2024 following regulatory action by the Reserve Bank of India [^1]. He was named to Time Magazine's 100 Most Influential People list in 2017 and received the Yash Bharati award (Uttar Pradesh's highest civilian honor) in 2016 [^1].

Sharma has been an active angel investor since at least 2010, when he participated in Milaap's seed round [^2]. In February 2020, he incorporated two personal investment vehicles — VSS Investco Private Limited and VSS Holdings — with his wife Mridula Parashar as the only other director [^3]. VSS Investco initially received Rs 8.5 crore in capital, with VSS Holdings capitalised at Rs 54 lakh [^3]. In October 2023, he launched the VSS Investments Fund, a SEBI-registered Category II Alternative Investment Fund with a base corpus of Rs 20 crore and a Rs 10 crore green shoe option (Rs 30 crore total), sponsored by VSS Investco and focused on artificial intelligence and electric vehicle startups [^4] [^5].

Sharma has also served as anchor investor in Roots Ventures' maiden Rs 200 crore fund, announced in June 2019 [^6].

## Stated Thesis

Sharma has publicly described the VSS Investments Fund as focused on artificial intelligence and electric vehicles, targeting Indian startups "incubated in India and specially focused to serve Indian consumers and businesses" [^4]. At the launch of the fund in October 2023, he said: "The Indian startup ecosystem has some of the brightest entrepreneurs in the world" [^4].

He has framed some of his personal angel investments — particularly in independent media companies such as The Ken and FactorDaily — as ecosystem-support rather than commercially-driven bets: "I personally don't have the ambition to invest in media startups but I definitely have a personal intention to support the startups in media who need support so that they can build a true north in the news. That's why I invested in The Ken and Factor daily" [^7].

At TechSparks 2023, Sharma emphasized that his primary metric for evaluating companies is free cash flow rather than accounting profit, and that an entrepreneur's job is to "keep the company relevant" as markets shift [^8].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 20 investments verified through independent press coverage, Sharma's actual investment behavior shows the following patterns. Aggregator platforms (Tracxn, CB Insights, PitchBook) report between 52 and 86 total investments across Sharma personally and VSS Investco [^9] [^10]; this analysis covers a subset with contemporaneous press verification.

**Sector distribution** (based on 20 verified investments):
- Consumer / D2C / retail: 6 of 20 (Innov8, Treebo, Yoho, Evenflow, Bombay Shirt Company [per Wikipedia], NoBroker)
- Media / content / OTT: 4 of 20 (The Ken, FactorDaily, STAGE, thumpN)
- EdTech / gigs: 3 of 20 (Unacademy, Masai School, TapChief)
- Deep tech / spacetech / dronetech / EV: 4 of 20 (Kawa Space, TechEagle, Ola Electric, Tvasta per VSS Investco disclosure)
- Health / wellness: 2 of 20 (GOQii, Karkinos Healthcare)
- SaaS / marketplace: 1 of 20 (Supersourcing)

**Stage distribution:** Predominantly seed and angel rounds. Aggregator data classifies Sharma's personal portfolio as "primarily pre-seed and seed" with follow-on Series A checks [^10]. VSS Investco has a Tracxn-listed distribution weighted toward seed (25 of 37 investments) and Series A (5 of 37) as of 2026 [^11].

**Geographic concentration:** Overwhelmingly India-focused. VSS Investco's Tracxn profile shows 35 of 37 companies in India, one in the UK (Leverage Edu), and one in the US [^11].

**Check size:** Sharma's earliest angel checks were small ($75K in Milaap in 2010; $150K in TapChief in 2016; $126.5K shared across seven investors in Sourceasy) [^2]. Later personal or VSS Investco checks have been larger — notably ₹75 Cr into Ola Electric's Series C in December 2021 [^12] and participation in the ₹35.7 Cr ($3.75M) pre-seed for thumpN in July 2026 [^13].

**Founder / co-investor patterns:** Sharma frequently co-invests alongside other Indian super-angels and operator-investors: Kunal Shah (CRED), Rajan Anandan (Google/Peak XV), Girish Mathrubhootham (Freshworks), Vishal Gondal (GOQii), and Vikram Limaye (IDFC Bank) appeared with him in Innov8's 2017 angel round [^14]. He co-invested with Sachin Bansal, Binny Bansal, Kunal Shah, Sandeep Tandon and Ashish Tulsian in Unacademy's 2016 angel round [^15]. In Masai School's 2021 angel round he was one of 21 named investors alongside Kunal Shah, Mohit Tandon (Delhivery), and Vipin Pathak (Cars24) [^16].

**Notable exits:** VSS Investco realized a public-market exit via Ola Electric's August 2024 BSE/NSE listing [^11]. Additional aggregator-reported exits include Unacademy, MediBuddy, Innov8, IIMjobs, FlyRobe, ShopKirana (acquired by Udaan, July 2025), and Karkinos Healthcare (December 2024) [^10] [^11].

**Structural note on personal vs. corporate capital:** From 2010–2019, most publicly reported deals were done in Sharma's personal name. Since 2020, deals are increasingly done via VSS Investco (family office) or the VSS Investments Fund (SEBI AIF). Founders should confirm which vehicle is participating when negotiating terms.

## In Their Own Words

On the Indian startup ecosystem, at the October 2023 launch of the VSS Investments Fund: "The Indian startup ecosystem has some of the brightest entrepreneurs in the world" [^4].

On his rationale for backing independent media: "I personally don't have the ambition to invest in media startups but I definitely have a personal intention to support the startups in media who need support so that they can build a true north in the news. That's why I invested in The Ken and Factor daily" [^7].

On Sachin Bansal, when asked to name his favorite Indian entrepreneur: "Sachin Bansal because he is the reason this country has this bright vibrant startup ecosystem" [^26].

On competitive dynamics in Indian consumer tech, in the same Inc42 interview: "Technically India will have three ecosystems… In the end, it will be these ecosystems" [^26].

On loneliness at the top, on FactorDaily's Outliers podcast (December 25, 2017): "When I lost my father few weeks ago, I also lost the only one who understood what I really do. Now, I have no one who understands me, and that will remain a gap forever" [^27].
