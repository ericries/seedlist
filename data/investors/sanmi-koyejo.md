## Background

Sanmi Koyejo is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Stanford University, where he directs the Stanford Trustworthy AI Research (STAIR) lab [^1][^2]. He is also an Adjunct Associate Professor at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, having previously held a faculty position at UIUC before joining Stanford [^1][^2]. Prior to his academic career, Koyejo served as a research scientist at Google DeepMind, where his work on AI evaluation, adversarial robustness, and safety assessment was implemented in production systems [^3].

Koyejo is a co-founder and Chief AI Officer of Virtue AI, an enterprise AI security and safety platform he founded in 2024 with Bo Li (CEO), Dawn Song, and Carlos Guestrin [^3][^4][^5]. The four co-founders are all academics with a combined 80+ years of foundational research in AI safety and security [^4].

His awards include the Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers (PECASE), an Alfred P. Sloan Research Fellowship, an NSF CAREER Award, the Skip Ellis Early Career Award, a Kavli Fellowship, and outstanding paper awards at NeurIPS and UAI [^2][^3]. He also serves on the board of the Neural Information Processing Systems Foundation, the Association for Health Learning and Inference board, and as President of the Black in AI organization [^2].

## Stated Thesis

Koyejo does not operate as a traditional angel investor with a public thesis. His public "investment thesis" is expressed through his research focus and his role at Virtue AI. He has publicly stated that his lab and Virtue AI focus on "measuring and operationalizing AI safety and security" [^6].

Virtue AI publicly positions itself around a specific belief: enterprises should not have to choose "between moving quickly with increased risk or implementing cumbersome safety measures that dramatically slowed innovation" [^4]. This is the closest analogue to a stated investment thesis for Koyejo — he backs (via founding) technical approaches that build safety into the AI stack rather than treating it as an add-on.
