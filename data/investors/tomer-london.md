## Background

Tomer London is co-founder and Chief Product Officer of Gusto, the payroll, benefits, and HR platform for small and mid-sized businesses [^1][^2]. He co-founded the company as ZenPayroll in November 2011 alongside CEO Joshua Reeves and Edward Kim, and the company went through Y Combinator's Winter 2012 batch [^3][^2]. As of recent reporting, Gusto serves over 400,000 businesses and has been valued at approximately $9.5 billion [^3]. Total capital raised across Gusto's history exceeds $700 million from investors including General Catalyst, CapitalG, Kleiner Perkins, T. Rowe Price, and Fidelity, alongside individual backers such as Sam Altman, Max Levchin, and Tobias Lütke [^4].

London grew up in Haifa, Israel. He taught himself to program at age 12, initially building an inventory management system for his father's clothing store [^1][^3]. He earned a B.S. in Electrical Engineering from the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology and subsequently completed both an M.S. and Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering at Stanford University, where he met his future Gusto co-founders [^1][^3]. Before Gusto, he co-founded Vizmo, a company building mobile self-service technology for enterprises [^1]. He was named to Inc.'s 30 Under 30 list in 2013 for his work on ZenPayroll [^5].

## Stated Thesis

London does not publish a formal angel-investing thesis. In his role as an angel, his focus areas — per his profile on multiple aggregator databases — are API, developer tools, enterprise SaaS, fintech, and health/wellness, spanning both Israel and the US/Canada [^6][^7]. He operates with a typical check size of around $50K, invests at pre-seed through Series A, and does not lead rounds [^6].

In his 20VC podcast appearance, he has discussed how founders should approach fundraising through a "values/motivation alignment test" rather than optimizing purely on valuation, and why "delight" is a critical variable in consumer-facing product design [^8]. Speaking on entrepreneur Alejandro Cremades' podcast, London emphasized that customer conversations build the intuition needed to build products, and that "the thing that pops up in my mind is probably the hardest and most important…is all around people" [^9].

## In Their Own Words

On building product intuition through customer conversations:

> "When you talk with a lot of your customers and you talk with them about their day, talk with them about the problems that they're seeing, what ends up happening, it's not just data points. You build intuition." — Tomer London, interview with Alejandro Cremades [^9]

On listening for emotional signal:

> "Look for emotions. When the customer is super pissed off, or when the potential customer is super excited about something, that's where your ears need to perk up." — Tomer London, interview with Alejandro Cremades [^9]

On the risk of over-relying on intuition:

> "You've got to spend time with the customer. There's I think the one mistake people make is to just completely rely on their intuition without even talking with customers." — Tomer London, interview with Alejandro Cremades [^9]

On go-to-market as an equal-weight problem:

> "The primary one is when you build a plan and try to figure out how the startup is going to work, obviously the product and technology are critical. But if you have that and you have no go to market, you're dead." — Tomer London, interview with Alejandro Cremades [^9]

On the shift between phases of company building:

> "What makes zero to one successful is not what makes one to end successful." — Tomer London, interview with Alejandro Cremades [^9]
