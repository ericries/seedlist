## Background

Othman Laraki is co-founder and CEO of Color Health, the healthcare company he started in 2015 alongside Elad Gil, Nish Bhat, and Taylor Sittler [^1][^2]. A Casablanca native based in San Francisco, he holds degrees in computer science and management from Stanford University and MIT [^3][^4].

Laraki began his career at Google, where he managed products including Google Toolbar, Google Gears, early Firefox extensions, and FastNet — a real-time fetching and caching infrastructure [^4][^5]. He left Google to co-found MixerLabs, a geolocation infrastructure company (creator of GeoAPI) that Twitter acquired in December 2009 [^4][^5][^6]. At Twitter, he served as Vice President of Product for Growth, Revenue and International, and by his own account helped grow the user base from roughly 15 million to nearly a quarter billion users [^4][^5].

After leaving Twitter around 2013, Laraki and Elad Gil began exploring how to apply data science and software methods to human genomics — the seed of what became Color Genomics, which launched publicly in 2015 with $9M in funding led by Khosla Ventures and Formation 8 [^1][^2]. Gil initially served as CEO; Laraki became CEO in December 2016 when Gil moved to the chairman role [^7]. Color pivoted from consumer genetic testing to population health and COVID-19 testing during the pandemic, then refocused again in 2023 as a Virtual Cancer Clinic providing end-to-end cancer prevention, screening, diagnosis, and survivorship care [^2][^8].

Alongside his operating role, Laraki has been an active angel investor and advisor for more than a decade. He was an advisor to Pinterest from 2013 to 2017 and describes himself as a long-time investor in and advisor to companies including AngelList, Slack, Instacart, Airtable, and Pinterest [^3][^9]. He also runs a syndicate on AngelList [^10].

## Stated Thesis

On his AngelList syndicate page, Laraki describes his focus in three areas: "consumer products that he enjoys using or have impressive metrics," "infrastructure companies with strong technological backing," and "health-related companies" [^10]. He runs the syndicate as a way to share his personal deals — "This syndicate will be following my personal investments" — and notes he expects roughly five deals per year and typical check sizes around $131.5K [^10].

Laraki has publicly framed his healthcare investing around access rather than productivity. On X in 2024 he wrote about Color's bet that "the GenAI revolution is the biggest opportunity to improve access and immediacy to critical health information & services" [^11].

## In Their Own Words

On learning a new market as an outsider:

> "When you enter a new space, it's important to be willing to question the status quo, but also have a lot of humility." [^18]

> "You're going to be naive at the beginning, and that naivete is excused if you show up with humility." [^18]

On decision-making:

> "Many 'good' plans fail. You need to be willing and able to assess and adjust." [^18]

> "I realized over time that when I saw people behaving in ways that I thought made no sense, it was actually because I just didn't understand their incentive structure." [^18]

On product-market fit and go-to-market:

> "Who influences, who decides, who sets the price, who sets the terms, how the transaction actually occurs and how you get paid — this is what you have to untangle." [^18]

> "Make sure you're not trying to sell a steering wheel to someone who wants to buy a car." [^18]

On founding rigor:

> "The rigor you establish at the beginning will last for the entire life of the company, for better or worse." [^18]

On MixerLabs' acquisition by Twitter:

> "joining Twitter gave us the kind of resources to really scale this up." [^6]

> "Twitter's view on geolocation was extremely aligned with how we saw the industry evolving." [^6]

On Twitter's geo product strategy:

> "at the end of the day the business model is not going to be around owning the geo data." [^6]
