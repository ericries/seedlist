## Background

Matt Pincus plays bass in the New York hardcore band Judge, which he joined at 17 [^1]. He told VICE that "Hardcore and straight-edge cleaned me up and put me on the right path" [^5]. He holds a BA (1995) and an MBA (2002) from Columbia [^1]. In college he interned at EMI and Jive Records and was a campus representative for Atlantic Records [^1]. After business school he worked as a strategy associate at EMI [^1].

In 2004 he founded SONGS Music Publishing with Ron Perry and invested $5 million of his own money [^1]. SONGS signed The Weeknd in September 2013 and Lorde in November 2013, in a deal reported at $2.5 million [^2]. It represented about 300 artists, including Diplo [^1]. Kobalt Music Group bought SONGS in December 2017 for about $160 million [^1][^2]. Pincus also co-founded the record label RECORDS with Barry Weiss [^1]. RECORDS is a joint-venture label with Sony Music, founded in 2015 [^9]. Billboard named him to its Power 100 in 2017, and he testified to the U.S. Senate in 2015 on songwriter royalty reform [^1]. In 2018 he joined LionTree as executive-in-residence and took an advisory role at Snap [^1].

He launched MUSIC in partnership with LionTree after the SONGS sale [^3]. In May 2022 MUSIC announced $200 million of initial capital from LionTree, JS Capital Management and Schusterman Family Investments [^3][^4]. It describes itself as "the first ever diversified investment company focused exclusively on music businesses" [^3]. In 2020 he co-founded Trust Records with Joe Nelson to "promote, preserve, and protect punk and hardcore music" [^6].

## Stated Thesis

Self-reported. MUSIC invests in three areas: music technology, record labels and music publishers, and web3 companies [^3]. Pincus has said that MUSIC invests "in talent, creativity, repertoire and innovation" and that good people "reside all across the capital stack - from venture to growth to private equity to yield" [^4]. He describes the aim as betting "on talent with flexible capital, ready for the long term" [^3].
