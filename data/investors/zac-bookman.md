## Background

Zachary Bookman is the Co-Founder and Chairman Emeritus of OpenGov, an AI-enabled cloud software company serving roughly 2,000 U.S. cities, counties, state agencies, school districts, and special districts [^1]. He co-founded OpenGov in Redwood City in 2012 with Joe Lonsdale [^2], and stepped down as CEO in April 2026 to become Chairman Emeritus [^1][^2].

Before OpenGov, Bookman was a partner at Formation 8, the San Francisco venture fund co-founded by Joe Lonsdale [^1]. Earlier, he served as an advisor to U.S. Army General H.R. McMaster on the Anti-Corruption Task Force at ISAF headquarters in Kabul, Afghanistan; practiced trial litigation at Keker, Van Nest & Peters in San Francisco; and clerked for Judge Sandra S. Ikuta on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit [^1][^2]. He studied transparency and corruption in Mexico as a Fulbright Fellow (2007-2008) [^2].

Bookman holds a BA from the University of Maryland, a JD from Yale Law School, and an MPA from the Harvard Kennedy School [^1][^2]. He currently serves on multiple boards and councils, including the Hoover Institution and the Council on Foreign Relations [^1]. He has been named a World Economic Forum Technology Pioneer and one of Goldman Sachs' 100 Most Intriguing Entrepreneurs [^1].

OpenGov was acquired by Cox Enterprises in February 2024 at a $1.8 billion valuation, with existing management (including Bookman) rolling over significant equity and continuing to operate the company under the Cox umbrella [^3][^4].

## Stated Thesis

Bookman does not run a fund and has not published a formal angel-investing thesis. His public commentary focuses on mission-driven infrastructure companies serving large, unglamorous end-markets — the same category OpenGov itself occupies. His interview with Harry Stebbings on 20VC, released in 2024, was titled "From Unsexy Startup to $1.8BN Acquisition… Why VCs and Founders are Fundamentally Misaligned… Why Valuations and Fundraising are BS" — signaling skepticism toward growth-at-any-cost venture behavior [^5][^6].

## Inferred Thesis

The verified angel portfolio is small (2 dated investments) and precludes reliable percentage breakdowns. What can be observed:

- **Public-sector and civic-infrastructure adjacency.** SewerAI (municipal wastewater inspection using AI) [^7] and Flock Safety (license-plate readers used by more than 4,000 U.S. law-enforcement agencies) [^8] both sell to state and local government — the same buyer OpenGov spent a decade selling to. This is Bookman's clearest edge as an angel.
- **Later-stage angel entries, not pre-seed.** The two verified checks are into a Series B and a Series C respectively, not early-stage bets. This is consistent with a busy operator writing selective checks into companies with already-proven traction.
- **Aggregator-listed but unverified**: Flexport, Addepar, Zamp, Airbase (exited September 2024), and Finia are listed as portfolio companies in Tracxn and PitchBook summaries [^9][^10], but individual round dates and check sizes could not be verified from primary announcement sources. These are directionally consistent with a fintech/enterprise-software tilt beyond civic infrastructure.

## In Their Own Words

Bookman appeared on Harry Stebbings' 20VC podcast in late 2024 to discuss OpenGov's path from founding to the $1.8B Cox acquisition [^5]. The episode listing describes his positions on the following topics — misalignment between VCs and founders, why valuations and fundraising are "BS," and lessons from Josh Kushner and Marc Andreessen — though a verbatim transcript was not accessible to confirm exact wording of individual claims [^5][^6].

At the time of the Cox acquisition, Bookman framed the deal as continuity rather than exit: OpenGov's leadership team rolled over "a significant portion" of its equity and the company continues to be run by existing management under Cox [^4].
