## Background

Deep Kalra is the Founder and Group Chairman of MakeMyTrip, India's largest online travel company and a NASDAQ-listed business he founded in 2000 [^1][^2]. He earned a Bachelor's degree in Economics from St. Stephen's College, Delhi (University of Delhi) in 1990, followed by an MBA from the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad (IIM-A) in 1992 [^1].

After IIM-A, Kalra spent three years at ABN AMRO Bank, then worked with AMF Bowling to establish bowling alleys in India [^1]. He joined GE Capital in 1999 as Vice President of Business Development before founding MakeMyTrip in 2000 — having recognized the internet's potential while attempting to sell his wife's car online [^1]. MakeMyTrip listed on NASDAQ in 2010, making it one of the first Indian internet companies to go public on a US exchange [^1][^2]. Kalra functioned as Group CEO until August 2013, when Rajesh Magow became Group CEO; in 2022 Kalra transitioned to Group Chairman and Chief Mentor [^1].

Beyond MakeMyTrip, Kalra heads the NASSCOM internet working group and is an executive member [^1]. He is a founder of Ashoka University and serves on its governing body, and he is a founding member of the NGO "I am Gurgaon" [^1]. From 2024 to February 2026 he served as Chair of the World Travel & Tourism Council, India Initiative [^1]. In 2011, KPMG ranked him #1 on its list of most powerful digital influencers in India [^1].

Kalra invests personally as an angel — Tracxn and CB Insights count between 16 and 28 portfolio companies depending on disclosure cutoffs [^3][^4]. His most recent disclosed investment is Lyskraft (Seed, April 2024) [^3][^4][^5].

## Stated Thesis

Kalra has not published a formal angel investing thesis. Aggregator profiles describe his focus as Indian early-stage consumer internet, fintech, healthtech, and edtech [^3][^6].

Speaking at the India Internet Day event organized by TiE Delhi NCR in September 2022, Kalra warned founders about the true cost of equity capital: "While equity money seems cheap, it is the most expensive capital and founders should beware." [^7] He elaborated: "While it does not cost much upfront and founders don't realize it at the beginning, it hits hard later when they see the amount of value they created and gave away to investors." [^7]

He has also publicly cautioned that "growth at any cost" remains a dominant ethos in the Indian startup ecosystem, and urged founders to exercise prudence when scaling [^7]. The throughline across his public statements is capital discipline — he frames himself less as a pure financial backer than as an operator-mentor who has run a public company through multiple cycles.

## Inferred Thesis

This analysis is based on the verified angel portfolio of approximately **16-28 disclosed investments** that aggregator databases (Tracxn, CB Insights, Crunchbase) attribute to Kalra, with full granular round-level data confirmed for a smaller subset (Lyskraft, Scripbox, PlanetSpark, Punt Partners, Eka.Care, Exotel, Mitron, Milkbasket, Ola) [^3][^4][^5][^6][^8]. Percentages should be read as directional given the limited disclosure on smaller checks.

**Sector distribution (verified disclosed portfolio of approximately 9 companies with sector identified):** Consumer internet and consumer commerce — Ola (mobility), Milkbasket (grocery), Mitron (short video), Lyskraft (consumer brands platform) — is 4 of 9 (44%) [^3][^4][^5]. Fintech — Scripbox (wealth management), Punt Partners (broking) — is 2 of 9 (22%) [^3][^4]. Edtech and healthtech — PlanetSpark (edtech), Eka.Care (digital health), Terra.do (climate edtech) — is 3 of 9 (33%) [^3][^6]. SaaS/enterprise — Exotel (cloud telephony) — is 1 of 9 (11%) [^3][^6].

**Stage distribution:** Predominantly seed and early Series A. Lyskraft was a $26M seed round in April 2024 led by Peak XV Partners with Prosus, Sofina, and partners of DST Global; Kalra participated in the syndicate [^5][^9]. Scripbox Series D-II in January 2022 was a follow-on through-line rather than a new entry [^4]. Punt Partners' December 2022 round was a $1.74M seed [^3].

**Geographic focus:** Almost entirely India. Tracxn lists India and the US as Kalra's two geographies, but the US exposure is largely Indian-founder companies registered offshore (e.g., Lyskraft) [^3][^5]. Domestically he is concentrated in Delhi NCR (his home base in Gurgaon), Bengaluru, and Mumbai [^3][^6].

**Founder profile patterns:** Kalra repeatedly backs second-time or operator founders building consumer or fintech businesses for the Indian middle class. Lyskraft is led by Mukesh Bansal (Myntra, Cult.fit founder) and Mohit Gupta (former MakeMyTrip President turned Zomato Food Delivery CEO) — Gupta is a direct MakeMyTrip alumnus [^5][^9]. Eka.Care, Scripbox, and PlanetSpark were all backed by Kalra at growth-of-conviction rounds rather than first checks [^3][^6].

**Co-investor patterns:** Peak XV Partners (formerly Sequoia Capital India), Accel India, Prime Venture Partners, Prosus, and other Indian operator-angels (Ashish Hemrajani of BookMyShow, Binny Bansal of Flipkart, Kunal Shah of CRED) recur as co-investors [^3][^5][^9][^10]. The Punt Partners December 2022 seed included Ashish Hemrajani plus 38 other angels — a syndicate signal that Kalra co-invests heavily with the Indian "operator-angel mafia" rather than alone [^3].

**Exits:** Four disclosed exits — Milkbasket (acquired by Reliance Retail, 2021), Ridlr (acquired by Ola, 2018), Biosense, and Mitron (acquired by Koo, June 2022) [^3][^4]. Ola is his lone unicorn that has not yet exited [^3].

**Notable gap:** Despite founding India's largest OTA, Kalra has very limited disclosed travel-tech investing — his portfolio is broader Indian consumer/fintech/edtech, not a travel rollup. This is the largest delta between stated thesis (aggregator profiles list "travel-tech" as a focus) and actual portfolio behavior.
