## Background

Halle Tecco is an entrepreneur, angel investor, and educator focused on digital health. She founded Rock Health in 2010 — the first dedicated digital-health venture fund and accelerator — and served as CEO until May 2016 [^1][^2]. She co-founded Techammer in 2007 with her husband, Cloudera co-founder Jeff Hammerbacher, as their family investment vehicle [^1][^3]. In 2019 she founded Natalist, a reproductive-health consumer products company, which was acquired by Everly Health in 2021 [^1][^2]. In 2022 she co-founded Cofertility, an egg-sharing platform where she serves as Board Chair [^1][^4].

Tecco holds a B.S. from Case Western Reserve University, an MBA from Harvard Business School, and an MPH in Women's and Reproductive Health from Johns Hopkins University [^1][^2]. She has been an Adjunct Professor at Columbia Business School since 2015, where she teaches an MBA-level digital health investing course, and joined Harvard Medical School as a Course Director in 2024 [^1][^4]. She hosts *The Heart of Healthcare* podcast, and in 2026 published *Massively Better Healthcare: The Innovator's Guide to Tackling Healthcare's Biggest Challenges* with Columbia University Press [^1][^5].

She was named to Forbes 30 Under 30 (2013), Goldman Sachs' Most Intriguing Entrepreneurs (2013), San Francisco Chronicle's Most Powerful Women in Technology (2014), and Fast Company's Most Creative People in Business (2023) [^1][^2].

**Current status as an active investor:** In an April 2025 essay, Tecco publicly announced she has not written a new angel check since 2023 and does not plan to resume [^6]. She now focuses on board work (Collective Health, Cofertility), LP commitments to venture funds, teaching, and content [^6][^7]. She serves as a Board Partner at Giant Ventures with a Series B+ focus [^7]. This profile documents her verified historical angel portfolio; treat "active investor" claims elsewhere with caution.

## Stated Thesis

*(Self-reported: These represent what Tecco says publicly. See Inferred Thesis for analysis of actual investment behavior.)*

Techammer describes itself as "LPs and angel investors supporting founders working on meaningful problems," concentrating on three primary sectors: data science, digital health, and companies accelerating scientific progress, with particular interest in "companies bettering the lives of everyday Americans" [^3].

Tecco's personal focus has been "investing in the future of healthcare since 2010 as an angel, LP, and the Founder of Rock Health" [^4]. Her writing emphasizes healthcare consumerization and women's/reproductive health as areas where entrepreneurship can address systemic gaps [^5][^5].

## Inferred Thesis

Sample size caveat: Tecco has disclosed 53 total angel investments since 2012, but only a subset are individually named in public sources [^6]. The named-portfolio analysis below is based on ~15 verified companies plus categorical patterns Tecco herself has disclosed.

- **Sector concentration:** Overwhelmingly digital health, women's/reproductive health, and consumer healthtech. Named investments (Marley Medical, Hued, Poppy Seed Health, Tia, Aavia, Function Health, Besound Health, Loom) all sit within healthcare/femtech [^8][^9][^10].
- **Stage:** Angel and seed. Approximately 90% of her checks were deployed through scout groups or angel funds rather than direct personal capital, per her own disclosure [^6].
- **Check size:** Small angel checks; $2.9M cumulative across 53 deals implies an average of ~$55K per position [^6].
- **Founder profile:** Notable tilt toward women founders and consumer-facing healthcare products, consistent with her own operating background (Natalist, Cofertility) [^8][^9].
- **Co-investors / LP relationships:** As an LP, she has committed to Union Square Ventures, Oak HC/FT, Seven Seven Six, and Coalition Operators [^7].
- **Realized returns (self-disclosed):** $2.48M cash returned on $2.9M invested (0.85x DPI), with $1.64M unrealized across 26 active positions [^6]. Tecco has said early-vintage returns (pre-2017) were "genuinely great" while the middle 2017-2020 vintage remains largely unrealized [^6].
- **Notable gap vs. stated interest:** Techammer's stated interest in "data science" and "companies accelerating scientific progress" is reflected mainly in her husband's board work (Datavant, PerkinElmer, Emids) rather than her own named angel checks [^3].

## In Their Own Words

On consumer-facing healthcare and Rock Health's founding thesis:

> "We saw that healthcare was about to become consumer-facing in a very real way." — Halle Tecco, Rock Health "Reflections on a decade+ of digital health" [^5]

On the limits of startup-led change in healthcare:

> "Some of the biggest barriers in healthcare cannot be solved by startups alone. Certain problems are rooted in policy, entrenched incentives, or what I call 'the incumbent blockade.'" — Halle Tecco, Rock Health interview [^5]

> "Real change often requires both innovation from builders and policy change that resets the rules of the system." — Halle Tecco, Rock Health interview [^5]

On why she stopped writing angel checks in 2023:

> "When you have 50+ companies in the portfolio, you can't be a real partner to any of them." — Halle Tecco, "How much I've made angel investing — an update," Substack [^6]

> "The asymmetry of longest time horizon, most company risk, most macro risk, no compensation got harder to justify when the rules kept shifting." — Halle Tecco, Substack [^6]

On joining the Collective Health board:

> "I'm thrilled to join Collective Health's board of directors and am eager to help propel the company forward in its mission to make healthcare more affordable." — Halle Tecco, Collective Health press release, July 30, 2024 [^12]
