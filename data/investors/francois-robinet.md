## Background

François Robinet is the Founder and Managing Partner of AVP (Atlantic Vantage Point), the global investment platform that was previously known as AXA Strategic Ventures (2015) and AXA Venture Partners, and that rebranded to AVP as an independent firm following a management buyout from AXA in 2024–2025 [^1][^2][^3][^4]. Robinet founded the firm in 2015 as the venture capital arm of insurer AXA and has served as Managing Partner since [^1][^4][^5].

Prior to founding AVP, Robinet spent most of his career at AXA Group. He was the first Chief Risk Officer of AXA, served as CEO of AXA Rosenberg (a quantitative-equity joint venture between AXA and the Rosenberg founding team in San Francisco), and was Managing Director / CEO of AXA Life Invest, where he led a turnaround of the life insurance entity after the 2008 financial crisis [^1][^2][^6]. AXA's own corporate profile lists him as Managing Partner of AXA Strategic Ventures and CEO of AXA Life Invest [^6].

Robinet holds a Master of Science from Stanford University's School of Engineering, a master's degree from ENSAE Paris Tech (École Nationale de la Statistique et d'Administration Économique), and a PhD from the Centre des Hautes Études d'Assurances (CHEA). He is an actuary by training [^2][^6][^5].

In August 2024, Robinet led AVP's management team in negotiating a buyout of AXA's 70% stake in the firm [^3][^4]. The transaction closed before the end of 2024 and the firm completed its public rebrand to AVP (Atlantic Vantage Point) in April 2025, simultaneously announcing that the European Investment Fund had joined as a co-anchor in AVP Growth Fund I (€1.5B) under the European Tech Champions Initiative [^7][^3]. AVP now manages more than €2.5B across four investment strategies — Venture, Early Growth, Growth, and Fund Investing — from offices in Paris, New York, and London [^7][^3][^8].

Robinet was named to Global Corporate Venturing's Powerlist in both 2022 and 2024, recognizing him among the leading corporate venturing professionals globally [^2][^5]. He was also listed in Institutional Investor's Fintech Finance 35 (2015) and Fintech Finance 40 (subsequent year) [^9][^10].

## Stated Thesis

Robinet publicly frames AVP's mission as building a transatlantic platform that supports technology companies across the full lifecycle. At the firm's rebrand he said AVP aims to be "best-in-class" for investors by supporting tech companies "from early stages to IPO, in Europe and in North America" [^7].

At AVP's five-year mark, he summarized the platform's evolution: "In five years, AVP has delivered on its initial vision and has become a global investment platform in technology, capable of helping create world-class technology leaders in Europe and in the US" [^5].

On the management buyout, Robinet said: "Today marks an incredible milestone for AVP and its team, and we feel both humbled and excited by this opportunity" [^4].

On quantum computing — a sector AVP has publicly entered through Alice & Bob — Robinet has framed AVP's view as moving beyond R&D toward industrial application: "We have been following the field of quantum computing for a long time at AVP and we are now convinced that quantum computing is leaving the pure R&D space and is entering into an 'industrial' phase to soon address 'real-life' use cases" [^11].

AVP at the firm level publicly describes itself as "a multi-stage platform that combines global reach with local expertise," with active engagement as a minority investor at the growth stage, focused on four sectors: enterprise software, fintech/insurtech, digital health, and consumer technology [^8].

## Inferred Thesis

This analysis is based on 12 portfolio companies where Robinet is publicly named as the AVP partner with an investor or board-member role, as listed on Signal/NFX's tracking of his personally-attributed deals and cross-referenced against firm announcements [^12][^13][^14]. AVP's full portfolio is materially larger (60+ direct investments firm-wide); the percentages below should be read as directional for Robinet's personal attribution rather than statistically definitive [^7][^8].

**Sector distribution (12 verified personally-attributed companies):**
- Insurtech / fintech infrastructure: 4 of 12 (33%) — Baobab Group (micro/SME finance, Africa & Asia), Futurae (authentication), SecurityScorecard (security ratings), Unlatch (real-estate transaction software) [^12][^15]
- Digital health: 2 of 12 (17%) — Vida Health (virtual care platform), Incepto (AI for medical imaging) [^12]
- Enterprise software / developer infrastructure: 3 of 12 (25%) — AxonIQ (event-driven microservices), InsideBoard (AI digital adoption), Tracktor (B2B tracking) [^12][^16]
- Consumer / commerce: 2 of 12 (17%) — Veesual (AI virtual try-on for fashion), Bene Bono (online seasonal produce) [^17][^5]
- Deep tech / quantum: 1 of 12 (8%) — Alice & Bob (cat-qubit quantum computing) [^11]

**Stage distribution:** Robinet's personally-attributed deals span the full AVP stack. Verified entry points include seed (Veesual $7.5M seed, 2024), Series B (Alice & Bob €100M, 2025), and earlier-stage venture rounds at AxonIQ, Vida Health, Baobab, and Incepto [^11][^17][^12]. AVP's overall check-size band runs from $3M to $50M+ across Venture/Early Growth/Growth, with Robinet personally engaged at both ends [^9][^8].

**Geographic focus:** Mixed transatlantic, skewing toward Europe for Robinet's personal portfolio. Of the 12 personally-attributed companies, at least 5 are France/Europe-headquartered (Alice & Bob, Veesual, Bene Bono, Incepto, Unlatch, with Baobab Africa-focused) and the remainder are US (Vida Health, SecurityScorecard, Qloo) or international (Futurae — Switzerland; AxonIQ — Netherlands) [^11][^17][^12]. This is consistent with Robinet's Paris seat at the head of the firm; US growth-stage deals at AVP are more commonly attributed to General Partner Alex Scherbakovsky in New York [^18].

**Founder profile patterns:** The personally-attributed portfolio skews to category-defining technical founders in regulated or scientifically rigorous domains — quantum hardware (Alice & Bob), medical imaging AI (Incepto), authentication (Futurae), security ratings (SecurityScorecard). This is consistent with Robinet's own actuarial / risk-management background.

**Co-investor patterns:** Across personally-attributed deals, frequent co-investors include Bpifrance and Future French Champions / FFC (Alice & Bob Series B), Techstars (Veesual seed), and a long tail of European seed/Series A funds. AVP's Alice & Bob co-lead with Bpifrance and FFC is the clearest signal of Robinet's European-sovereign-capital relationships [^11].

**Notable patterns vs. stated thesis:** AVP's firm-level thesis lists four sectors equally (enterprise software, fintech/insurtech, digital health, consumer). Robinet's personally-attributed track record adds a fifth meaningful theme — deep tech / scientific computing — exemplified by Alice & Bob, which is not a headline sector on AVP's website but receives Managing-Partner attention. The Baobab board seat (microcredit in Africa and Asia) also signals a sustainable-finance interest that is not part of AVP's stated commercial thesis [^14][^11].
