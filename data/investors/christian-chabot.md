## Background

Christian Chabot is the co-founder of Tableau Software, the data visualization company acquired by Salesforce in June 2019 in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $15.7 billion [^1][^2][^3]. He founded Tableau in 2003 with Stanford PhD Pat Hanrahan and Chris Stolte, based on research the two academics had done at the Stanford Computer Graphics Laboratory [^2][^4]. Chabot served as Tableau's CEO from 2003 to 2016 and as Chairman of the Board until 2019, when the Salesforce acquisition closed [^2][^5]. He led the company through its May 2013 NYSE IPO after the founders had raised approximately $15 million in venture capital [^1][^2].

Before Tableau, Chabot was an investor at SoftBank Venture Capital (later Mobius Venture Capital), where he specialized in enterprise software [^4][^5]. Earlier, he co-founded and served as CEO of BeeLine Software, a next-generation digital mapping company acquired by Vicinity Corporation (NASDAQ: VCNT) [^4][^5]. He began his career as a data analyst at Cornerstone Research [^4].

Chabot holds a BS from Stanford's School of Engineering, an MSc from the University of Sussex (Robert Bosch Foundation Fellow), and an MBA from Stanford Graduate School of Business [^1][^4]. He is the author of *Understanding the Euro: The Clear and Concise Guide to the Trans-European Currency* (McGraw-Hill, 1998) [^4]. In 2016 he received New Enterprise Associates' Outstanding Leadership Award [^5].

As of July 2026, Forbes lists Chabot as a self-made billionaire with a net worth of approximately $1.1B and ranks him #3224 (previously #3185) on its billionaires list [^1]. According to Forbes, "Chabot now focuses most of his time on his two nonprofits: The Holocene Foundation and Washington Neurodiversity Project" [^1][^7].

## Stated Thesis

*(Self-reported: no public stated investing thesis has been located for Chabot post-Tableau. This section reflects his views as a founder and operator.)*

Chabot is a well-known advocate for capital efficiency and delayed fundraising. Tableau took only ~$15M in venture capital across its private life before its IPO — an unusually small amount for a company that reached its public-market valuation [^2][^6]. His public commentary emphasizes bootstrapping, revenue-financed growth, and skepticism of raising large sums early:

> "I believe it's a myth that you need to bring a lot of capital to the table in order to be able to bootstrap. Frankly, I don't think most people are willing to make the sacrifices that you need to make to be an entrepreneur and to bootstrap a company from nothing." — Christian Chabot [^8]

## In Their Own Words

On Tableau's long journey to visibility:

> "We've been working our whole lives to become an overnight success." — Christian Chabot, GeekWire Q&A, 2013 [^11]

On skepticism about early large fundraises:

> "I believe it's a myth that you need to bring a lot of capital to the table in order to be able to bootstrap. Frankly, I don't think most people are willing to make the sacrifices that you need to make to be an entrepreneur and to bootstrap a company from nothing." — Christian Chabot [^8]

> "The hardest thing you have to do when you bootstrap is to work for free… you have to be able to adjust your lifestyle for some period of time, maybe a year or year-and-a-half." — Christian Chabot [^8]

On investor relationships in the Tableau context:

> "Great, great investors want to get involved and they know there's going to be subsequent fundraising opportunities." — Christian Chabot [^8]

On the founding team dynamic:

> "We had a great journey among the three founders. I can't think of a single instance over that entire journey that we had to fight. And I think one reason for that is there were some, some values and some ways of looking at life that we all share." — Christian Chabot, Logan Bartlett Show discussion of Tableau's founding [^6]
