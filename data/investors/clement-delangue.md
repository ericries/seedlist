## Background

Clément Delangue is a French entrepreneur, co-founder and CEO of Hugging Face, an open-source machine learning platform he founded in 2016 in New York City alongside Julien Chaumond and Thomas Wolf [^1]. Born in La Bassée, a small town in northern France, Delangue became one of France's top eBay sellers by age 17 [^2]. He earned a Master in Management from ESCP Business School (2008–2012) and took non-degree computer science coursework at Stanford (2011–2012) [^2].

Before Hugging Face, Delangue held product roles at Moodstocks (a computer vision ML startup later acquired by Google in 2016), eBay, VideoNot.es, Makesense, and Curioos [^2]. Hugging Face was originally launched as a chatbot app targeted at teenagers; after open-sourcing the model behind the chatbot in 2019, the company pivoted to become a platform for machine learning models, datasets, and applications [^1]. As of 2025, Hugging Face has approximately 250 employees and had reported revenue of $15 million in 2022 [^1]. The company acquired French robotics startup Pollen Robotics in April 2025 [^1].

Delangue is based in Miami, Florida [^3]. He is a prolific angel investor: by his own account, he had made "100 angel investments in the past two years" as of a 2023 interview, mostly in the community around Hugging Face [^4].

## Stated Thesis

Delangue's public angel investing thesis centers on open-source AI, technical founders, and the Hugging Face community. In a 2023 Fast Company interview announcing his partnership with Factorial Capital, he framed his approach around founder-to-founder investing: "Founders love working with founders, and Factorial enables that" [^3]. He specifically cited excitement about "backing incredible founders like Substrate [and] Modal through this partnership" [^3].

He has repeatedly emphasized the importance of scientific/technical co-founders: "Having one co-founder who is a scientist, I think is a big, big plus" [^4]. He also argues AI startups require a different playbook from traditional software: founders should "trash their lean startup book when they're starting an AI company" [^4].

## Inferred Thesis

Only a small number of Delangue's angel investments have been independently verified through primary sources — far fewer than the 100+ he claims. The sample below (7 verified rounds) is too small to compute reliable percentages, so the analysis is qualitative:

- **Stage:** Predominantly pre-seed and seed (Twin Labs pre-seed, Resolvd AI pre-seed, MicroFactory pre-seed/seed, Helical seed, Boltz seed). Occasional Series A follow-on (Beside, Flower Labs) [^3][^5][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10].
- **Sector:** Overwhelmingly AI/ML infrastructure and applications. Includes open-source AI infrastructure (Nomic AI/GPT4All, Flower Labs — federated learning), AI-native applications (Beside — AI for calls/texts, Twin Labs — AI agents), AI for science (Helical — bio foundation models), AI observability (Resolvd AI), robotics (MicroFactory, Boltz), and AI dev-tools (Substrate, Modal) [^3][^5][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10].
- **Geography:** Both US (New York, San Francisco, Miami) and Europe (France, Germany, Switzerland). Notable French-founder concentration, likely reflecting his own network.
- **Check size:** Via Factorial Capital, ~$100K on average [^11]. Individual angel checks outside Factorial are undisclosed.
- **Co-investor patterns:** Matthew Hartman/Factorial Capital is the most frequent co-investor by far (Delangue is Factorial's founding sourcing partner) [^11]. Other repeat co-investors: Olivier Pomel (Datadog CEO), Naval Ravikant, Betaworks, and fellow Hugging Face co-founders Julien Chaumond and Thomas Wolf [^6][^7][^8].
- **Founder profile pattern:** Technical/scientific founders building open or infrastructure-layer AI products. Notable concentration of ex-DeepMind, ex-OpenAI, and Hugging Face community founders (Twin Labs' cap table includes DeepMind and OpenAI alumni) [^6].

**Sample size caveat:** This analysis draws on 7 verified rounds (2024–2026). Delangue has publicly claimed ~100 investments, so the observed patterns should be treated as directional rather than comprehensive.
