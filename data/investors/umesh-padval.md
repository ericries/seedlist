## Background

Umesh Padval is Managing Partner of Seligman Ventures, the $500M early-stage venture arm of $30 billion asset manager Seligman (Columbia Threadneedle) that launched publicly in February 2026 [^1][^2]. He leads the fund alongside Managing Partner Ashish Kakran and CFO/Operating Partner Eddie Ackerman [^1][^2].

Before Seligman Ventures, Padval spent more than eight years as a Managing Director at Thomvest Ventures, where he focused on cybersecurity, cloud, and AI infrastructure investments [^3][^4]. Prior to Thomvest he spent over eight years at Bessemer Venture Partners in Menlo Park [^3]. His operating career includes serving as President and CEO of C-Cube Microsystems, the public video-infrastructure and distribution semiconductor company, which he grew to over $300M in revenue and 500 employees before its acquisition by LSI Logic, where he then served as Executive Vice President [^3][^4]. He first met Paul Wick, Seligman's Chief Investment Officer, in 1999 while running C-Cube [^5].

Padval holds a B.Tech. from IIT Bombay and an M.S. from Stanford University; some sources also cite an M.S. from Pennsylvania State University [^3][^6]. He brings, by his own count, more than 35 years of operating, management, and board experience and 18 years of venture investing [^5]. He has served on 40+ public and private boards over his career [^3].

## Stated Thesis

Seligman Ventures publicly targets four verticals: AI infrastructure, cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity, and modern data-center hardware, investing from early stage through pre-IPO [^5][^7]. Padval has framed the firm's model as bridging early-stage innovation with public-market scale — leveraging the sister mutual-fund business at Seligman to inform which private companies have durable value versus late-stage valuation inflation that doesn't survive an IPO [^2][^5].

He publicly favors a disciplined fund-size posture: "I've always believed in the Benchmark-type model, stay in the $400 million to $800 million-range, build those companies, and get 10x from there" [^2].

His personal thesis skews toward infrastructure over models, and toward technical founders with systems-level insight (see quotes below).

Based on 9 publicly dated investments from the Signal by NFX profile [^8]:

Distribution skews Series A and later (6 of 9 dated rounds), consistent with a thesis-driven infrastructure investor writing conviction checks after early technical validation.

**Sector distribution:**
- AI infrastructure / AI applications: Cohere, Cognichip, Modiqo (unspecified but pre-seed), Relyance AI (AI data privacy) — at minimum 3 of 9 (33%)
- Cloud / cybersecurity infrastructure: StackGen, Bolster AI (fraud/phishing), Isovalent (eBPF networking) — 3 of 9 (33%)
- Semiconductor / hardware: Cognichip (physics-informed AI for chip design), EPIC Microsystems, Avalanche Technology (MRAM, board seat, from separate Thomvest coverage), Impinj (public board seat, RAIN RFID)

Sample size (9 dated rounds) is small, so treat percentages as directional.

**Board footprint.** Padval currently sits on boards at Relyance AI, StackGen, Bolster, Quiet AI, Impinj (public, NASDAQ: PI), and Avalanche Technology, and serves as board observer at Clari and Exaforce [^3][^4]. He joined Cognichip's board with Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan in April 2026 [^7]. This is an unusually board-heavy footprint reflecting his operator background and a preference for direct portfolio engagement rather than passive checkwriting.

**Notable pattern — semiconductor return.** After a decade primarily focused on cybersecurity and AI/cloud software at Thomvest, Padval's 2026 Seligman Ventures rounds have re-centered on semiconductor / chip-design AI (Cognichip Series A, EPIC Microsystems Series A) — closer to his C-Cube operating roots. This is a visible strategy shift, not just marketing.

## In Their Own Words

On what he looks for in founders:

> "We start with the team. Technical founders with inspiring life stories often have the ability to make the impossible possible." — Umesh Padval, Unite.ai interview, 2026 [^4]

On distinguishing durable companies from momentum plays:

> "We spend a lot of time distinguishing between velocity and durability. True defensibility in AI infrastructure comes from deep technical differentiation, workflow insight, and integration into critical systems, not just early traction." — Umesh Padval, Unite.ai interview, 2026 [^4]

On where value accrues in AI:

> "While models are important, we believe a disproportionate amount of value will be created in the infrastructure layers that enable performance, cost optimization, and scalability in real-world environments." — Umesh Padval, Unite.ai interview, 2026 [^4]

On category-defining founders:

> "The founders who stand out combine deep technical expertise with a systems-level understanding of how infrastructure evolves." — Umesh Padval, Unite.ai interview, 2026 [^4]

On physical constraints as design parameters:

> "Founders need to build with constraints in mind. Compute, energy, and data locality are no longer abstract considerations; they are first-order design parameters." — Umesh Padval, Unite.ai interview, 2026 [^4]

On Seligman Ventures' model:

> "We saw an opportunity to build a platform that bridges early-stage innovation with public-market scale." — Umesh Padval, Unite.ai interview, 2026 [^4]

On fund sizing discipline:

> "I've always believed in the Benchmark-type model, stay in the $400 million to $800 million-range, build those companies, and get 10x from there." — Umesh Padval, Fortune, February 12, 2026 [^2]

On the Cognichip thesis:

> "The next wave of progress will not come from incremental optimization of existing design tools, but from using AI to parallelize what has historically been a highly serial chip design process." — Umesh Padval, Cognichip announcement, April 1, 2026 [^7]
