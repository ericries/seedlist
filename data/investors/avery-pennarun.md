## Background

Avery Pennarun is the co-founder and CEO of Tailscale, the WireGuard-based mesh networking company headquartered in Toronto, Ontario [^1][^9]. He is based in Montreal, Quebec [^2]. He is primarily an operator and writer rather than an active angel investor — Crunchbase records only one publicly disclosed angel investment, in the seed round of Tangled [^2].

Pennarun grew up in Thunder Bay, Ontario and attended the University of Waterloo, where he co-founded Net Integration Technologies (also known as Nitix), a maker of plug-and-play networking appliances for small businesses [^3][^4]. IBM acquired Net Integration Technologies in 2008 [^3]. He subsequently spent more than seven years at Google as a senior staff software engineer, including work on Google Fiber's gigabit Wi-Fi and platform analytics, and on Google Wallet/payments [^3][^4].

In 2019 he co-founded Tailscale together with David Crawshaw, David Carney, and Brad Fitzpatrick — all former Google engineers [^1]. Tailscale has since raised four publicly disclosed venture rounds: a 2020 seed; a $12M Series A led by Accel in November 2020; a $100M Series B co-led by CRV and Insight Partners in May 2022; and a $160M Series C led by Accel in April 2025, at an approximately $1.45B post-money valuation [^1][^5].

Beyond Tailscale, Pennarun was appointed in April 2020 to the board of directors of DRT Cyber Inc., the Washington, D.C. cybersecurity subsidiary of Canadian Schedule I bank VersaBank [^6]. He maintains a widely read engineering blog at apenwarr.ca, where he writes long-form essays on software, organizations, and infrastructure [^7]. He is the author of open-source projects including `wvdial`, `sshuttle`, `bup`, and `redo` [^3].

## In Their Own Words

On going down-market first (Stratechery interview, March 13, 2025):

> "It's easy to move up market later, it's very hard to move down market later." [^8]

On product focus over diversification (same interview):

> "I don't think we've maximized the opportunity of our first product yet." [^8]

On infrastructure pragmatism (same interview):

> "Stop implementing your own usernames and passwords. It's the most ridiculous thing at this point." [^8]

On the value of advisors, in a DealMakers Podcast interview with Alejandro Cremades:

> "Somebody has done this before… if you can find that person and ask them for advice even if it's for just 1 hour it can completely stop you from making horrific mistakes." [^4]

> "The difference between success and failure can be like… a little bit of advice that you get from somebody." [^4]

On Tailscale's naming and worldview (Stratechery origin-story blog post on tailscale.com):

> "Tailscale, the name, came before the company. I wanted something that was the opposite of 'Internet-scale.'" [^9]
