## Background

Meredith Fisher, PhD, is a Partner at Mass General Brigham Ventures (MGBV), the venture arm of Mass General Brigham and formerly known as Partners HealthCare Innovation. MGBV was founded in 2008 and invests in early-stage life science companies emerging from Mass General Brigham's research base [^1][^2]. The firm has more than $450M in assets under management, has backed 55 companies since inception, and has produced 20 exits (12 acquisitions and 8 IPOs) [^2].

Fisher focuses on early-stage investments and company creation, and has co-founded multiple MGBV portfolio companies — three as founding CEO [^3][^4]. Before joining MGBV, she was Director of Private Investments at Bracebridge Capital, where she led investments in early-stage life science companies; before that she led business development at Ginkgo Bioworks, was Senior Director of Technology and Business Development at PureTech's Enlight Biosciences, and worked as a scientist in anti-viral drug discovery at Idenix Pharmaceuticals and Anadys Pharmaceuticals [^1][^3].

She holds a PhD in Molecular Microbiology from Harvard University, an MBA from MIT Sloan School of Management, and an undergraduate degree from Mount Holyoke College [^1][^3]. She co-founded Harvard Graduate Women in Science and Engineering (HGWISE) while at Harvard [^1].

## Stated Thesis

MGBV publicly describes its focus as "advancing new life science technologies emerging from Mass General Brigham" across drugs, devices, diagnostics, and digital health, with initial investments at the seed and Series A stages [^2]. The firm emphasizes working with strong co-investor syndicates rather than leading alone in every round [^2].

Fisher's individual focus, as recorded on Signal by NFX, is biotech at seed and Series A in Boston/New England, with checks of $100K-$5M and a $1.5M sweet spot [^5]. On the therapeutic areas she cares about, she has said publicly, referring to Violet Therapeutics: "Synaptic loss is the strongest predictor of cognitive decline for Alzheimer's and other neurodegenerative diseases, yet we have very few programs that address this key pathophysiology." [^6]

## Inferred Thesis

Fisher's activity is unusual for a VC in that a substantial portion of her deals are company-creation efforts spun out of Mass General Brigham research, not external investments. Analysis based on her disclosed board and observer seats plus MGBV's active portfolio:

- **Therapeutics-heavy**: of the 17 active MGBV portfolio companies published, 12 are therapeutics-focused (small molecules, antibodies, AAV gene therapy, cellular therapies) — including her board seats at Mediar Therapeutics (fibrosis antibodies), Nocion Therapeutics (small molecule sodium channel blockers), ClarisBio (front-of-eye therapeutics), FireCyte (neurodegenerative eye disease), and Violet (synaptic preservation) [^2][^3].
- **CNS/neuroscience concentration**: Violet, FireCyte, Spur Therapeutics, and QurAlis in the MGBV portfolio all target CNS or neurodegenerative disease — a theme mirrored in Fisher's direct company-creation work at Violet [^2][^6].
- **Boston/New England only**: MGBV explicitly focuses on companies emerging from Mass General Brigham, so geographic concentration is near-total [^2][^5].
- **Syndicate-partner style**: rather than leading rounds, MGBV typically participates alongside dedicated healthcare investors — Nocion's Series B extension syndicate included Arkin Bio Capital, Monograph, Canaan Partners, F-Prime, Lumira, Morningside, and Osage University Partners [^7]. Violet's 2026 seed extension was led by Lifespan Vision Ventures with MGBV alongside Dementia Discovery Fund, UTEC, and Ono Venture Investment [^6].
- **Realized exits**: portfolio successes include Amolyt Pharma (acquired by AstraZeneca in March 2024 for $800M upfront + up to $250M milestones) and Scorpion Therapeutics (acquired by Eli Lilly in 2025) [^4][^8].

Sample size for personal attribution beyond board seats is small; the pattern is best described qualitatively as an operator-scientist co-founding MGB-originated biotech companies, with a bias toward CNS and immunology therapeutics.
