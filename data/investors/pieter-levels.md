## Background

Pieter Levels (@levelsio) is a Dutch indie hacker and serial bootstrapper based in Lisbon, Portugal [^1]. He is best known for founding Nomad List (now Nomads.com), Remote OK, Photo AI, and Interior AI, which together generate several million dollars in annual revenue and which he operates solo, without employees or outside funding [^1][^2].

Levels first attracted public attention in 2014 with his "12 startups in 12 months" challenge, in which he publicly committed to launching twelve fully functional products — each with a live payment system from day one — over a single year [^3]. The first three projects failed; the fourth, a Google Sheet ranking destinations for digital nomads, became Nomad List [^3]. He has subsequently become a prominent voice in the indie hacker and "build in public" communities, with a substantial X (Twitter) following at @levelsio [^1].

His current portfolio of operating businesses, by his own disclosure, includes Photo AI, MAKE (a self-published book on bootstrapping), Nomads.com, Interior AI, and Remote OK, generating a combined revenue run-rate that he has publicly reported at over $420,000/month at ~80% profit margins [^4].

## Stated Thesis

Levels does not publicly maintain a stated investment thesis in the conventional venture-capital sense. He has been openly skeptical of the venture model and of his own role as a startup investor.

In a January 2024 thread on X, he wrote that his investment portfolio is open-sourced at levels.vc and that "there's no big strategy here," and that he tries "to buy mostly ETFs" [^5]. He has been explicit that he is not running a venture portfolio.

In a 2025 interview on Cheeky Pint, Levels said of investing in startups: "I've been starting to invest" and added, "Every time I invest, I feel bad because I feel like a faker, you know?" [^6]. He cited Cursor as one specific company he has invested in [^6][^7].

On the broader question of venture capital, in the same interview he stated: "What I hate most about VC stuff is when you see people burn money," and "I think it's much more interesting to validate first and build a business first, and see if you get traction" [^6].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size is too small for meaningful inference.** Only one angel investment — Cursor (Anysphere) — is independently verified from Levels' own first-person disclosure as of June 2026 [^6][^7]. He has stated publicly that he does not run a startup-focused investment portfolio, and that the majority of his investable capital sits in ETFs rather than private companies [^5].

What can be qualitatively inferred from the single confirmed data point and his public commentary:

- **Selection bias toward AI developer tools.** His one verified angel check (Cursor) sits squarely in the AI developer-tools category, which is also where his own operating businesses are increasingly focused (Photo AI, Interior AI) [^4][^7].
- **Bootstrapped-first preference.** Levels has repeatedly expressed skepticism of capital-intensive venture-funded businesses [^6][^8] and has built every one of his own businesses without outside funding [^1][^2].
- **Operator-level access.** His angel activity, to the extent it exists, appears to flow from his operator network and personal product use rather than from a sourced deal-flow process. In the Cursor case, he disclosed his investment publicly only when criticizing the product's bugs — noting that he would "not gonna change my tweets just cause I invested" [^7].

Founders considering reaching out to Levels should treat him as a high-signal operator and public commentator on bootstrapping and AI, **not** as an active venture-style angel. He has stated he is in many VCs' DMs and turns down the pitches he receives to invest in his own companies [^6].

## In Their Own Words

On his approach to investing, from a 2025 Cheeky Pint interview:

> "I've been starting to invest... I invested in Cursor, for example, very nice. Every time I invest, I feel bad because I feel like a faker, you know?" [^6]

On venture capital and bootstrapping, from the same interview:

> "What I hate most about VC stuff is when you see people burn money." [^6]

> "I think it's much more interesting to validate first and build a business first, and see if you get traction." [^6]

On founder pressure under venture funding:

> "If you're a founder and you raise money, you kind of need to go big or bust, right?" [^6]

On his own investment portfolio, from X in January 2024:

> "People always ask me how I invest my money, so I open sourced my investment portfolio at [levels.vc]. There's no big strategy here and no certainty that this is a good portfolio either... I try buy mostly ETFs." [^5]

On disclosure and product criticism after investing in Cursor, from X in July 2025:

> "I am investor in Cursor though and am sure they can fix it, I'm not gonna change my tweets just cause I invested though, I'll use whatever works best for me at the current moment." [^7]

On venture-backed exits, from X in June 2024:

> "So many VC funded exits you hear about are actually massive failures and nobody made any money. Which is completely fine if they weren't then hailed as massive successes on TechCrunch and by the investors and founders on X. It's flat out lying." [^8]
