## Background

Justine Moore is a partner on the investing team at Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), focused on AI — both foundation models and applications, with an emphasis on generative media and consumer AI tools [^1] [^2]. She holds a BA in Economics and an MBA from Stanford. Before joining a16z, she was an early-stage investor at CRV backing consumer and SMB tech, and earlier worked on Goldman Sachs' Private Equity team [^1] [^2]. Together with her twin sister Olivia Moore (also a partner at a16z), she co-authors the "Accelerated" newsletter tracking consumer AI trends [^3].

## Stated Thesis

Justine Moore publicly frames her focus as AI foundation models and AI-native applications, with a particular emphasis on generative media (image, video, voice, avatars) and creative-professional tools [^1] [^2]. She and her sister Olivia have written extensively for the a16z blog on themes including consumer AI as a "momentum" business, AI avatars escaping the uncanny valley, and the shape of the generative video landscape [^4] [^5].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on the small number of investments publicly attributed to her (via a16z investment announcements), the pattern is: AI-native generative media companies at seed / Series A stage. Verified investments include ElevenLabs (voice), Black Forest Labs (image generation), Krea (creative interface), and Hedra (character animation) [^6] [^7] [^8] [^9]. Sample size is too small (four verified announcements) to compute reliable percentages, so sector percentages are omitted. The stage pattern is early — Series A or earlier — and check-size ranges reported in third-party trackers describe USD 500K–40M [^2].
