## Background

Bhavish Aggarwal was born on 28 August 1985 in Ludhiana, Punjab, and completed a B.Tech in Computer Engineering at IIT Bombay in 2008 [^1]. He began his career at Microsoft Research India, where he worked for about two years as a research intern and then assistant researcher, filed two patents and published three papers in international journals [^1].

In 2011 Aggarwal co-founded Ola Cabs (now Ola Consumer) with IIT batchmate Ankit Bhati after a bad taxi experience between Bangalore and Bandipur [^1]. He went on to found Ola Electric in 2017 (which IPO'd on the BSE and NSE on 9 August 2024) [^3], and Krutrim in 2023, which became India's first AI unicorn in January 2024 after raising $50M at a $1B valuation led by Matrix Partners India (now Z47) [^4][^5].

Aggarwal was named to Time magazine's 100 Most Influential People list in 2018 [^1]. Following Ola Electric's listing, his stake was valued at roughly ₹12,104 crore (≈ $1.44B), placing him in India's billionaire ranks [^3].

His personal angel investing has run alongside — but is dwarfed by — the companies he founded. Most of his wealth sits in Ola, Ola Electric and Krutrim, and his outside angel cheques have been concentrated in a small number of consumer, mobility and healthcare bets in India.

## Stated Thesis

Aggarwal has not published a personal angel-investing thesis. His public framing of what he backs is dominated by themes he expresses as an operator: building Indian companies that own deep capabilities (manufacturing, battery cells, foundation models) rather than re-packaging foreign technology. In a widely cited LinkedIn / X post around the Krutrim launch he framed India's strategic problem as needing "to grow beyond just being the AI 'use case' capital of the world" and to build domestic platforms instead of being the largest user base for foreign tech [^6].

On Ola Electric's hardware bet, he has argued: "The cost of the battery is 30–40% in an EV. In the ICE era, companies that were successful had complete control over the engines, because that was the most important part of the car. Therefore, in the EV era, those companies that will make the battery-cell technology themselves will become global leaders" [^7].

These are operator statements, not an angel thesis — but the angel portfolio is consistent with them: Indian consumer brands, Indian mobility/EV hardware, and Indian healthcare infrastructure.

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample is small — qualitative description only.** Independently verifiable personal angel cheques are limited to roughly 6–7 investments over a decade (2015–2021). That is too few to compute meaningful sector percentages, and aggregator counts of "14 portfolio companies" [^8][^9] include investments that were actually made by Ola Cabs as a corporate entity (e.g., Vogo, Avail Finance) [^10][^11] rather than by Aggarwal personally. The summary below reflects only investments where Aggarwal is named as a personal participant in a primary press source.

Patterns from the verified set:

- **India-first, exclusively.** Every confirmed angel investment is an Indian company in Bengaluru, Gurugram or Pune: Chaayos (Gurugram) [^12], Tork Motors (Pune) [^13], OfBusiness (Gurugram) [^14], MadRat Games (Bengaluru) [^15], HashLearn (Bengaluru) [^16], Karkinos Healthcare (Bengaluru) [^17], O'Be Cocktails (Bengaluru) [^18].
- **Predominantly seed and Series A.** Confirmed entries are at the angel / seed / Series A stage; he has not appeared as a personal participant in later-stage rounds.
- **Frequent co-investor: Ankit Bhati.** Aggarwal's Ola co-founder participates alongside him in multiple rounds — Chaayos [^12], Tork [^13], MadRat [^15], O'Be Cocktails [^18].
- **Sector mix is broad but operator-adjacent.** Consumer F&B (Chaayos, O'Be Cocktails), electric mobility (Tork Motors), edtech (HashLearn, MadRat Games), B2B commerce (OfBusiness), healthcare (Karkinos). The common thread is Indian operators building physical or distribution-heavy businesses, not pure software bets.
- **Notable outcomes:** OfBusiness reached unicorn status [^8]. Karkinos and HashLearn are listed as portfolio exits by aggregators [^8]. O'Be Cocktails wound down operations in 2024 [^19].
- **No verified investments since late 2021.** The most recent personal angel participation in a primary source is Karkinos in December 2021. From 2022 onward Aggarwal's public capital deployment has been into companies he founded (Ola Electric Series E in October 2023; Krutrim in January 2024) [^4][^20].

## In Their Own Words

On Ola Electric's IPO and listing day (August 9, 2024): "This moment is finally sinking in. It felt like a process till yesterday — where we did our work and put in our time and made India one of the largest EV 2W markets. Our hard work has paid off and the world recognises that. But today it feels like an important duty, a responsibility to double down on our aspirations and to build the country of our dreams. To be dedicated and faithful custodians of public money and faith. We didn't come this far, to just come this far." [^3]

On battery-cell control as the moat for EV companies: "The cost of the battery is 30–40% in an EV. In the ICE era, companies that were successful had complete control over the engines, because that was the most important part of the car. Therefore, in the EV era, those companies that will make the battery-cell technology themselves will become global leaders." [^7]

On public-market discipline: "Public markets want to understand risk. They are not averse to risk." [^7]

On valuation philosophy: "Valuation for me is just a number. I believe in value creation." [^7]

On generational work ethic: "This generation is not driven by the need to have a certain salary to de-risk their life. There has to be one generation that does tapasya. Gen Z wants a purpose in life. It is incumbent upon us to inspire them." [^7]

On India's tech sovereignty (around the Krutrim launch): "We need to grow beyond just being the AI 'use case' capital of the world. We became the social media use case capital of the world with the highest number of users for global platforms. Everyone knows the strategic risk we have because of this and not having our own tech." [^6]

On Ola Electric's Gigafactory ambition (October 2023, alongside the Temasek-led Series E): "At Ola, our vision is to end ICE age in automobiles and our upcoming Gigafactory will be a big leap in India's journey towards becoming a global EV hub." [^2]

On Krutrim's mandate at unicorn announcement (January 2024): "India has to build its own AI, and at कृत्रिम, we are fully committed towards building the country's first complete AI computing stack." [^4]
