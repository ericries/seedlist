## Background

Florian Hagenbuch is a German-born, Brazil-based operator and investor. He graduated from The Wharton School in 2010 with degrees in Finance and International Studies and began his career as an analyst at Goldman Sachs (summer 2008), Blackstone (summer 2009), and Silver Lake Partners (2010–2012) [^1][^2].

In 2012, Hagenbuch co-founded Printi with fellow German-Brazilian operator Mate Pencz. Printi grew into Latin America's largest online printing marketplace [^3]. In October 2014, Vistaprint (Cimpress, NASDAQ: CMPR) acquired a $25M minority position in the company [^3][^4]; Cimpress fully acquired Printi in 2018 [^1].

Also in 2018, Hagenbuch and Pencz co-founded Loft, an iBuyer and transactional real-estate marketplace based in São Paulo [^5]. Loft's Series A was led by Andreessen Horowitz in July 2018 — Loft was a16z's first Brazilian investment [^5][^6]. The company raised a $175M Series C led by a16z in January 2020 [^6], a $425M Series D at a $2.2B valuation led by D1 Capital in March 2021 [^5], and a $100M Series D extension in April 2021 at a $2.9B valuation [^7]. Total capital raised across equity and debt exceeded $1B [^8].

Hagenbuch stepped from co-CEO to Board Member / President of Loft in 2022 and remains Founding Partner at Canary, the LatAm early-stage venture firm he co-founded in 2017 with Marcos Toledo, Mate Pencz, Patrick de Picciotto, and Julio Vasconcellos [^1][^9]. Canary has raised four funds — $50M, $75M, $100M (2021), and $150M (Fund IV) — with LPs including IFC, JICA, and IDB Lab [^9][^10]. He has been an Endeavor Entrepreneur since 2014 [^2].

## Stated Thesis

Hagenbuch publicly describes his investing approach as being anchored in operator empathy and the specific pain points of Latin American markets. Speaking at Wharton's 2014 Latin America Conference, he stated: "Entrepreneurship is like running against a wall, and you keep running into the wall until it starts to fall" [^11]. He advises founders to "be aware of the opportunities that inhabit the pains and problems that our personal experiences offer us" [^11].

Canary's stated focus is early-stage (pre-seed through Series A) LatAm startups across fintech, insurtech, healthtech, e-commerce/social commerce, and B2B infrastructure and marketplaces [^10]. Fund IV named fintech, healthcare, climate tech, consumer, and B2B enterprise software as priority themes [^12].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample caveat: 21 angel/Canary-attributed investments are visible in public aggregators [^13]; Hagenbuch has claimed 100+ personal angel checks [^14], and Canary's platform portfolio includes 100+ companies since 2017 [^10]. The analysis below is qualitative rather than percentage-based due to sample size and attribution overlap between his personal and firm activity.**

Observed patterns from verified deals:

- **Stage: predominantly pre-seed / seed / Series A.** Of nine dated angel entries visible in Signal (2022–2026), five are Series A, three are seed/pre-seed, and one is a later Series A [^13].
- **Geography: heavy LatAm anchor, expanding globally.** Canary's mandate is Brazil and LatAm [^10]. Personal angel activity extends to LatAm-focused companies headquartered outside the region — e.g., Jeeves (built by a founder for LatAm and other emerging markets, based in the US) [^15].
- **Sector: consumer internet + enterprise software split.** Signal specifically ranks Hagenbuch on "Enterprise (Series A)" and "Consumer Internet (Seed)" [^13]. Verified entries include consumer marketplaces (Muni Tienda, Comp) and enterprise infrastructure (3Box, Cado, Niva) [^13].
- **Co-investor patterns.** Hagenbuch appeared in Jeeves' 2021 Series A alongside a syndicate of LatAm unicorn founders including David Vélez (Nubank), Carlos García (Kavak), Sebastián Mejía (Rappi), and Daniel Vogel (Bitso), with a16z leading [^15][^16]. Canary's co-investors on portfolio rounds include Andreessen Horowitz, monashees, Kaszek, QED, and Valor Capital [^10].
- **Founder profile: repeat operators and immigrant/emerging-market founders.** Hagenbuch's own trajectory (German-born, Wharton-educated, LatAm operator) is echoed in his angel picks (Jeeves' Dileep Thazhmon is an emerging-markets fintech founder) [^15].

**Notable divergence from stated thesis:** Canary publicly emphasizes LatAm focus, but Hagenbuch's personal angel checks skew more globally distributed and include US-headquartered companies serving multiple emerging markets [^15].

## In Their Own Words

On the origin of Loft, describing the personal experience that motivated the company:

> "I bought an apartment in São Paulo after going through the broken journey of the Brazilian real estate market... I found out, after buying it, that my apartment is 15 square meters less than what was described in the deed." [^11]

On finding opportunities:

> "I recommend everyone should be aware of the opportunities that inhabit the pains and problems that our personal experiences offer us." [^11]

On entrepreneurship:

> "Entrepreneurship is like running against a wall, and you keep running into the wall until it starts to fall." — Wharton Latin America Conference, 2014 [^11]

On Loft's market position at the time of the Series D:

> "We are a pioneer at iBuyer in Brazil, and we started our marketplace operation at the beginning of the pandemic." [^11]
