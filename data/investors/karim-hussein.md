## Background

Dr. Karim Hussein is Managing Partner at Algebra Ventures, Egypt's largest venture capital firm [^1][^2][^3]. He co-manages the firm alongside Managing Partner Tarek Assaad, with General Partners Laila Hassan and Omar Khashaba [^1][^4].

Hussein returned to Egypt from the United States roughly a decade before joining Algebra to support the local tech ecosystem, first as a mentor and angel investor before joining Algebra Ventures in 2017 [^2][^5]. His pre-VC operating career centered on health-tech and product engineering: he served as SVP of Products and Engineering at WebMD, the leading US online healthcare information provider, and co-founded and served as CTO of Riskclick [^1][^5][^6].

In 2013 he co-founded **D-Kimia Diagnostic Solutions**, a biotech company developing nano-particle-based diagnostic solutions for detecting a broad range of diseases affordably [^2][^5]. Before joining Algebra as Managing Partner, he made angel investments in Shezlong, Simplex CNC Solutions, Integreight, and Eonite Perception (a computer-vision company later acquired by Facebook/Oculus) [^2][^5].

Hussein holds a **PhD in Information Systems from MIT** and a dual BSc in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Carnegie Mellon University [^2][^5]. Forbes Middle East named him among The Middle East's Top Venture Capitalists in 2024 [^7].

**Algebra Ventures** is Egypt's largest VC fund. Fund I closed at ~$54 million in late 2016 and invested in 21 startups [^1][^2][^8]. Fund II closed at $100 million (first close $90M+ in April 2021, exceeded the target), with LPs including IFC, EBRD, EAEF, FMO, BII, MSMEDA, DGGF, and regional family offices [^1][^4][^8]. The firm reports having deployed capital across 35+ portfolio companies with total portfolio value exceeding $825M [^1]. Hussein currently serves on boards including MNT-Halan (fintech / mobility) and Little Thinking Minds (Jordanian edtech) [^5].

## Stated Thesis

Self-reported. Algebra Ventures' publicly stated thesis is to partner with founders "addressing long-standing problems through technology" across four pillars: Egypt & Africa market opportunity, founder support (capital, strategy, operations, team building), impact-driven solutions, and strong co-investor networks [^1].

Hussein's own stated sector priorities, per his 2019 MENAbytes interview and subsequent commentary, are that Algebra is **sector-agnostic but prioritizes businesses where technology is central to competitive advantage and scalable operations** [^2]. He looks for founders who "know what they don't know, have the tenacity to break down barriers" and possess unique market perspectives [^2]. His stated biggest turnoff in a pitch: hearing "I have no competitors" — because "no business exists without competition, otherwise, they're not addressing a real market need" [^2].

On sector focus, Fund II is publicly aimed at **fintech, agritech, edutech, logistics, and healthcare**, with primary focus on Egypt and opportunistic MEA-region investments [^8]. Ticket sizes for Fund II are $500K–$2M [^8]. Geographic scope is Egypt-focused with partial allocation for MENA, East Africa, and West Africa [^5].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on Algebra Ventures' 35+ portfolio companies and Hussein's role as one of two Managing Partners, the following patterns emerge. Note: this is firm-level portfolio analysis; individual deal attribution to Hussein (vs. Assaad or the GPs) is not fully public.

**Sector concentration (based on named Fund I + Fund II portfolio companies: Blnk, DeepEcho, Trella, MNT-Halan, Yodawy, Cartona, Sylndr, elmenus, Connect Money, Dsquares, Khazna, Shift EV, Mozare3, GoodsMart, Little Thinking Minds — 15 named companies) [^1][^4][^8]:**

- **Fintech / embedded finance:** approximately 6 of 15 (40%) — Blnk (POS consumer lending), MNT-Halan (super-app fintech), Khazna (payroll advance), Connect Money (embedded finance), Dsquares (loyalty/payments), Yodawy (health-fintech)
- **B2B marketplaces / e-commerce:** approximately 4 of 15 (27%) — Trella (trucking logistics), Cartona (B2B retail), Sylndr (used cars), elmenus (restaurant tech)
- **Healthtech:** approximately 2 of 15 (13%) — DeepEcho (AI diagnostics), Yodawy (pharmacy)
- **Mobility / cleantech:** approximately 1 of 15 (7%) — Shift EV (electric mobility)
- **Agritech:** approximately 1 of 15 (7%) — Mozare3 (agriculture)
- **Edtech:** approximately 1 of 15 (7%) — Little Thinking Minds

The firm's stated agritech and edutech focus areas are represented but modestly (7% each); **fintech is materially over-represented** at ~40%, consistent with the actual capital-formation opportunity in Egypt.

**Geographic concentration:** Egypt-dominant. Fund II retains opportunistic mandate for East Africa, West Africa, and MENA [^5][^8]. Every named portfolio company above is Egypt-headquartered or Egypt-operating with the exception of Little Thinking Minds (Jordan) — approximately 14 of 15 (93%) Egypt-based.

**Stage focus:** Seed through Series A. Fund II ticket range $500K–$2M for initial checks, with follow-on reserves [^8]. Recent lead-check activity confirms Series A capability (Blnk Series A, June 2026, $12.5M equity round led by Algebra) [^9].

**Co-investor patterns:** For the Blnk Series A, Algebra led with participation from SANAD Fund for MSME, Endeavor Catalyst, and Emirates International Investment Company [^9]. Recurring institutional co-investors across the portfolio include DFIs (IFC, EBRD), impact funds, and regional strategics.

**Founder profile:** The MENAbytes interview and Disrupt Africa profile both describe Hussein's preference for founders addressing base-of-pyramid needs through technology, with an emphasis on tenacity and unique market perspective [^2][^5]. Portfolio composition — heavy financial-inclusion and B2B-infrastructure tilt — supports this pattern.

**Portfolio-level impact:** Per Algebra's own reporting on Fund I, six portfolio companies reached $350M+ valuations and the fund's investments have generated more than 20,000 direct and indirect jobs [^2][^5][^8]. MNT-Halan and elmenus are the most-cited exits/mega-rounds from Fund I.

## In Their Own Words

**On his biggest pitch turnoff:**

> "I have no competitors. No business exists without competition, otherwise, they're not addressing a real market need."

— Karim Hussein, MENAbytes VC Chats interview, 2019 [^2]

**On the African tech opportunity:**

> "The continent has a young and growing population and many challenges. With those challenges come significant opportunities."

— Karim Hussein, Disrupt Africa "Meet the Investor" interview, May 20, 2021 [^5]

**On Egypt's tech transformation, marking Fund II's first close:**

> "I see tremendous opportunities for tech transformation in the Egyptian economy and continue to meet exceptional entrepreneurs."

— Karim Hussein, Algebra Ventures Fund II announcement, April 2021 [^4]

**On what he looks for in founders:** In the same MENAbytes interview, Hussein described seeking founders who "know what they don't know, have the tenacity to break down barriers" and who possess unique market perspectives [^2]. He also stated Algebra prioritizes "businesses that are able to create a competitive advantage through the technology solutions they provide" [^2].

**On Algebra's role beyond capital:**

— Karim Hussein, Disrupt Africa "Meet the Investor" interview, May 20, 2021 [^5]
