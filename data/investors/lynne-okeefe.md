## Background

Lynne Chou O'Keefe is the Founder and Managing Partner of Define Ventures, an early-stage digital health venture capital firm she launched in January 2020 [^1][^2]. Define Ventures focuses exclusively on health tech companies at the incubation, seed, Series A, and Series B stages, and manages approximately $800 million in assets under management as of 2023 [^3][^4].

Prior to founding Define Ventures, Chou O'Keefe was a Senior Partner at Kleiner Perkins, which she joined in 2013 as part of its life sciences group, focusing on digital health and connected devices [^2][^5]. While at Kleiner Perkins, she served on the board of Livongo Health, which was later acquired by Teladoc for $18.5 billion [^2][^5]. Before her venture career, she spent roughly six years at Abbott Vascular and Guidant in product and marketing leadership roles, where she led the commercial launches of more than ten medical device product families in the U.S. and internationally [^5][^6]. Earlier, she worked at Apax Partners on enterprise software venture investing and at Goldman Sachs in the Mergers and Acquisitions group [^1][^5].

Chou O'Keefe holds a B.S. in Industrial Engineering from Stanford University and an M.B.A. from Harvard Business School [^1][^5]. She has been named to the Wall Street Journal's "Women in Venture Capital to Watch" list (2020) [^5].

## Stated Thesis

Define Ventures publicly positions itself as a digital health specialist firm that partners with founders from day zero. On the firm's website, Chou O'Keefe is quoted: "Collaborating with founders to figure out how to take an idea and scale it to build a meaningful business that benefits all of our lives is the most rewarding role I have ever had" [^1].

In a 2023 interview marking the firm's $460 million fund close, she described the opportunity as follows: "The healthcare system is evolving rapidly as multiple healthcare, technology and consumer trends converge, and even with the progress over the last few years, it remains a $4 trillion market opportunity that desperately needs greater digital transformation" [^3].

She has summarized the firm's approach as: "We really focus on the convergence of healthcare" and "We invest in both incubation, seed, Series A and Series B" [^4]. She has emphasized that her firm's pitch evaluation centers on use cases over technology: "It's never about the technology. It's about the use case" [^4].

## Inferred Thesis

This analysis is based on 11 verified Define Ventures portfolio companies identified through primary sources (firm website, press releases, and contemporaneous press coverage). Sample size is small relative to the firm's roughly 21 active investments [^7], so percentages below are presented as counts rather than ratios.

**Sector concentration: exclusively digital health, with sub-sector breadth.** All 11 verified portfolio companies operate in healthcare. Sub-sectors include:
- Consumer / direct-to-consumer healthcare: Hims & Hers, Tia, Folx Health (3 of 11)
- Health system / provider infrastructure: DexCare, Laudio, Cohere Health, Unite Us (4 of 11)
- Chronic disease management: 9amHealth, Livongo (2 of 11)
- Behavioral health: Concert Health (1 of 11)
- Clinical data / life sciences: Verana Health (1 of 11)

**Stages: predominantly seed through Series B, with a willingness to incubate.** Chou O'Keefe has stated check sizes range from $750,000 to over $6 million [^2], consistent with seed and Series A leads. Folx Health was incubated by Define Ventures [^8], and DexCare was incubated at Providence and spun out with Define as Series A lead [^9].

**Behavior: routinely leads rounds despite VCSheet listing.** Verified leads include DexCare Series A (2021) [^9], Concert Health Series B (2022) [^10], and 9amHealth Series B (2026) [^11]. Define also participated as a non-lead in Tia's Series A (Threshold Ventures led) [^12] and Cohere Health's Series C (Temasek led) [^13].

**Board participation pattern.** Chou O'Keefe takes board seats on lead investments: she joined the boards of DexCare (2021) [^9], 9amHealth (2026) [^11], and Hims & Hers (pre-IPO, November 2020) [^14]. She was a director of Livongo from her Kleiner Perkins years [^2][^5].

**Geographic focus: U.S.** All verified portfolio companies are U.S.-based. VCSheet lists geography as "USA" [^7].

**Notable patterns.** Define Ventures has incubated multiple consumer-healthcare companies (Folx [^8]), and per founder Tom Stanis the firm provided meaningful day-zero partnership to his company as well [^15].

## In Their Own Words

On founding Define Ventures and serving healthcare founders:

> "Digital health entrepreneurs deserve investors that have real digital health expertise and experience in building companies in the space. Define Ventures needed to be created because entrepreneurs need focus, expertise, and networks to succeed in their vision of defining healthcare." [^4]

On evaluating pitches:

> "It's never about the technology. It's about the use case." [^4]

On the healthcare opportunity (2023 fund announcement):

> "The healthcare system is evolving rapidly as multiple healthcare, technology and consumer trends converge, and even with the progress over the last few years, it remains a $4 trillion market opportunity that desperately needs greater digital transformation." [^3]

On the value of being a specialist investor (from a TechCrunch interview at fund launch):

> "A lot of founders have said, 'It's so nice not to have to explain the space to you.' Having true partnerships is something they've needed." [^2]

On the rationale for investing in DexCare:

> "DexCare's unique solution to make digitally-enabled care more discoverable and actionable for consumers, while at the same time making it effective for health systems, is the kind of solution that will lead to a better healthcare system for everyone." [^9]

On joining the Hims & Hers board pre-IPO:

> "Hims & Hers has proven to be one of the category defining leaders in digital health… I look forward to joining the Hims & Hers Board as the company works to build a better healthcare experience." [^14]
