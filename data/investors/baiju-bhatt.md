## Background

Baiju Prafulkumar Bhatt was born in 1984/1985 in Poquoson, Virginia, to Gujarati immigrant parents [^1]. His father worked at NASA's Langley Research Center during Bhatt's childhood, an early influence on his interest in space and physics [^1][^2]. Bhatt earned a bachelor's degree in physics and a master's degree in mathematics from Stanford University, completing his graduate work in 2008 [^1].

At Stanford he met Vladimir Tenev, with whom he co-founded Robinhood in 2013, a zero-commission stock trading platform [^1]. Bhatt led product design and user research at Robinhood and the company won an Apple Design Award in 2015 [^1]. Following a May 2018 funding round that valued Robinhood at $6 billion, both co-founders became billionaires [^1]. Robinhood went public on the Nasdaq in July 2021. Bhatt stepped down as co-CEO in November 2020 to become chief creative officer, then departed that role in March 2024 while retaining a seat on the Robinhood board [^1].

In October 2024, Bhatt announced the launch of Aetherflux, a space-based solar power startup based in San Carlos, California, of which he is founder and CEO [^1][^3]. In May 2026 the company rebranded as Cowboy Space Corporation, broadening its scope to in-orbit compute and vertically integrated launch alongside the original space-solar mission, and raised a $275M Series B at a $2B valuation led by Index Ventures [^4][^5]. Forbes estimated his net worth at $2.5 billion as of December 2024 [^1].

## Stated Thesis

Bhatt does not operate a fund and has not published a formal investing thesis. Public statements indicate that his investing interests track his operating focus: space, aerospace, and the physical infrastructure of an emerging "space economy." He is an investor in private space companies including Reflect Orbital and Apex [^6].

Speaking about his motivation for building in space, Bhatt has said: "Until you do stuff in space, if you happen to be an aerospace company, you're actually an aspiring space company" [^3].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size warning:** Only 2-3 angel checks are independently verifiable from public sources. The portfolio is too thin to compute meaningful percentages; qualitative descriptions are used instead.

- **Operator first, investor second.** Bhatt's primary capital deployment is into his own company — he self-funded the first $10M of Aetherflux (now Cowboy Space) before institutional capital arrived [^3]. Angel checks appear to be selective and infrequent.
- **Space-only.** Every publicly known angel investment is in a space or aerospace company: Apex (satellite bus manufacturer, also Cowboy Space's hardware supplier) and Reflect Orbital (orbital sunlight reflection for solar farms) [^6][^7].
- **Deep-tech / hard-tech bias.** Both verified angel bets are capital-intensive hardware companies, not software. Both involve dual-use physics (optics, lasers, orbital mechanics) similar to Cowboy Space's own technology stack.
- **Co-investor patterns.** On Reflect Orbital's seed round he co-invested with Sequoia Capital and Starship Ventures [^6]. He is not yet a frequent name on round announcement press releases, consistent with a selective rather than high-volume angel practice.
- **Strategic adjacency.** The Apex investment is also a commercial supplier relationship — Apex builds the satellite bus for Aetherflux/Cowboy Space [^6]. This suggests Bhatt's checks often blend investment with operational alignment.

## In Their Own Words

On why he left Robinhood for space:

> "My dad worked at NASA through my whole childhood... he was very adamant: 'When you grow up, I'm not going to tell you you should study physics.'" — Baiju Bhatt, TechCrunch, June 2025 [^3]

On the definition of a space company:

> "Until you do stuff in space, if you happen to be an aerospace company, you're actually an aspiring space company." — Baiju Bhatt, TechCrunch, June 2025 [^3]

On Aetherflux's core technology:

> "We take the solar power that we collect from the sun with solar panels, and we take that energy and put it into a set of diodes that turn it back into light." — Baiju Bhatt, TechCrunch, June 2025 [^3]

On the rebrand to Cowboy Space Corporation:

> The name "captures the scale of ambition that we've got with the new set of objectives," painting an optimistic picture of the company "blazing our own trail in space." — Baiju Bhatt, on the Cowboy Space rebrand, May 2026 [^4][^5]
