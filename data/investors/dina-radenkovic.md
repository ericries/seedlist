## Background

Dina Radenkovic (now Dina Turner) is a Serbian-born physician-turned-entrepreneur who is co-founder and CEO of Gameto, a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing cell-engineered therapies for female reproductive health and menopause [^1] [^2]. She earned dual degrees in medicine and physiology from UCL Medical School in London and completed her residency at St Thomas' Hospital in London, with additional research experience at Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust, King's College London, Massachusetts General Hospital (Harvard), and the Buck Institute for Research on Aging [^2] [^3] [^4].

Before founding Gameto in 2021, she joined the SALT Bio Fund in New York as a Partner focused on early- and mid-stage life-sciences investments, and continues to serve as a General Partner at the Programmable Biology SALT Fund [^3] [^4]. In parallel, she is co-founder and Chief Science Officer of Hooke, a longevity research clinic in London launched in partnership with Dr. Eric Verdin of the Buck Institute [^3] [^4].

Gameto has raised approximately $127 million in venture funding across five rounds to date under her leadership, plus a $10 million ARPA-H award [^2] [^5]. The company's lead program Fertilo — a stem-cell-derived therapy that reduces IVF ovarian stimulation from roughly two weeks to two to three days — is the first induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)–derived therapeutic to reach Phase 3 in the United States [^2] [^5].

## Stated Thesis

Radenkovic's public statements as an investor and operator center on "programmable biology" — using engineered cells and stem-cell reprogramming to treat aging-linked diseases, particularly in female reproductive health and longevity [^3] [^4]. Through the Programmable Biology SALT Fund she describes herself as backing "companies that bring new science or technology to improve health and longevity" [^4].

On her personal mission, she has said: "I do not compromise. I want to make menopause optional" [^6].
