## Background

Ryan Hudson is the co-founder of Honey, the shopping and rewards browser extension acquired by PayPal for approximately $4 billion in January 2020 [^1][^1]. He co-founded the company with George Ruan in Los Angeles, launching the first version of the extension in October 2012 [^2]. Before Honey, Hudson worked as an analyst at Forrester Research after graduating from Cornell University with a BS in Operations Research and Computer Science (2002), and later earned an MBA from MIT Sloan School of Management (2007) [^2][^3]. He also served as a product manager at the Pasadena-based ad tech firm OpenX and worked on digital advertising strategy at the Los Angeles Times before starting Honey [^4].

After the acquisition, Hudson and Ruan continued to lead the Honey team as part of PayPal's global consumer product and technology organization, reporting to SVP John Kunze [^1]. Between them, Hudson and Ruan received a pre-tax haul of approximately $1.5 billion from the sale, with Hudson netting roughly $575 million after taxes [^5].

Hudson departed PayPal in 2022 and in 2024 founded Pie (The People's Internet Experiment / pie.org), a browser extension that combines ad blocking with cash-back and affiliate rewards [^6]. In 2025 he launched ZeroClick, a spinout building what he calls a "reasoning-time ad network" for AI chatbots and agents; the company raised $55 million from Anthos Capital, Ludlow, Anfa, Wonder, and Protagonist — many of whom previously backed Honey [^7][^4]. As of July 2026, ZeroClick is Hudson's primary focus [^6].

## Stated Thesis

Hudson has not published a formal angel investing thesis on a personal website or fund page. His investments to date have concentrated in consumer commerce and shopping-experience startups that share the "shopping companion" DNA of Honey. Publicly, he has framed his post-Honey work around a mission to "reboot the economic engine that powers the internet" — building consumer-facing tools that redistribute affiliate and advertising revenue back to end users [^8]. That worldview is consistent with the two verified angel investments below.

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size disclaimer:** Only 2 angel investments by Ryan Hudson are independently verifiable in public databases (Tracxn, Crunchbase, and CB Insights) [^9][^10][^11]. Contemporaneous press coverage of Pogo credits "the founders of Honey" (plural) rather than Hudson by name [^12]. The sample is far too small to compute meaningful percentages; the notes below are qualitative pattern observations only.

- **Sector pattern:** Both verified checks are into consumer-commerce enablement — Batch, a QR-code-driven one-tap checkout tool [^12], and Pogo, a mobile app that helps users earn cash back on real-world purchases and was pitched as "Honey for the real world" [^13]. Both are legible extensions of Honey's core insight that consumer shopping surfaces can be intermediated by a lightweight, discovery-oriented layer.
- **Stage:** Both investments were seed-stage (Batch's $5M seed co-led by Coatue and Seven Seven Six in June 2021 [^12]; Pogo's $12.3M seed led by Buckley Ventures announced July 26, 2022 [^13]).
- **Geography:** Both companies are US-based. Batch was based in San Francisco; Pogo was co-founded by Dom Wong.
- **Co-investor pattern:** Alongside high-profile firms (Coatue, Seven Seven Six, Slow Ventures, Village Global, 20VC) and operator angels (The Chainsmokers, MrBeast's Night Ventures, Sophia Amoruso, Lenny Rachitsky, founders of Front and Rent the Runway) [^12][^13]. Hudson appears in the "operator angel" tier of these rounds rather than as a lead.
- **Notable absence:** No verifiable investments in enterprise software, deep tech, or non-consumer categories. No verifiable checks after 2022 — the sample suggests angel activity slowed as Hudson refocused on operating (Pie in 2024, ZeroClick in 2025).

Founder profile patterns cannot be reliably inferred from n=2.

## In Their Own Words

On the origin of Honey (2020 interview):

> "I realized how tedious the process was to search for a coupon code." [^2]

> "We launched in October, 2012, just before the holidays. We got great feedback and immediately knew this was something special." [^2]

> "It took about 3 years for the company to be profitable and amass a substantial following." [^2]

On the PayPal acquisition (2020 interview):

> "This is not an exit. We're still going to be leading Honey and its growth." [^2]

On why he chose to build ZeroClick as an independent company rather than sell into an incumbent (2025 interview):

> "I strongly believe the native ad format for AI is going to be — and we're going to try to make it be — paid consideration at the reasoning time." [^6]

> "I'd prefer for it to exist outside the walls of any core incumbent." [^6]

On his motivation for building ZeroClick (2025 interview):

> "That's why I want to win, so that we can help everybody else build stuff that can't exist otherwise." [^4]
