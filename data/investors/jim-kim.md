## Background

Jim Kim is Founder and General Partner at Builders VC, a San Francisco-based venture firm he co-founded in 2017 focused on "modernizing antiquated industries" — agriculture, industrial technology, construction, healthcare operations, and life sciences [^1][^2]. Builders VC is headquartered at 601 California Street, Suite 700, San Francisco, with satellite offices in Chicago and Calgary [^1][^3].

Before Builders VC, Kim founded Formation 8 (later restructured into 8VC and other successor vehicles) and remains a Managing Partner of that legacy vehicle [^2][^4]. Earlier in his career he was a General Partner at Khosla Ventures and a Senior Partner at CMEA Capital, and he began his venture career founding GE's venture capital effort, where he led investments in China High Speed Transmission and comScore [^2][^4].

Kim holds undergraduate degrees in Computer Science & Electrical Engineering and Political Science from MIT, where he also co-founded a venture-backed internet infrastructure startup with fellow Course 6 students; he also holds a Master's in Quantitative Data Analysis from Columbia University and an MBA from Columbia Business School [^2][^4].

Builders VC closed a $175M Fund I by June 2018, a $250M Fund II, and announced Fund III targeting approximately $500M in January 2024 [^4][^5][^3].

## Stated Thesis

Kim describes Builders VC's thesis as backing entrepreneurs applying modern technology to industries with historically low IT spend:

> "The whole thesis behind Builders is to go after antiquated, unsexy industries." [^4]

> "My career...has always been going after unsexy spaces, looking at real, meaningful problems in these spaces." [^4]

He has publicly framed the vertical selection quantitatively:

> "We analyzed how much IT spend happens in every vertical. On one end, there were sectors like fintech where 6 to 7 percent of revenue was spent on IT. On the opposite end, there were sectors like agriculture, livestock, real estate, construction and industrial tech. Those were the spaces we found interesting." [^4]

On founder profile:

> "We're not looking to back the Stanford kid that knows nothing about the industry firsthand. We're looking to back the 20-year industry veteran who's teamed up with an engineer. They know things because they've lived them." [^4]

On stage timing:

> "There are seed funders everywhere. When a company has some early customer traction, it's the perfect time for us to come in." [^4]

## Inferred Thesis

Based on the verified portfolio below and Builders VC's published firm portfolio, Kim's investment behavior aligns closely with the firm's stated "antiquated industries" thesis — a rare degree of stated-versus-inferred convergence.

**Sector patterns.** Kim's directly attributed deals cluster in industrials/manufacturing (Breaking, Bolt Threads, Gradiant), construction/PropTech (Fieldwire, Bowery Valuation), synthetic biology / life sciences (Colossal, Notable Labs), transportation/logistics (Motive/Keeptruckin), agriculture, and consumer hardware (Bowers & Wilkins via EVA Automation). The portfolio is notably light in enterprise SaaS and pure-software consumer categories.

**Stage.** Predominantly Series A leads with $3M-$10M average checks, consistent with the firm's stated positioning [^4]. Kim has also participated in later-stage rounds of legacy Formation 8 portfolio companies (Bolt Threads, Fieldwire Series C).

**Founder pattern.** A recurring pattern: pairing a "20-year industry veteran" domain expert with a technical co-founder. Colossal (George Church + Ben Lamm), Breaking (Wyss/Harvard research team + biotech operators), and Motive (industry-experienced logistics team) all fit this template.

**Board seat concentration.** Kim's active board seats span the same industrial focus areas: Bowers & Wilkins (via EVA Automation, consumer hardware), Bolt Threads (materials science), Fieldwire (construction, since acquired by Hilti), Bowery Valuation (real-estate PropTech), and Notable Labs (oncology data platform) [^6].

**Portfolio partitioning.** Signal.nfx aggregates 13 deals across Kim's Formation 8 and Builders VC tenures. Many of the pre-2017 rounds (Fieldwire Series A 2015, Bolt Series B 2015, Notable Labs Series A 2017) are Formation 8-era; the newer rounds (Breaking 2024, plus subsequent Builders VC-led deals) reflect the current firm's activity [^7].

**Notable structural bet.** Builders VC employs a full-time psychiatrist for founder conflict resolution — a stated first in VC — and retains industry-specific venture partners (e.g., Jim Blome, former CEO of Bayer Crop Sciences, on the agriculture side), a structural reflection of the "expertise-in-the-dirt" thesis [^8].

## In Their Own Words

**On sector selection:**
> "We analyzed how much IT spend happens in every vertical. On one end, there were sectors like fintech where 6 to 7 percent of revenue was spent on IT. On the opposite end, there were sectors like agriculture, livestock, real estate, construction and industrial tech. Those were the spaces we found interesting." [^4]

**On agriculture specifically:**
> "less than one tenth of one percent of revenue is spent on IT...it's kind of shocking...given it's a trillion dollar global industry." [^4]

**On founder selection:**
> "We're not looking to back the Stanford kid that knows nothing about the industry firsthand. We're looking to back the 20-year industry veteran who's teamed up with an engineer. They know things because they've lived them." [^4]

**On the entrepreneur–investor relationship:**
> "we try to find entrepreneurs who are technology savvy but they also have to have industry expertise." [^4]

**On timing:**
> "There are seed funders everywhere. When a company has some early customer traction, it's the perfect time for us to come in." [^4]

**On differentiation:**
> "I realized that every firm needs to have some differentiation. You've got to have a special sauce, a reason why entrepreneurs want to work with you." [^8]

**On personal motivation for entering VC:**
> "I realized that people on the other side of the table were having a lot of fun. So I said, 'I want that job.'" [^4]
