## Background

Deepinder Goyal is co-founder and CEO of Eternal Limited (formerly Zomato), which he founded on July 10, 2008 alongside Pankaj Chaddah while both worked at Bain & Company [^1]. The pair launched an initial restaurant-listing website called FoodieBay before formally incorporating as "DC Foodiebay Online Services Private Limited" on January 18, 2010, and the founders left Bain in November 2009 to work on the company full-time [^1]. Under Goyal's leadership, Zomato expanded to more than 20 countries by 2015, later refocusing on India and the UAE between 2021 and 2024 [^1]. The company went public on the Indian stock exchanges in 2021 and rebranded to Eternal in February 2025 [^2][^1]. Goyal was awarded EY Entrepreneur of the Year in 2025 [^1].

Beyond his operating role, Goyal has become one of India's most prolific technology angel investors, with a portfolio of approximately 32 companies according to Tracxn [^3][^4]. His investing activity has intensified in 2025-2026, including sizeable commitments to frontier-technology projects that he himself is founding or co-founding — LAT Aerospace (hydrogen airships), Temple (brain-monitoring wearables), and Continue Research (longevity) [^2][^5][^6].

## Stated Thesis

No self-reported public thesis document exists. Public statements from Goyal are focused on operating Eternal rather than framing an angel-investing thesis. He has publicly described Temple, his wearables company, as "the ultimate wearable for elite performance athletes," with technology that "measures metrics existing wearables cannot," including continuous cerebral blood flow tracking via a temple-mounted sensor [^2].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 19 verified portfolio companies plus his three founder-led frontier bets in 2025-2026, several patterns emerge:

- **High-frequency consumer + operational complexity**: Portfolio skews toward consumer-facing businesses with operational depth — Shiprocket (logistics), BlueStone (jewellery), Bira 91 (beverages), Ultrahuman (wearables), Park+ (parking), Pristyn Care (healthcare services), Airblack (skill development), Multiplier (employment) [^4][^7]. This mirrors Zomato's own DNA of high-frequency consumer + physical/operational supply chain.
- **India-first, with select US bets**: The vast majority of confirmed investments are Indian startups, with Tracxn characterizing his geographic focus as "primarily India and the United States" [^3][^4].
- **Consumer/retail concentration**: Tracxn classifies his portfolio as concentrated in "Consumer and Retail sectors" [^3].
- **Recent shift to frontier tech (2025-2026)**: Three of his largest personal commitments in the past 18 months are to companies he founded or co-founded — approximately USD 20M into LAT Aerospace (hydrogen airships, with another ~USD 50M planned) [^8], USD 25M into Continue Research (longevity) [^6], and Temple (USD 54M friends-and-family round at USD 190M post-money valuation) [^2]. This represents a decisive move beyond passive angel investing into founder/operator-investor territory in aerospace, longevity, and precision health.
- **Repeat co-investor pattern with Indian founder-angels**: Named co-investors include Kunal Shah (CRED), Nithin and Nikhil Kamath (Zerodha), Vijay Shekhar Sharma (Paytm), Amit Agarwal, and Rohit Bansal — a tight network of India's founder-angel class [^2][^7].
- **Sample size caveat**: 32 total investments per Tracxn, of which ~19 have publicly-documented round detail via StartupTalky. Percentages are not computed given inconsistent round-level disclosure.
