## Background

Christopher ("Chris") Ré is a full professor in the Department of Computer Science at Stanford University, affiliated with the Stanford AI Lab (SAIL), the Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM), and the Machine Learning Group. He leads the Hazy Research group, whose work focuses on the foundations of AI systems and, in his own framing, on "data-centric AI" [^1][^2]. Ré earned his B.S. from Cornell and his Ph.D. from the University of Washington under Dan Suciu, and joined Stanford's faculty in 2013 [^2].

Ré was named a MacArthur Fellow in 2015 (age 36) for "democratizing big data analytics through open source data-processing products that have the power of machine learning algorithms but can be integrated into existing and applied database systems" [^3][^4]. His other honors include the VLDB Early Career Award (2015), the NSF CAREER Award (2011), and the ACM SIGMOD Jim Gray Dissertation Award (2010) [^2].

Ré has co-founded a series of Stanford research spin-outs commercialising his lab's work: Lattice.io / DeepDive, acquired by Apple in May 2017 [^5]; Inductiv (HoloClean), also acquired by Apple in 2020 [^2]; SambaNova Systems, co-founded November 2017 with Kunle Olukotun and Rodrigo Liang [^6][^7]; Snorkel AI, co-founded in 2019 with Alex Ratner, Braden Hancock, Henry Ehrenberg, and Paroma Varma [^8][^9]; and Together AI, co-founded in 2022 with Vipul Ved Prakash, Ce Zhang, Percy Liang, and Tri Dao [^10]. Alongside these operating companies, in 2021 he co-founded Factory (also known as FactoryHQ), a venture firm focused on data-centric AI, with Lip-Bu Tan [^11][^12].

## Stated Thesis

Factory publicly describes itself as a firm built around "the fundamental technology shift that is enabled by Data-Centric AI" and pitches itself as "research led" [^11].

In his own writing about Snorkel, Ré has framed the shift he is investing behind as follows: "data is the primary encoding of domain knowledge, and data is growing increasingly prominent inside AI" [^9]. He argues that "model differences have traditionally been overrated and that data differences are usually underrated when it comes to building better AI" [^9]. Factory's portfolio construction — foundation model infrastructure (Together AI), AI compilers and inference runtimes (Modular), specialised silicon (SambaNova), evaluation and observability (Galileo AI, acquired by Cisco in April 2026) — is consistent with that stated thesis [^12].

Ré publishes a transparency page on his Stanford website listing "companies I advise or invest in," reflecting an explicit commitment to disclosing potential conflicts of interest between his academic and commercial roles [^2].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 5 verified co-foundings and Factory's disclosed portfolio activity via Tracxn / CB Insights (Factory has invested in ~18 companies since founding in 2021, with 2 unicorns and 2 acquisitions to date) [^12], the pattern is unusually concentrated and readable:

- **Data-centric and foundation-model infrastructure, not applications.** Every publicly-disclosed Factory bet and every operating company Ré has co-founded sits in the AI infrastructure or platform layer — training data (Snorkel), foundation-model serving (Together AI), compilers/runtimes (Modular), silicon (SambaNova), evaluation (Galileo AI). None of the verified investments are consumer AI apps or vertical SaaS.
- **Stanford academic origin.** All of the operating companies he has co-founded were spun out of Stanford research groups he was directly involved in — SAIL, CRFM, Hazy Research, or the Pervasive Parallelism Lab. His hiring/backing pattern strongly favours technical co-founders drawn from Stanford PhD cohorts (Ratner, Zhang, Dao, Chami, Goel, Gu, etc.) [^2][^8][^10].
- **Long-time-horizon deep tech.** Factory positions itself as "The Art of Deep Tech Investment" [^11]; the portfolio is heavy on companies whose value depends on multi-year systems R&D (silicon, compilers, foundation models) rather than short-cycle SaaS.
- **Repeat co-investor and co-founder patterns.** Ré's academic co-authors and PhD students recur as co-founders across Snorkel, Together AI, Numbers Station, and Cartesia (which was co-founded by former Hazy Research members Karan Goel, Arjun Desai, Albert Gu, and Brandon Yang) [^2].
- **Sample-size caveat.** Factory does not publish its full portfolio; the analysis here is drawn from third-party aggregators and named companies in press coverage rather than a first-party disclosed list. Percentages would be misleading given the sample.

**Notable gaps between behaviour and marketing:** Factory brands itself as a broad deep-tech firm, but every publicly-verified investment to date is in AI infrastructure specifically. Founders outside the AI/ML systems niche should not read Factory's "deep tech" framing as an invitation.

## In Their Own Words

On data-centric AI and where the field goes wrong:

> "data is the primary encoding of domain knowledge, and data is growing increasingly prominent inside AI"
> — Chris Ré, Snorkel AI blog, "Snorkel's journey to data-centric AI" [^9]

> "model differences have traditionally been overrated and that data differences are usually underrated when it comes to building better AI"
> — Chris Ré, Snorkel AI blog [^9]

On the failure mode he saw in AI teams before Snorkel:

> "we jokingly referred to as 'new-model-itis.' People focused a ton of energy on constantly writing new models"
> — Chris Ré, Snorkel AI blog [^9]

> "those teams who examined the encoding of the problem more deeply — who scrutinized, measured, and audited data as their primary focus — were building better, more efficient AI applications"
> — Chris Ré, Snorkel AI blog [^9]

On what Snorkel is for:

> "if data is the record of your problem, Snorkel seeks to produce tools to better reflect what you care about inside that data"
> — Chris Ré, Snorkel AI blog [^9]

On the role of the Hazy Research lab:

> "While we're very proud of our research ideas and their impact, the lab's real goal is to help students become professors, entrepreneurs, and researchers"
> — Chris Ré, personal Stanford page [^2]
