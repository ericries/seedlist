## Background

Mike Schroepfer is a venture capitalist and the founder of Gigascale Capital, an early-stage climate-tech VC firm that announced a $250 million Fund I in June 2026 [^1][^2]. Before founding Gigascale, Schroepfer served as Chief Technology Officer of Meta (formerly Facebook), where he led the company's engineering and infrastructure organization [^1].

Schroepfer launched Gigascale in 2023 as a personal investment vehicle focused on climate technology, building a portfolio of more than 25 companies before raising the firm's first institutional fund in 2026 [^3].

## Stated Thesis

Schroepfer's publicly stated investing thesis centers on what he calls "rebuilding the physical economy" — backing companies that win on cost and performance, with climate benefits as a downstream consequence of mass adoption [^1][^2]. He has explicitly distanced his approach from values-driven climate investing:

> "Climate impact won't come from asking the world to adopt worse systems for virtuous reasons. It comes when a different technical approach makes the cleaner system also simpler, more scalable, and cost competitive." [^2]

He frames the current moment as one of severe supply constraints — particularly in power, critical materials, and grid infrastructure — driven by AI compute demand and broader electrification [^1][^2].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on the publicly disclosed Gigascale portfolio (small sample of 25+ portfolio companies):

- **Stage distribution:** Early-stage (seed and Series A) deeptech, per firm self-description [^4].
- **Sector concentration:** Heavy weighting toward energy generation (fusion, nuclear, baseload), grid/power electronics, critical-mineral supply chains, and industrial automation. Disclosed names include Commonwealth Fusion Systems, Xcimer, Radiant, Arbor Energy, Heron Power, Form Energy, Dioxycle, Mill, and Solcoa [^1][^4].
- **Founder profile:** Strong preference for technical operators from at-scale companies (Tesla alumni, Thiel Fellows, national-lab researchers). Specific examples: Drew Baglino (ex-Tesla SVP, now Heron Power) and Hooman Nezhad (Thiel Fellow, Solcoa) [^1].
- **Geographic focus:** Predominantly US-based deeptech.

Sample size is small relative to the full portfolio; percentages are not computed until more portfolio disclosure is available.

## In Their Own Words

> "Climate impact won't come from asking the world to adopt worse systems for virtuous reasons. It comes when a different technical approach makes the cleaner system also simpler, more scalable, and cost competitive." [^2]

> "The world needs more power, more resilient supply chains, more critical materials, and better infrastructure. In many cases, the question for the best companies is no longer whether demand exists. It's how fast they can supply it." [^2]

> "Solar went from 40 gigawatts a year to 600 in a decade because it got cheaper, not because it got more virtuous." — on the firm's adoption-first thesis [^1]

Schroepfer has also said that bring-your-own power "is going to be a competitive advantage over time" for energy-intensive industries [^1].
