## Background

Jeremy Allaire is Co-Founder, Chairman, and CEO of Circle Internet Group, the USDC-stablecoin issuer that went public on the New York Stock Exchange (ticker CRCL) in June 2025 [^1][^2]. He has spent more than two decades building and leading internet software platforms. In 1995 he co-founded Allaire Corporation with his brother J.J. Allaire, on $18,000 of startup capital; the company created the ColdFusion web application platform, IPO'd on NASDAQ in January 1999, and was acquired by Macromedia in March 2001 for $360 million [^3]. He served as Macromedia's Chief Technology Officer from 2001 to 2003, where he led development of the Macromedia MX suite and drove video capabilities into Flash Player [^3]. From 2003 to 2004 he was a Technologist and Entrepreneur-in-Residence at General Catalyst Partners, focused on broadband media, mobile content, and internet security [^1][^3]. In 2004 he founded Brightcove, an online video platform that IPO'd in 2012; he stepped down as CEO in 2013 to launch Circle [^3]. Circle raised a $9M Series A in October 2013 from Jim Breyer, Accel, and General Catalyst, followed by a $17M Series B in March 2014 and additional rounds through 2016, and closed a $222 million presale for its Layer 1 blockchain "Arc" at a $3 billion fully diluted valuation on May 11, 2026, backed by BlackRock, Apollo, and a16z [^3][^4]. Allaire was named to the TIME100 list of the world's most influential people in 2026 [^1][^3].

## Stated Thesis

Circle's official leadership page describes Allaire as responsible for "strategy, vision, and operating execution" at the company; his public thesis is largely expressed through Circle's mission to bring digital dollars to mainstream internet infrastructure, and he has stated Circle's goal is to "make this as easy to use as Gmail, Skype, and other consumer services" [^1][^3]. He has not published a stand-alone angel-investing thesis.

## Inferred Thesis

Most of Allaire's capital allocation happens through Circle itself and its balance sheet, not personal angel checks — treat the "portfolio" here as illustrative rather than representative.
