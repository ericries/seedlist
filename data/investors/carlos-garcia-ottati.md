## Background

Carlos García Ottati is the co-founder and CEO of Kavak, the Mexico City-based used-car marketplace that became Mexico's first technology unicorn in October 2020 and reached an $8.7 billion valuation in September 2021 [^1][^2][^3]. Born September 6, 1983 in Caracas, Venezuela, he holds an economics degree from Universidad Católica Andrés Bello and earned an MBA at the University of Oxford's Saïd Business School in 2010 [^1]. He interned at Amazon during his MBA, then spent two years as a consultant at McKinsey & Company before joining Rocket Internet–backed Latin American marketplace Linio, where he served as Chief Marketplace Officer and helped build the company's logistics and digital payments operations [^1][^4].

He founded Kavak in October 2016 with his sister Loreanne García Ottati and Roger Laughlin after a personal experience being defrauded while trying to sell a car in Mexico, where roughly 90% of used-car transactions were informal at the time [^2][^4][^5]. The company raised an initial $3M seed round from Mountain Nazca, led by Héctor Sepúlveda [^5][^6]. Subsequent rounds were led by Kaszek Ventures, SoftBank Latin America Fund, and General Catalyst, ultimately raising more than $1.5B and expanding the platform from Mexico into Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Turkey, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Oman [^2][^3][^6].

Alongside his operating role, García Ottati has made a small number of angel investments in other Latin American startups, primarily in fintech, insurtech, and marketplace companies, and is a backer of LatAm-focused early-stage fund Latitud Ventures [^7][^8][^9][^10]. He has been named to Bloomberg Línea's "500 People Who Move Latin America" list [^1].

## In Their Own Words

On founding Kavak after being defrauded while selling a car in Mexico:

> "I realized the risks that exist when there is no transparency or guarantee for these transactions." — Carlos García Ottati, Forbes, July 23, 2022 [^2]

On the company's product ambition:

> "I like to describe it as if Spotify, Amazon, Toyota and Citibank had a kid, then something like Kavak would be born." — Carlos García Ottati, TechCrunch, September 21, 2021 [^3]

On the motivation for Kavak's $700M Series E:

> "What excites me about this raise is a reflection of Kavak's ambition we have and what we want to build, and less so about need... Most importantly, it's going to allow us to think long term and build a true generational company." — Carlos García Ottati, TechCrunch, September 21, 2021 [^3]

On the broader thesis for emerging-market financial inclusion:

> "If we do our job well, in the next 20 years, the middle class should grow up by 30% in emerging markets just by granting financing products to a huge amount of people that are excluded today, giving them a tool that can definitely enhance their lives." — Carlos García Ottati, TechCrunch, September 21, 2021 [^3]

On Kavak as a platform business:

> "We want to be a super app, or an ecosystem of services, that empowers mobility and financial health and do more than just help people buy and sell cars." — Carlos García Ottati, TechCrunch, September 21, 2021 [^3]

On international capital entering LatAm:

> "It's wonderful bringing in new funds who had not previously invested in the region to see how LatAm looks and works." — Carlos García Ottati, TechCrunch, September 21, 2021 [^3]
