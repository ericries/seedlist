## Background

Bo Li is Co-Founder and CEO of Virtue AI, an enterprise AI security and compliance platform founded in 2024 [^1][^2]. She holds concurrent academic appointments as the Abbasi Associate Professor of Computer Science at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign [^3] and as a Research Associate Professor in the Computer Science Department and Data Science Institute at the University of Chicago [^4].

Li earned her PhD in Computer Science from Vanderbilt University and joined the UIUC faculty in 2018 [^5]. Her research focuses on trustworthy machine learning — robustness, privacy, generalization, and their intersection — with applications to safe autonomous vehicles and federated learning [^4]. She co-authored "DecodingTrust: A Comprehensive Assessment of Trustworthiness in LLMs," which won the Best Paper Award at NeurIPS and the NSA's Best Cybersecurity Paper Award in 2024 [^1].

Recognitions include the Sloan Research Fellowship, MIT Technology Review's Innovators Under 35 (TR-35), the IJCAI Computers and Thought Award, NSF CAREER, and the Intel Rising Star Faculty award [^4]. She advises the U.S. AI Safety Institute, the U.K. AI Safety Institute, and AI security initiatives in Japan and Singapore [^2].

Virtue AI was co-founded with Dawn Song (UC Berkeley), Sanmi Koyejo (Stanford), and Carlos Guestrin (Stanford) [^1][^2].

## Stated Thesis

Li is not a general-purpose venture investor. Her stated public focus is building enterprise infrastructure for AI safety, robustness, and compliance. She describes Virtue AI's mission as "Building the Trust Layer for AI and Agentic Systems" [^1], and has articulated the founding motivation as: "The same issues kept surfacing—clunky red teaming, inefficient safety guardrails, and a reliance on manual oversight that simply doesn't scale" [^6].

## In Their Own Words

On AI safety measurement: "In practice, AI safety cannot be measured through a single static benchmark because modern AI systems continuously evolve through fine-tuning, retrieval augmentation, and tool or agent interactions" [^7].

On why traditional security tools fall short for AI agents: "Traditional security tools were built for predictable applications with fixed paths. They were never designed for systems that reason, adapt, and act autonomously" [^7].

On the enterprise risk surface of agentic AI: "Agents are being handed access to the most sensitive parts of enterprise infrastructure: executing code, calling APIs, browsing the web, and making chained decisions that touch data, finances, and operations" [^7].

On responsible deployment: "Faster and safer aren't opposites, even though most enterprises treat them that way... Responsible deployment means you know what your agents can do, you can see what they're doing, and you can stop them when something goes wrong" [^7].

On the origin of Virtue AI: "The same issues kept surfacing—clunky red teaming, inefficient safety guardrails, and a reliance on manual oversight that simply doesn't scale" [^6].
