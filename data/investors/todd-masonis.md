## Background

Todd Masonis is an angel investor and the co-founder and CEO of Dandelion Chocolate, a bean-to-bar chocolate maker based in San Francisco's Mission District [^1][^2]. He is best known in tech circles as one of the co-founders of Plaxo, the address-book synchronization service he co-founded with Stanford classmate Cameron Ring and Sean Parker in 2001-2002 [^3][^4]. Masonis and Ring were the "two Stanford University engineering students" who built and ran the company after Parker's 2004 departure [^3][^5]. Plaxo launched in public beta on November 12, 2002 [^3], grew to roughly 20 million users, and was acquired by Comcast in a deal announced May 14, 2008 and closed July 1, 2008 for a reported $150-170 million [^5][^6].

After selling Plaxo, Masonis took a sabbatical and, with Ring, began experimenting with chocolate-making — first in a friend's garage, then at farmer's markets — before opening Dandelion Chocolate as a small-batch bean-to-bar factory in 2010 [^2][^7]. His wife and Stanford classmate Elaine Wherry (also a Plaxo co-founder) is a co-founder of Dandelion Chocolate and is closely associated with the venture-capital firm Long Journey Ventures (formerly Edelweiss), where she serves as an Advisor [^8][^9].

As an angel investor, Masonis has been listed as a syndicate member of the AngelList "Edelweiss" syndicate that is affiliated with Long Journey Ventures [^10]. Long Journey Ventures itself was originally founded in 2015 as Edelweiss by Lee Jacobs with operator partners Brian Balfour, Elaine Wherry, and Jonathan Bruck; the firm rebranded as Long Journey Ventures in 2020 and Masonis is not listed on its current team page [^9][^11]. His angel investing is primarily reported on his own name, and public records show a modest, individually-sourced portfolio (see Portfolio below).

## Stated Thesis

Masonis has not published a formal investing thesis. His publicly available statements focus on operating philosophy rather than investment criteria. In interviews about Dandelion Chocolate, he has emphasized product quality over speed and a bias toward starting with the smallest possible version of a product:

> "The problem is you don't have a product. For us, it was: Could we make a bar of chocolate? We made a bar of chocolate. Could we make two bars?" [^7]

> "Do not compromise quality for speed efficiency" [^12]

Because there is no published investment thesis, the sections below rely on inferred behavior and general operating principles Masonis has articulated.

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size is small.** Independent public sources report only a handful of confirmed personal angel investments by Masonis — PitchBook and Crunchbase-derived aggregators variously report 2-5 investments, with his most recent verified check being a 2022 seed round in Valence [^13][^14]. Because the sample is so small, percentages would be misleading. The following qualitative patterns are visible in the confirmed data:

- **Operator-angel style, not full-time investor.** Masonis's primary role has been operating (Plaxo, then Dandelion Chocolate); his angel checks are made as an ex-founder writing personal checks, often alongside other operator-angels from the Plaxo network. MediaSpike's 2013 Series A, for example, included "seven people from Plaxo" as angel investors, of which Masonis was one [^15].
- **Consumer, food, and marketplace bias.** Confirmed personal investments cluster around consumer-facing companies — Foodzie (artisan food marketplace, exited to Joyus in 2012), BrightCrowd (consumer collaboration), and Valence (professional network for Black professionals) [^13][^14]. This is consistent with his own operating focus on consumer product.
- **Bay Area geographic focus.** All confirmed personal investments are US-based, primarily in the San Francisco Bay Area, which matches his network and physical location [^13].
- **Syndicate participation via Edelweiss/Long Journey.** Beyond personal checks, Masonis is a listed team member of the AngelList "Edelweiss" syndicate affiliated with Long Journey Ventures, which sources deals to operator-LPs [^10]. Participation in that syndicate is a distinct activity from Long Journey's core fund — Masonis is not listed as a partner, venture partner, or advisor on Long Journey's current team page [^11].

**Note on aggregator data.** Multiple third-party aggregators (Wellfound, Bloomberg, Tracxn) describe Masonis as an "Edelweiss" investor and attribute investments in Fitbit, Honor, HipChat, Blue Bottle Coffee, HoneyBook, Loom, and others to "Edelweiss." These may or may not correspond to personal Masonis checks — they most likely reflect Edelweiss/Long Journey fund investments and syndicate deals rather than his personal cap-table participation. Because we could not verify Masonis's personal participation in those specific rounds from a primary source, they are not included in the Portfolio table below.

## In Their Own Words

On starting small and shipping the smallest possible product:

> "The problem is you don't have a product. For us, it was: Could we make a bar of chocolate? We made a bar of chocolate. Could we make two bars?" [^7]

On downside risk as an entrepreneur:

> "The worst case scenario is that you go back to the life you're living right now; no use in worrying." [^12]

> "If you fail, you can just go back to working like you did before." [^12]

On product quality:

> "Afraid of having chocolate that just tastes like everyone else's chocolate." [^12]

On Plaxo's founding:

> "I had started a company called Plaxo with my friend Cam and Sean Parker in 2001, the year we graduated." [^7]
