## Background

Binny Bansal is an Indian software engineer and internet entrepreneur best known as the co-founder of Flipkart, the Bengaluru-based e-commerce company acquired by Walmart for approximately $16 billion in May 2018 [^1]. He was born on December 1, 1982, was educated at St. Anne's Convent School in Chandigarh, and graduated from IIT Delhi with a degree in computer science and engineering [^1]. After a brief stint at Sarnoff Corporation and nine months as a software engineer at Amazon (starting January 2007), he co-founded Flipkart later in 2007 with Sachin Bansal (no relation) on an initial investment of approximately $6,000 [^1].

At Flipkart he served as Chief Operating Officer until January 2016, was promoted to CEO in January 2016, and became Group CEO in January 2017 [^1]. He held roles of chairman and Group CEO after Walmart's 77% acquisition in May 2018, then resigned from Flipkart in November 2018 following personal-misconduct allegations [^1]. Bansal completely divested his Flipkart stake by January 2024, and has been reported to have earned approximately $1.5 billion in aggregate proceeds; Forbes and Wikipedia estimated his net worth around $1.4 billion in early 2024 [^1]. He stepped down from the PhonePe board in November 2024 while retaining roughly 1% ownership as the largest individual minority investor [^2]. Bansal resides in Singapore [^1].

Bansal now runs two operating vehicles and one investment firm. **xto10x Technologies** is a Bengaluru-based platform he co-founded in early 2019 with former Flipkart and Ekart executives Saikiran Krishnamurthy (CEO) and Neeraj Aggarwal (COO) to provide learning programs, SaaS, and consulting to growth-stage startups [^3][^4]. xto10x raised a $25 million Series A in February 2022, led by Bansal himself alongside 28 founders and senior leaders from India and Southeast Asia (including Razorpay's Shashank Kumar) [^5]. **OppDoor**, incorporated in Singapore in May 2021, is a technology-enabled managed-services platform Bansal founded to help emerging consumer brands expand across US, UK, Europe, Canadian, Japanese, and Australian marketplaces [^6][^2]. **3State Ventures** (also written "Three State Ventures" and "3STATE Ventures") is the Singapore-based investment firm Bansal founded to deploy capital into early- to growth-stage technology companies, with a stated focus on digital commerce and AI-native platforms [^7][^8].

Before setting up 3State Ventures, Bansal anchored **021 Capital**, a Bengaluru VC firm launched in 2017 by his personal investment adviser Sailesh Tulshan; 021 raised $32 million in January 2019 with Bansal as anchor and additional LPs including the Azim Premji Foundation [^9]. In September 2019, Bansal was also reported to be assembling a $300-400 million growth-stage VC fund for India and Southeast Asia, in which he would serve as general partner and anchor investor with a personal commitment of 10-20% of the fund [^10]; that vehicle has since taken shape as 3State Ventures rather than a syndicated third-party fund.

## Stated Thesis

Bansal has not published a written thesis but has consistently described his post-Flipkart mission as helping large numbers of Indian founders scale. Announcing xto10x in February 2019 he told TechCrunch: "Person to person, I can help 10 startups, but the ambition is to help 10,000 early and mid-stage entrepreneurs, not 10" [^3]. He added: "Today, software is built for large enterprises and not small startups," and "Instead of making a thousand mistakes, if we can help other startups make a hundred or even a few hundred, that would be worth it" [^3].

On 3State Ventures' investment focus, Bansal — in his only publicly quoted commentary on a portfolio investment — framed the firm around Indian fintech and consumer platforms. On the November 2023 Scapia Series A he stated: "The rise of fintech in India has been nothing short of revolutionary, democratizing access to financial services and empowering millions," adding that "among such innovations, Scapia stands out with its differentiated solutions tailored for travel, addressing a vital need for aspiring Indians" [^11]. On the July 2025 SuperK Series B he stated: "SuperK is well-positioned to reimagine retail in deeper Bharat by empowering entrepreneurs from these towns to easily own and operate supermarkets" [^12].

## Inferred Thesis

This analysis is based on **11 verified investments** across seven years, where Bansal's participation is confirmed by a primary or contemporaneous press source: SigTuple Series C (April 2019), Increff Series A (April 2019), Rupeek (February 2020), Zoko seed (March 2021), Scapia Series A (November 2023), Curefoods Series D (March 2024), Curefoods debt round (September 2024), Posha Series A (May 2025), ShopOS (June 2025), SuperK Series B (July 2025), and OppDoor follow-on (January 2026) [^13][^14][^15][^16][^11][^17][^18][^19][^20][^12][^6]. Tracxn lists 76 portfolio companies and 3State Ventures separately shows 9 core investments; PitchBook, CB Insights and Wikipedia collectively describe him as having backed roughly 47-76 companies across 64+ rounds [^21][^7][^1]. Percentages below are directional given the deliberately small verified sample.

**Stage distribution (11 verified investments):** Series A and later dominate — Series A/B/C/D account for 9 of 11 (SigTuple C, Increff A, Rupeek, Scapia A, Curefoods D, Posha A, ShopOS, SuperK B, OppDoor follow-on). Only Zoko is unambiguously seed-stage. This is a **growth-stage checkbook**, not a seed portfolio in the classic sense — a marked contrast to most founder-turned-angels in India. Wikipedia and aggregator profiles noting 28 seed-stage transactions overall reflect small pre-2020 tickets that do not appear in press-verifiable form; the deals large enough to make press are almost all Series A or later [^1][^21].

**Sector distribution (11 verified investments):** Consumer commerce and commerce-enablement platforms dominate — ShopOS (AI commerce OS), OppDoor (cross-border e-commerce enablement), SuperK (small-town retail), Curefoods (cloud kitchens) and Scapia (travel-linked credit card) together make 5 of 11 (~45%). Fintech accounts for 2 of 11 (Scapia, Rupeek — the latter a gold-loan lender). Healthtech/AI (SigTuple), supply-chain SaaS (Increff), consumer hardware/robotics (Posha), edtech/study-abroad (LeverageEdu, cited separately below), and B2B messaging (Zoko) round out the mix. The consistent through-line is **commerce infrastructure and consumer platforms serving India (or Indian founders building for the developed world)** — a natural extension of Bansal's Flipkart operating background.

**Geographic focus:** Predominantly India-founded and India-operating, with a growing thread of India-founded companies serving developed markets. Curefoods, SuperK, Rupeek, LeverageEdu, ShopOS, Increff, SigTuple and Scapia are all India-headquartered [^17][^12][^15][^22][^20][^13][^14][^11]. OppDoor is Singapore-incorporated but Indian-founder-led and targets US/UK/EU/Canada/Japan/Australia markets [^6]. Posha (formerly Nymble) is India-founded but sells its cooking robot in the US [^19]. Bansal himself is based in Singapore [^1].

**Check size:** 3State Ventures typically leads or co-leads with checks between $2 million and $25 million. Verifiable individual writes include ~$20 million (ShopOS lead, June 2025) [^20], $25 million (Curefoods Series D tranche, March 2024) [^17], ~$9.6 million (Curefoods debt, September 2024) [^18], $6.4 million (OppDoor follow-on, January 2026) [^6], $3.5 million (LeverageEdu, July 2023) [^22], and roughly $2 million cumulative (initial OppDoor investment across 2023-2024) [^23]. Earlier angel tickets through 021 Capital were smaller — the Increff Series A in April 2019 totaled just $3 million with 021 leading [^14].

**Co-investor patterns:** For 3State Ventures deals in particular, Bansal has repeatedly appeared alongside **Blume Ventures** (Scapia, SuperK), **Elevation Capital** (co-lead on Scapia Series A) [^11], and **existing growth investors following on** (Matrix Partners India and Tanglin Venture Partners on Scapia; Blume and Xeed Ventures on SuperK) [^11][^12]. On individual angel deals he has co-invested with **Accel** (Posha, SigTuple), **Chiratae Ventures** (SigTuple), **Pi Ventures** (SigTuple), **Y Combinator** (Zoko), and jeweler Mithun Sacheti (CaratLane) as SuperK Series B co-lead [^13][^19][^16][^12]. **Notable single-name co-invest**: Indian cricketer Shubman Gill joined the SuperK Series B alongside 3State [^12].

**Founder profile:** Bansal has a distinctive preference for **Flipkart/Ekart/Myntra alumni founders**. Scapia (Anil Goteti, former Flipkart SVP) [^11], Increff (Rajul Jain, Nirmal Jain, Anshuman Agarwal, Romil Jain — former Myntra executives) [^14], and OppDoor (his own vehicle, recruiting Flipkart-group executives) are three of eleven verified deals with direct Flipkart-lineage founders [^23]. This is a rare pattern-match signal for founders in the extended Flipkart mafia.

**Notable gaps versus stated positioning:** Aggregator sites describe Bansal as an "angel investor" with ~76 portfolio companies, but only a small fraction have primary-source citations; most verified activity is concentrated at Series A or later, and much is executed through 3State Ventures leading or co-leading rather than participating passively in party rounds [^7]. Bansal is publicly light on written commentary — most portfolio deals are announced without a Bansal quote, which is unusual for a founder-turned-VC and consistent with his stated preference for operating support over public positioning.
