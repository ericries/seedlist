## Background

Avishai Abrahami was born May 3, 1971 in Jerusalem, Israel [^1]. He served in the Israel Defense Forces' Unit 8200 (signals intelligence and cybersecurity), completing his service in 1992 [^1]. He briefly attended college afterward but "dropped out after three months" [^1].

Before Wix, Abrahami co-founded Sphera Corporation (later acquired by SWsoft) [^1]. In 2006, he co-founded Wix.com in Tel Aviv alongside his brother Nadav Abrahami and Giora Kaplan, and has served as CEO since inception [^1]. Wix listed on NASDAQ in November 2013 (WIX); the company reached a market capitalization of approximately $12 billion in summer 2020 during the COVID-driven surge in online business creation [^1].

Alongside operating Wix, Abrahami has been an active angel investor for more than a decade, focused primarily on Israeli founders and SaaS/product-led businesses. As of 2022, press reported he held stakes in 14 companies that had achieved successful exits [^1]. He sits on the boards of Wix.com, monday.com, and OpenWeb [^1].

## Stated Thesis

Abrahami has not published a formal investment thesis. His public commentary suggests he backs founders who share his product-first, SMB-oriented worldview. Speaking on the 20VC podcast in 2026, he argued that trust — not features — is the durable moat in SaaS: "What is the biggest value Salesforce provides? It's not the CRM. It's the fact that you trust a SaaS provider with your data" [^2].

On the durability of complex vertical SaaS in the AI era, he told the same interviewer: "You're not going to vibe code Shopify, no matter how good you are. The business stack is too hard" [^2].

His advice to monday.com co-founder Roy Mann — recounted by Mann in a 2019 Insight Partners interview — captures his operating-first bias: "Good companies raise money, so build a company" [^3].

## Inferred Thesis

Sample size is small — only a handful of Abrahami's angel investments are independently confirmable through primary press sources. Percentages are not computed; the pattern below is qualitative.

**Sector pattern (from verified investments):**
- Product-led SaaS for SMBs and workforces: monday.com (2012), Connecteam (2021)
- Sales/demo tooling: Walnut (2020)
- Consumer conversation infrastructure: OpenWeb (board seat) [^4]
- Robotics / applied AI: Unlimited Robotics (2024)
- Historical: Soluto (consumer tech-support software, exited to Asurion in 2013) [^5]

The through-line is Israeli-founded product companies that empower non-technical users or workforces — closely mirroring Wix's own market. His most consequential bet, monday.com, came from a former Wix employee (Roy Mann) whose collaboration tool grew out of Wix's internal use [^6]. This "operator-adjacent" sourcing pattern is characteristic: many of his known deals involve founders he has worked with or investors from his own Wix orbit (OG Tech Ventures — the family office of Yahoo co-founder Jerry Yang's brother-in-law Eyal Ofer; Wix's leadership team as a co-investing group in Connecteam) [^7].

**Stage:** Predominantly seed and early growth. Confirmed rounds range from $2.5M seed (Walnut, 2020) to $37M Series B (Connecteam, 2021).

**Geography:** Overwhelmingly Israeli-founded companies, with occasional US-based exceptions. Abrahami himself is based in Tel Aviv.

**Check size:** Not publicly disclosed. As an individual angel, checks are likely in the range typical for high-net-worth operator-investors ($50K–$500K), but this is not verified.

**Co-investor patterns (from small sample):** Insight Partners (monday.com, Connecteam), NFX (Walnut), lool ventures (Unlimited Robotics), Micha Kauffman / Fiverr founder (Unlimited Robotics), OG Tech Ventures (Connecteam). Recurring alongside other Israeli tech founders operating as angels.

**Notable gap:** Despite Wix being a public company with a large treasury, Abrahami's personal angel activity appears highly selective and low-volume — the record does not support a "spray-and-pray" pattern.

## In Their Own Words

On the durability of complex SaaS in the AI era (20VC podcast, 2026):

> "You're not going to vibe code Shopify, no matter how good you are. The business stack is too hard." [^2]

> "There's a massive gap between what AI can do in a demo and what a pizza shop owner in São Paulo needs to run their business." [^2]

> "I think that we all give too much credit for AI and what it can do. It's not that smart. It keeps making silly mistakes. It's not very good at reasoning." [^2]

On trust as the real SaaS moat:

> "What is the biggest value Salesforce provides? It's not the CRM. It's the fact that you trust a SaaS provider with your data." [^2]

> "JPMorgan will trust Salesforce with their customer data. They won't trust anybody else." [^2]

On advice to founders he has backed, as recounted by monday.com's Roy Mann:

> "Good companies raise money, so build a company." [^3]

On Wix's May 2026 20% layoffs and the AI-era company reset:

> "It is one of the hardest decisions I have had to make." [^10]

> "We have witnessed the most significant shift in how companies are built since the invention of modern programming languages in the 1970s." [^10]

> "It is a painful change, a change that touches the lives of many, but I truly believe we have no other choice — we must evolve." [^10]

On investing in Connecteam (Series B announcement, April 2021):

> "Like Wix, Connecteam is a great product-led company that doesn't just focus on the problem it solves, but also on the people behind it." [^8]
