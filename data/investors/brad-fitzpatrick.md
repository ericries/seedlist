## Background

Brad Fitzpatrick is an American programmer born February 5, 1980, in Iowa, and raised in Beaverton, Oregon [^1]. He attended Aloha High School and majored in computer science at the University of Washington in Seattle [^1]. While in high school he founded FreeVote.com, and during his college freshman year he created LiveJournal as a personal journaling program that grew into a full company; he sold LiveJournal and its parent Danga Interactive to Six Apart in January 2005 and became chief architect there before departing in August 2007 [^1].

Fitzpatrick is the original author of several widely used free software projects, including memcached, WebSub (formerly PubSubHubbub), OpenID, and Perkeep (formerly Camlistore) [^1][^2]. He joined Google in 2007 as a Staff Software Engineer and spent 13 years on the Go programming language team, contributing foundational work to Go's networking and crypto stacks, HTTP/2 implementation, and developer tooling [^1][^3].

In January 2020, Fitzpatrick left Google to join Tailscale as a late-stage co-founder and chief engineer, focused on making WireGuard-based networking simpler [^3][^4]. Tailscale, founded in 2019, builds a mesh VPN product on top of WireGuard [^4].

Fitzpatrick is not a full-time investor. His investment activity appears limited and opportunistic, concentrated on open-source and developer-infrastructure projects he has direct affinity for. Aggregator databases show a single publicly documented angel investment (Bluesky, July 2023) [^5][^6].

## In Their Own Words

On engineering values, from his January 2020 post announcing that he was joining Tailscale:

> "I now value simplicity above almost all else and tolerate complexity only when it's well isolated, well documented, well tested." [^3]

On what drew him to the networking problem Tailscale addresses:

> "I've always found networking and authentication and web apps to be a bit tedious and overly complex." [^3]

On WireGuard as the substrate for a better developer experience:

> "What I discovered is how it also solves a lot of identity/authentications issues." [^3]

On his product vision at Tailscale:

> "You should be able to write private IP/UDP/TCP/HTTP servers where you can check who the user is by looking at the IP address only." [^3]

On the importance of open source to earning user trust:

> "We want to open source much of this, not only because it's what we enjoy, but also because it'll let you trust us." [^3]
