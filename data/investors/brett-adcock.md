## Background

Brett Adcock (born April 6, 1986, Moweaqua, Illinois) is an American serial entrepreneur whose companies span workforce marketplaces, electric aviation, humanoid robotics, security hardware, and AI [^1]. He graduated from the University of Florida with a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration in 2008 [^1].

Adcock co-founded **Vettery**, a talent marketplace, in 2013. The company was acquired by The Adecco Group in 2018 for approximately $100 million and merged into the "Hired" brand [^1][^2].

In 2018 he co-founded **Archer Aviation**, an eVTOL (electric vertical take-off and landing) air-taxi company. Archer went public via SPAC merger on the New York Stock Exchange in February 2021 [^1][^2].

In May 2022 Adcock founded **Figure AI**, a Sunnyvale-based general-purpose humanoid robotics company [^1][^3]. He remains its Chief Executive Officer. Figure raised a $70M Series A in May 2023, a $675M Series B in February 2024 at a $2.6B post-money valuation, and a Series C of approximately $1B in September 2025 at a $39B valuation [^3][^4]. Adcock is reported to own roughly 50% of Figure [^4].

In October 2023 he founded **Cover**, a school-shooting-detection company that licenses technology from NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory [^1].

In 2025 Adcock founded **Hark**, a stealth AI hardware and agentic-AI company that he seeded with $100 million of his own capital. On May 21, 2026, Hark announced a $700 million Series A led by Parkway Venture Capital at a $6 billion valuation, with participation from Nvidia, AMD Ventures, ARK Invest, Brookfield, Greycroft, Intel Capital, Prime Movers Lab, Qualcomm Ventures, Salesforce Ventures, Align Ventures, and Tamarack Global [^5][^6].

Forbes and The New York Times estimated Adcock's net worth at approximately $19 billion as of February 2026, driven principally by his Figure AI stake [^1][^2].

## Stated Thesis

Adcock does not operate as a professional investor. He describes himself on his personal site as someone who likes to "build impossible things" with "extreme practicality" to "make crazy ideas real" [^7]. He has publicly argued that harder ideas are easier to fund and staff than easy ones, that speed and audience-building are competitive advantages, and that founder-CEO governance ("Founder Mode") should be encoded into a company's structure from day one [^8][^9].

The consistent through-line across the companies he has founded and personally financed is: physical hardware businesses at the intersection of AI and consumer or infrastructure-scale demand (robots, air taxis, personal-AI devices, security hardware), executed by teams he assembles himself with an emphasis on hiring speed and mission clarity [^7][^8].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size caveat.** No verifiable third-party angel portfolio was found for Adcock during research on 2026-07-08. Sacra, Forbes, Wikipedia, Crunchbase and his own website document only his self-funded ventures [^1][^2][^4][^7]. The analysis below is therefore based on the five companies he has personally founded and capitalized (Vettery, Archer, Figure, Cover, Hark), not on external angel checks.

- **Capital deployment style:** concentrated, self-funded, at scale. His largest publicly documented single check is the $100M he personally put into Hark in late 2025 [^5][^6]. This is orders of magnitude larger than a typical angel check.
- **Sector concentration (5 founded companies):** humanoid robotics (Figure), AI hardware / agentic AI (Hark), eVTOL aerospace (Archer), physical-security AI (Cover), and one legacy marketplace (Vettery). Four of five are AI-adjacent hardware businesses; the outlier (Vettery) predates his hardware-AI era [^1].
- **Stage:** every venture he has capitalized has been a company he co-founded at inception. He has no documented seed, Series A, or later-stage participation as an outside investor.
- **Geography:** all founded companies headquartered in the United States (California, New Jersey/NYC), with Figure and Hark specifically in Silicon Valley [^1][^3].
- **Co-founder / hiring pattern:** Adcock has publicly stated he has hired "over a dozen people via Twitter" for Figure and treats audience-building on X as a primary sourcing channel for both talent and capital [^9]. His X account (@adcock_brett) is unusually active by CEO standards and openly discusses hiring, product, and fundraising decisions.
- **Governance preference:** he has stated that Figure was "built from the ground-up to be in Founder Mode: from the bylaws, board, values, comp, org design" [^8]. This is a clear signal that any future investment vehicle he might launch would preserve founder control.

**Delta between stated and inferred:** Adcock talks like a founder-operator, and his behavior matches. Founders considering him as a lead angel should expect that his time and capital will be spent almost entirely inside the companies he runs, not on outside checks.
