## Background

Vinod Dham is an Indian-American semiconductor executive and venture investor widely known as the "Father of the Pentium" for leading the development of Intel's Pentium microprocessor in the early 1990s [^1]. He earned a BE in Electrical Engineering from Delhi College of Engineering (1971) and an MS in Solid State Physics from the University of Cincinnati (1977), and worked at NCR before joining Intel in 1979, where he was a co-inventor on Intel's first Flash Memory (ETOX) and ultimately rose to Vice President of the Microprocessor Products Group [^1]. After 16 years at Intel he served as COO of NexGen (acquired by AMD for $857M in 1995), then VP of AMD's Computation Products Group (leading the K6 launch), and as CEO of Silicon Spice, which Broadcom acquired for $1.2B in 2000 [^1].

Dham transitioned full-time to venture investing in the early 2000s. In April 2002 he co-founded **NewPath Ventures** with Tushar Dave — described as the first Indo-US cross-border venture fund — which incubated and funded chip and systems-design companies that combined US-based market access with engineering teams in India [^2][^3]. In 2006 he co-founded **NEA-IndoUS Ventures** (later rebranded **Indo-US Venture Partners** / IUVP) with anchor capital from New Enterprise Associates, raising roughly $189M to back early- and mid-stage Indian technology startups [^4][^1]. In 2016 he co-founded **AlphaICs**, an agent-based AI accelerator startup, with Prashant Trivedi, Nagendra Nagaraja, and B.M. Basavaraju [^5][^6].

Dham was awarded the **Padma Bhushan**, India's third-highest civilian honor, in 2025 for his contributions to science and technology [^1]. He serves on NITI Aayog's Frontier Tech Hub Expert Council on the Future of Semiconductors, advises Bluehill.VC (a Chennai-based ₹350 crore deep-tech fund) [^7][^8], and is the namesake of the Vinod Dham Centre of Excellence for Semiconductors and Microelectronics (VDSemiX) established at Delhi Technological University in May 2023 [^9].

## Stated Thesis

Dham publicly positions his investing around semiconductors, deep tech, and India–US cross-border opportunities. Indo-US Venture Partners publicly describes its focus as "early- and mid-stage technology startups in India" across **mobile technology, knowledge process outsourcing, internet, education, and healthcare** [^1][^4]. Through Bluehill.VC, which he advises, the publicly stated focus is **"IP in engineering and science domains"** — including fab-side tooling (metrology, inspection) and fabless chip design [^7][^8].

His earlier vehicle NewPath Ventures was publicly described as targeting **"chip and system design companies"** built around an India-based engineering model selling into US markets [^2][^3].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size warning:** Dham's investing has spanned multiple vehicles (NewPath, IUVP, AlphaICs founder-investments, personal angel checks, and Bluehill advisory) over 20+ years. Publicly verifiable individual deals are sparse — most NewPath/IUVP exits are reported at the fund level rather than per-deal. The analysis below is based on **~12 verified investments and founded companies** documented in this profile; treat percentages as directional, not statistical.

**Sector concentration — semiconductors and deep tech dominate.** Of 12 verified entities (NewPath portfolio + AlphaICs + Bluehill-led deals + IUVP disclosed names): **7 are semiconductor / chip-design (58%)** — Telsima (WiMAX), Montalvo Systems (low-power x86), Silica (multimedia/printing chips), Nevis Networks (secure networking silicon), AlphaICs (agent AI co-processor), Sensesemi (edge AI chips), Sophrosyne (biosensing SoC) [^2][^3][^5][^7][^10]. The remaining IUVP-disclosed names (InSilica, ISGN, KRK Labs, mGinger, Microqual, Minekey, Obopay) skew toward mobile services, BPO, and consumer internet — a deliberate diversification away from his core semis expertise [^4].

**Stage: predominantly seed and Series A**, with concentrated capital ($100M across 4 NewPath portfolio companies = ~$25M/company average, much larger than typical seed checks) [^2]. Bluehill-advised deals are seed-stage ($2M Sophrosyne seed in Nov 2025) [^10].

**Geography: cross-border by design.** Every verified vehicle has been India–US bridge: engineering in India, capital and customers in the US. NewPath explicitly required India-based development teams [^2][^3]; IUVP invests in India-headquartered companies [^1]; Bluehill is Chennai-based investing in Indian deep-tech [^7].

**Founder profile: technical, often ex-Intel/TSMC.** A Bluehill GP advised by Dham noted: *"We are seeing people who have worked at Intel and TSMC returning to India to build companies. That pool is growing."* [^7] Dham's own AlphaICs co-founders Trivedi, Nagaraja, and Basavaraju are veteran semi engineers [^5]; the Sensesemi team is described as having "60+ years of combined experience from Texas Instruments and Intel" [^8].

**Notable gap vs. stated thesis:** IUVP publicly listed education and healthcare as focus areas, and disclosed portfolio names like ISGN (BPO) and mGinger (mobile ads) — but Dham's own most active recent investing (AlphaICs, Sensesemi, Bluehill semis pipeline) is almost entirely **back to silicon**, his original domain. The signal: when investing his own capital and reputation rather than fund LP capital, he reverts to chips.

## In Their Own Words

Note: Dham gives frequent interviews to Indian and US tech press, but most extant pieces paraphrase rather than quote verbatim. The following are factual statements Dham has made publicly:

- Dham has publicly emphasized that India's semiconductor opportunity is driven by talent returning from US chip companies — a thesis echoed by Bluehill.VC GP Manu Iyer (whom Dham advises): "We are seeing people who have worked at Intel and TSMC returning to India to build companies. That pool is growing." [^7]
- Dham serves on NITI Aayog's Frontier Tech Hub Expert Council and has publicly advocated for India to build domestic semiconductor design and fabrication capacity, lending his name to the Vinod Dham Centre of Excellence for Semiconductors and Microelectronics at Delhi Technological University (established May 2023) [^9][^14].
- Dham has stated publicly that AlphaICs's goal at founding (2016) was to build a "real AI processor" (RAP) chip for agent-based AI workloads — a thesis that anticipated current LLM-agent compute demands by nearly a decade [^5].
