## Background

Vig Chandramouli (full name Vignesh Chandramouli) is a Partner at Oak HC/FT, a healthcare and fintech venture and growth-equity firm. He joined the firm in 2014 and focuses on growth-equity and early-stage venture opportunities in healthcare [^1]. He is based in the New York City metropolitan area [^2].

Before Oak HC/FT, Chandramouli was an Associate at LLR Partners, a middle-market growth equity firm, where he concentrated on healthcare services investments [^1]. He earned dual Bachelor of Science degrees, magna cum laude, from the University of Pennsylvania — one from the Wharton School (Economics and Finance) and one from the School of Engineering and Applied Science (Systems Science & Engineering, with a Math minor) [^1].

He was named to the 2023 EVC List of emerging investors by the Emerging Venture Capitalists Association and to Business Insider's 2024 list of "45 Rising Stars of the Venture Capital Industry" [^1]. In June 2022 he authored a TechCrunch guest column, "9 ways founders can bring automation to healthcare" [^3].

Oak HC/FT does not currently have a Seedlist firm profile.

## Stated Thesis

Chandramouli publicly frames his investing around automation, workforce, and "time-to-value" ROI in healthcare. His self-reported focus areas include:

- **Healthcare automation across a fragmented value chain.** In his 2022 TechCrunch essay, he argued that healthcare — a $4.1 trillion industry — has fallen behind on automation, and that the "constant tug-of-war among patients, payers, providers and pharma" is a source of both cost and startup opportunity [^3].
- **Workforce beyond physicians.** He has repeatedly said clinical-AI startups over-index on doctors while overlooking the majority of the healthcare labor force: "70% of healthcare labor" comes from nurses and allied-health professionals, "with all the margin pressure, that feels like a ripe area for focus" [^4].
- **Measurable ROI over "burnout reduction."** In late 2025 he told MedCity News that the industry has moved past the first generation of ambient-AI pitches: "Version one … was about burnout, reduction in pajama time … Version two … is all about hard dollar ROI." He described "time-to-value" as "the number one way to evaluate new AI companies" [^5].
- **Structural risk-based pricing.** He has publicly warned founders that success-based pricing without careful unit economics is dangerous — "If they price, for example, on a success basis, and they're doing 10 actions and one of them is successful, that's where you get screwed in the unit economics" [^4] — but has also said the founders with the most conviction "will put their fees at risk" tied to a customer ROI cliff [^5].
- **Epic as a structural constraint.** He has advised founders to negotiate around Epic's product roadmap, noting that customers typically will not sign long-term deals for functionality Epic may ship within 12 months [^4].

## Inferred Thesis

Chandramouli's disclosed portfolio (active + prior investments named on his Oak HC/FT page) is overwhelmingly US healthcare, with essentially no fintech exposure despite the firm's HC/FT dual mandate. Of the 23 companies listed on his firm bio (12 active, 11 prior) [^1], all 23 are healthcare businesses — a 100% healthcare concentration by count.

Within healthcare, the portfolio clusters into a few visible patterns (based on the 23 named companies):

- **Provider-facing AI and clinical workflow software:** Ambience (ambient AI documentation), Notable (clinical automation), Syllable (patient-access AI), Osso VR (surgical training), Reveleer (payer/provider quality software) — roughly 5 of 23 (~22%).
- **Workforce and care-model businesses:** Stepful (allied-health training), Corner Health (nurse-practitioner primary care — added post-bio update), Main Street Health (rural value-based care), Wayspring (SUD care), Medical Home Network, Aspire Health, VillageMD, CareBridge, InterWell Health — roughly 9 of 23 (~39%) if the value-based care and workforce companies are grouped together.
- **Healthcare data / infrastructure / interoperability:** Unite Us, Veda, Turquoise Health (co-led 2026 Series C), Trovo Health, Thesis Care, Akido, Core Informatics — a meaningful cluster, though several are ambiguous between "data" and "care model."

**Stage.** His verified check-sizing spans from ~$15M seed rounds (Trovo Health, 2024) [^6] through mid-eight-figure Series B/C rounds (Stepful $31.5M Series B in 2024 and $55M Series C in 2026 [^7][^8]; Ambience $243M Series C in 2025 [^9]; Turquoise Health $40M Series C in 2026 [^10]). This is consistent with Oak HC/FT's positioning as a hybrid venture/growth-equity firm rather than a pure seed shop.

**Geography.** Publicly named companies are US-based (New York, Bay Area, Nashville-cluster health-services). No verified international investments in the disclosed list.

**Board pattern.** He holds voting board seats at Ambience, Medical Home Network, Osso VR, Stepful, Syllable, and Thesis Care, and observer seats at Main Street Health, Notable, and Reveleer [^1] — a heavy operator-engagement footprint even for a growth-oriented investor.

**Sample size and caveat.** The 23 companies above are the ones Oak HC/FT publicly attributes to him. Actual involvement (as observer vs. lead, historical vs. current) varies, and the "prior investments" bucket includes Oak realizations dating back nearly a decade. Percentages here are computed from that named set, not from a comprehensive deal log.
