## Background

Romain Lapeyre is the Co-Founder and CEO of Gorgias, a customer support helpdesk and conversational-AI platform built for e-commerce (originally focused on Shopify merchants) [^1][^2]. He founded the company in 2015 with Alex Plugaru [^1][^3]. Gorgias went through NUMA in Paris, then Techstars NYC, before relocating to the San Francisco Bay Area; Lapeyre is now based in New York [^3][^11].

Lapeyre studied at HEC Paris (2011–2015), earning a Master's in management with a focus on entrepreneurship, and spent an exchange semester at Fudan University in 2012 [^4]. Prior to Gorgias, he worked at A Little Market, a Paris-based online marketplace for handmade goods [^3].

Gorgias has raised roughly $100M+ across Seed through Series C-2, with backers including Alven, SaaStr Fund, CRV, Sapphire Ventures, Transpose Platform, and Shopify [^2][^5][^6]. Lapeyre is also active as an angel investor in operator-adjacent SaaS, e-commerce and developer infrastructure companies [^7][^8][^9][^10].

## Stated Thesis

Lapeyre has not published a formal angel-investing thesis. Third-party aggregators characterize his focus as E-Commerce, SaaS and Software at Pre-Seed, Seed and Series A, in the United States and France — but this is aggregator interpretation, not a self-authored statement [^7].

His own public writing centers on operating Gorgias (sales, partnerships, customer acquisition, AI in customer service) rather than investing philosophy [^12][^13][^14].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size is small — four publicly verifiable angel checks across primary sources.** Percentages would be misleading; qualitative observations only.

- **Sector concentration**: All four verified checks are in the e-commerce / SaaS stack that Gorgias itself sells into — a headless commerce platform (Swell), a Shopify BI/analytics tool (Polar Analytics), an e-commerce experience/page-builder platform (Shogun), and a webhook infrastructure tool used by SaaS/commerce teams (Hookdeck) [^7][^8][^9][^10][^15].
- **Stage**: Ranges from pre-seed ($320K Hookdeck) through Series A ($20M Swell) and Series B ($35M Shogun). No clear stage preference beyond "commerce-adjacent SaaS at whatever stage the operator network surfaces" [^7][^8][^9][^10].
- **Co-investor pattern**: Frequently appears alongside other e-commerce operator-angels — Brian Long (Attentive CEO), Domm Holland (Fast CEO), Bryant Chou (Webflow CTO), Mark Lavelle (ex-Magento CEO), Brian Magida (Warby Parker), his own Gorgias co-founder Alex Plugaru, and Chris Nguyen (LogDNA co-founder) [^8][^9][^10]. Institutional co-investors include Accel, VMG Partners, Y Combinator, Initialized, VMG Catalyst, Headline, and Matrix Partners [^8][^9][^10].
- **Geography**: Portfolio is majority U.S.-based (Swell in SF, Shogun in SF, Hookdeck in Montreal). Polar Analytics is Paris-based [^8][^9][^10][^15].
- **Notable gap**: No verified fintech, crypto, healthcare or deep-tech checks — his angel activity clusters tightly around the e-commerce infrastructure adjacent to his day job.

The best characterization is **operator-angel writing small checks into the Shopify/e-commerce SaaS ecosystem he knows deeply**, typically alongside other e-commerce founder-operators rather than as a systematic investing program.

## In Their Own Words

On the shift in the e-commerce environment (TechCrunch, Aug 2022):

> "DTC brands need to generate more money from their existing customers" [^5]

> "The environment we're working in has changed quite a bit." [^5]

> "Lower margins and higher acquisition costs require businesses to capture more revenue from their existing customers, and customer experience is at the heart of that effort." [^5]

On managing burn during the 2022 downturn:

> "slowed down hiring on the go-to-market side, assessed where we can cut costs and asked the team to be smart about how we use the cash we have on hand." [^5]

On how the CEO role changes with company stage (The Org profile):

> "The job of CEO changes all the time, basically every time the startup is moving to the next stage." [^11]

> "In the beginning, when it's just the founders, the job of the CEO is to find product-market fit, which requires a 'keep trying until it works' mindset." [^11]

> "Today, we're at the village stage, between 100 and 500 employees, where a CEO can't know personally everyone so my role is to figure out how to communicate a clear direction for the company to my team while distributing ownership to make sure that decisions are taken by people who have the information and not by people who only have decision power." [^11]

On his and Alex Plugaru's co-founder fit:

> "Our skill sets were very complimentary. Alex had a tech background, I'm more a go-to-market guy and we were both very product-oriented." [^11]

On the pre-PMF grind:

> "To use a hiking metaphor, it was like walking under the rain for a long time, but all we were thinking about wasn't how hard it was but that we had to keep moving forward." [^11]
