## Background

Pete Koomen is a General Partner at Y Combinator and the co-founder and former CTO of Optimizely, the A/B testing and experimentation platform [^1][^2]. He co-founded Optimizely with Dan Siroker in January 2010 after both had worked together at Google; the company went through YC's Winter 2010 batch [^3]. Before Optimizely, Koomen was a product manager at Google from 2006 to 2009, where he helped launch and grow Google App Engine [^4].

Koomen helped Optimizely grow from inception to over $100M in annual recurring revenue [^1]. The company raised a $28M Series A led by Benchmark in 2013, a $57M Series B led by Andreessen Horowitz in 2014, and a $58M Series C led by Index Ventures in 2015 [^3]. Optimizely was acquired by Episerver in 2020, with the combined company rebranded as Optimizely in January 2021 [^3]. Koomen co-authored "A/B Testing: The Most Powerful Way to Turn Clicks Into Customers" [^1].

In October 2023, Koomen announced he was joining Y Combinator as a Group Partner; he was later promoted to General Partner [^1][^5]. He holds a master's degree in Computer Science from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign [^1][^6].

## Stated Thesis

Koomen has not published a formal investment thesis, but his writing on AI-native software design points to where he focuses his attention. In his April 2025 essay "AI Horseless Carriages," he argues that most current AI applications are poorly designed because they mimic pre-AI software patterns [^7]. He writes:

> "Most AI apps should be _agent builders_, not agents." [^7]

He uses Gmail's Gemini integration as a representative failure case, arguing AI-native software should "maximize a user's leverage in a specific domain" rather than generating generic outputs the user must then edit [^7].

At YC, Koomen has advised notable alumni including PostHog, Ditto, Treble.ai, Waterplan, and TigerEye; companies he has worked with at YC are collectively valued at approximately $1.3B [^1].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size caveat:** Public records of Koomen's angel checks are limited. Signal by NFX lists him with roughly 100+ recorded investments, but only a handful of named deals are publicly verifiable. The picture below is qualitative rather than statistical.

Based on the publicly named investments — Cerbos, Sublime Security, Milk Video, and others — Koomen's angel checks cluster around:

- **Stage:** Pre-seed and seed only. Typical check size is reported as $5K–$50K with a sweet spot near $25K [^8].
- **Sector:** Developer infrastructure, security, and enterprise SaaS dominate the named deals. Cerbos (authorization-as-code), Sublime Security (email security platform), and Milk Video (video creation tooling) all fit the "tools for technical or product teams" pattern that aligns with his Optimizely background [^8].
- **AI angle:** Koomen's recent writing and YC work emphasize AI-native software design, suggesting newer checks skew toward AI-first developer tooling.
- **Geography:** US-centric, with most named investments in the San Francisco Bay Area.

Note that since joining YC as a Group/General Partner in late 2023, the primary mechanism by which Koomen backs companies is through YC's standard investment, not personal angel checks. Founders seeking him should expect the YC application path rather than a direct angel pitch.

## In Their Own Words

On evaluating founders, from a 2026 interview with the University of Illinois Grainger College of Engineering:

> "You're trying to bet that these are the super smart, relentlessly resourceful founders who are going to realize their idea is probably bad and keep at it and find another one and find another one until they find something that works." [^11]

> "It's a bet on the people more than anything else." [^11]

On the current opportunity for founders:

> "I can't think of a time when there's been more good ideas lying around for founders." [^11]

On what makes a great founder, from a 2018 Y Combinator Q&A:

> "The two most important skills in a founder are 1. resilience and 2. the ability to attract people that are better than you are. Develop those and you can solve any problem." [^12]

On the design of AI applications, from "AI Horseless Carriages" (April 2025):

> "Most AI apps should be _agent builders_, not agents." [^7]

> "Many of them are infuriatingly useless in the same way that Gmail's Gemini integration is." [^7]

> "It's a world where I don't have to spend time doing mundane work because agents do it for me." [^7]
