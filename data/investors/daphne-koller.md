## Background

Daphne Koller (born August 27, 1968, in Jerusalem, Israel) is a machine learning researcher, technology entrepreneur, and Adjunct Professor of Computer Science at Stanford University [^1][^2]. She earned a B.Sc. from the Hebrew University of Jerusalem in 1985 (age 17), an M.Sc. from the same institution in 1986 (age 18), and a Ph.D. in Computer Science from Stanford in 1993 under Joseph Halpern [^1]. She was a postdoctoral researcher at UC Berkeley (1993–1995) under Stuart J. Russell before joining the Stanford computer science faculty in 1995 [^1].

Koller served on Stanford's faculty for 18 years as the Rajeev Motwani Professor of Computer Science, where her research spanned probabilistic graphical models, machine learning, computer vision, and computational biology [^1][^2]. She co-authored the standard textbook *Probabilistic Graphical Models: Principles and Techniques* (MIT Press, 2009) with Nir Friedman [^1]. Her doctoral students include Lise Getoor, Carlos Guestrin, Su-In Lee, Mehran Sahami, Suchi Saria, Eran Segal, and Ben Taskar [^1].

In 2012, Koller co-founded Coursera with Stanford colleague Andrew Ng, launching one of the earliest and largest MOOC (massive open online course) platforms; she served as co-CEO and later President before departing in 2016 [^1][^2][^3]. Coursera went public on the NYSE (COUR) in 2021 [^3]. She left Coursera to join Alphabet's longevity research company Calico Labs as Chief Computing Officer (2016–2018) [^1][^2].

In 2018, Koller founded insitro, a machine-learning-driven drug discovery and development company based in South San Francisco, where she serves as Founder and CEO and sits on the board [^2][^4]. insitro has raised more than $600 million in venture financing: a Series A led by ARCH Venture Partners in 2019 with participation from Andreessen Horowitz, GV, Foresite Capital, and Bezos Expeditions; a $143M Series B led by Andreessen Horowitz on May 26, 2020 (Vijay Pande joined the insitro board); and a $400M Series C led by Canada Pension Plan Investment Board on March 15, 2021, with participation from Andreessen Horowitz, T. Rowe Price, BlackRock, ARCH, Foresite, GV, Third Rock, Two Sigma Ventures, Casdin Capital, HOF Capital, Alexandria Venture Investments, Temasek, and Softbank [^5][^6][^7].

In October 2020, Koller co-founded Engageli, an interactive digital learning platform, alongside her husband Dan Avida (General Partner at Opus Capital), Stanford emeritus professor Serge Plotkin, and Jamie Nacht Farrell (former executive at 2U and Trilogy); Engageli launched with a $14.5M seed round, with Benchmark partner Alex Balkanski investing privately [^8][^9][^10]. Koller serves on Engageli's board [^11].

Koller also holds the title of Founding Pillar at Pillar VC, a Boston-based early-stage venture firm focused on backing scientists and technical founders [^12].

Recognition includes: MacArthur Fellowship (2004); ACM Prize in Computing (2008); election to the National Academy of Engineering (2011); National Academy of Sciences (2023); ACM–AAAI Allen Newell Award (2019); TIME 100 Most Influential People (2012); TIME 100 Most Influential People in AI (2024); American Academy of Arts and Sciences Fellowship (2014); and ISCB Fellowship (2017) [^1][^2].

## Stated Thesis

Koller has not published a personal angel-investing thesis distinct from her operating focus at insitro. Her public commentary consistently emphasizes the same two convictions:

1. **Machine learning applied to biology at scale.** In her Forbes interview with Jillian D'Onfro, she explained insitro's approach as: "We can do experiments in a matter of weeks instead of years" [^4].
2. **Integrated interdisciplinary teams over siloed specialists.** She has repeatedly described insitro's differentiation as: "Getting them to work together as an integrated team is really our secret sauce" [^4].

Her Pillar VC affiliation is described in terms of "backing scientists and technical founders" [^12]; her personal angel activity outside her founded companies is not publicly documented in detail.

## Inferred Thesis

Koller's publicly verifiable "portfolio" is dominated by companies she personally founded rather than external angel investments. This is characteristic of a founder-operator rather than a systematic angel investor:

- **Founded companies (3 verified):** Coursera (2012, edtech / MOOCs), insitro (2018, ML-driven drug discovery), Engageli (2020, higher-ed video learning).
- **Sector concentration:** two of three founded companies are in education/learning; the third (insitro, her current primary role) is at the intersection of machine learning, biology, and drug discovery. This mirrors her academic research trajectory (Stanford CS + computational biology).
- **Stage:** all three founded companies were founded from scratch (pre-seed / founding). insitro then scaled through Series A (2019), Series B (2020), and Series C (2021) with a rotating set of top-tier biotech and generalist investors.

Founders seeking Koller's involvement should treat her primarily as an operator/founder-friendly figure whose current bandwidth is committed to insitro. Her Founding Pillar role at Pillar VC [^12] indicates she also serves as a scientific advisor and check-writer through that firm, though specific Pillar-portfolio deals she has personally led are not disclosed on the public Pillar site.

The sample size (3 founded companies, no independently verified external angel checks) is too small for percentages. What can be said qualitatively:

- **Deep technical / academic pedigree of co-founders.** Every founded company involves a co-founder with academic or top-tier operator credentials (Andrew Ng at Coursera; Serge Plotkin, Dan Avida, Jamie Nacht Farrell at Engageli).
- **Data-and-ML-first product architectures.** All three companies use large-scale data collection and machine learning as core product infrastructure, not as marketing veneer.
- **Preference for missions with a clear public-benefit narrative** (democratizing higher education, accelerating drug discovery for hard-to-treat diseases).
