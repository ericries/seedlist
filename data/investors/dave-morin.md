## Background

Dave Morin was born October 14, 1980 in Helena, Montana, and holds a BA in Economics from the University of Colorado Boulder (2003) [^1]. He began his career at Apple in marketing (2003–2006) [^1], then joined Facebook (2006–2010) as a Senior Platform Manager, where he co-created Facebook Platform and Facebook Connect [^1].

In 2010, Morin co-founded Path, a private social network, and served as CEO; Path was sold in 2015 and shut down on October 18, 2018 [^1]. He was a co-founder and general partner at Slow Ventures, where he participated in investments including Slack, Pinterest, Nextdoor, and Robinhood [^2] [^7].

In 2020, Morin co-founded Offline Ventures with his wife Brit Morin [^3]. Offline is an early-stage venture firm and product studio; the debut fund closed at $100 million with Apple serving as an anchor investor, and the fund allocation was structured as 80% to early-stage investments and 20% to incubating new ventures [^3]. Morin is also a co-founder of Fwd.us (2013), a 501(c)(4) advocacy organization [^1].

## Stated Thesis

Offline Ventures publicly describes itself as focused on "humanist technology" — companies whose products support mental health, real-world connection, and human wellbeing rather than growth-at-all-costs [^2] [^7].

Per TechCrunch's coverage of the fund's launch, Offline's stated focus areas include mental health and digital therapeutics, connected hardware and fitness technology, telemedicine platforms, web3, and social networking [^3].

Morin has stated the firm is "exploring opportunities and companies at the intersection of fundamental humanist technological shifts in consumer, Web 3.0, women's health, brain health, sustainability, and bringing the offline online" [^2].
