## Background

Sarah Friar is Chief Financial Officer of OpenAI, a role she has held since June 2024 [^1]. She joined OpenAI after six years as CEO of Nextdoor (2018-2024), which she took public in 2021 (NYSE:KIND) [^2][^3]. Before Nextdoor she was CFO of Square (now Block) from 2012 to 2018, leading the company's 2015 IPO [^2][^3]. Earlier in her career she was SVP of Finance & Strategy at Salesforce, a Managing Director in Equity Research at Goldman Sachs, and began her career as a consultant at McKinsey in London and South Africa [^3][^4].

Friar earned an MEng in Metallurgy, Economics, and Management from the University of Oxford and an MBA from the Stanford Graduate School of Business, where she was an Arjay Miller Scholar [^4]. She was granted an OBE by Queen Elizabeth II in 2019 for services to entrepreneurship [^4], and she co-founded the nonprofit Ladies Who Launch [^4].

Friar currently sits on the board of Walmart (joined 2018; chairs the Technology and eCommerce Committee) and ConsenSys, is a trustee of Stanford University, and serves on the advisory boards of Operation HOPE, the Blavatnik School of Government at Oxford, and the Stanford Digital Economy Lab [^3][^4]. Prior board seats include Slack Technologies (2017-2021), New Relic (2013-2018), and three Dragoneer Growth Opportunities SPACs [^5].

## Stated Thesis

Friar does not publish a formal angel-investing thesis. In podcast interviews about her operating career, she has said she is drawn to investing time and capital in businesses that combine commercial ambition with purpose. On Bessemer Venture Partners' *Wish I Knew* podcast, she has discussed why "strong conviction in a market thesis can buoy you through hard times" and why founders should never assume it is a bad time to raise money [^6]. In a 2022 interview she described her leadership stance as "people first" and said her mantra is to hire well and give people "autonomy, encouragement, and tough love" [^7].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 6 independently verified angel investments between 2020 and 2022, patterns emerge — though the sample is small and inferences should be treated as directional:

- **Fintech and payments dominate**: Bond Financial Technologies (banking-as-a-service, 2020) [^8], Step Mobile (teen banking, 2021) [^9], and UNIPaaS (embedded payments for marketplaces, 2021) [^10] — 3 of 6 confirmed rounds. This aligns with her CFO tenure at Square.
- **Northern Ireland / UK diaspora**: MedAll (Belfast healthtech, 2022) [^11] and Cloudsmith (Belfast developer tools, 2021) [^12] reflect ties to her home region.
- **Co-investment with alumni networks**: Alongside former Square colleagues (Gokul Rajaram appears in UNIPaaS and Step rounds; Jacqueline Reses appears in Step) [^9][^10], and other operator-angels (Eric Yuan, Ryan Petersen in Bond) [^8].
- **Later-stage angel participation**: 4 of 6 verified rounds are Series A or later, unusual for a pure angel — reflects her senior operator profile giving her access to already-priced growth rounds.
- **Sector spread**: fintech (3), developer infrastructure (1), healthtech (1), real-estate marketplace (1). Not a specialist thesis.
- **Check size**: NFX Signal characterizes her sweet spot as $25K, range $5K-$50K [^13]. Individual round sizes she participates in range from ~$3M to $100M+.

Friar is also an LP in Tapestry VC Fund III (announced July 2026), which focuses on European repeat founders [^14].
