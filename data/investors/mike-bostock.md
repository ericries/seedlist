## Background

Mike Bostock is an American computer scientist and data-visualization specialist best known as the lead developer of D3.js and as co-founder and CTO of Observable [^1][^2].

Bostock completed his PhD in Computer Science at Stanford University, advised by Jeffrey Heer in the Stanford Visualization Group. He collaborated with Heer on Protovis, a JavaScript-based visualization toolkit that preceded D3 [^1][^2]. In 2011, Bostock, Heer, and Vadim Ogievetsky released D3.js, which the IEEE Computer Society recognized in 2017 as the "de facto standard for web-based interactive visualization" [^1].

From roughly 2011 until May 2015, Bostock worked as a graphics editor at *The New York Times*, where he built interactive data-journalism pieces using D3. He shared Gerald Loeb Awards for Images/Visuals in 2013, 2014, and 2015 for that work [^1].

In 2020, Bostock and former Google VP of Engineering Melody Meckfessel launched Observable publicly, a collaborative platform for data analysis and visualization. Observable announced a $10.5M Series A on November 26, 2020, led by Sequoia Capital and Acrew Capital [^3][^4]. Bostock serves as CTO; Meckfessel is CEO [^3][^4].

Bostock is based in San Francisco [^2][^5]. He maintains an active presence on GitHub (`@mbostock`) and X/Twitter (`@mbostock`), and continues to publish visualization notebooks on Observable at `@mbostock` [^5][^6].

## Stated Thesis

Bostock has not publicly stated a personal investment thesis. He is an operator and open-source maintainer rather than an active venture investor.

His publicly stated *product* philosophy — relevant context for founders who might approach him for advice on data-tooling companies — centers on lowering the effort required to make sense of data. In a November 2021 Observable Q&A he described his goal as building "a ladder of abstraction" that lets users "ascend or descend the ladder without having to start over," so that "not only will more people visualize data but these people will visualize data more often" [^7].
