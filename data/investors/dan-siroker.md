## Background

Dan Siroker is the co-founder and CEO of Limitless (formerly Rewind AI), a personal AI memory product. He previously co-founded Optimizely, the experimentation and A/B testing platform.

Siroker was a Google product manager who left the company to join Barack Obama's 2008 presidential campaign as Director of Analytics, where he applied A/B testing to email, donation pages, and volunteer sign-up flows on the campaign website [^1][^2]. After the campaign, in 2010 he co-founded Optimizely with Pete Koomen, who had also been at Google [^3]. Optimizely was part of Y Combinator's Winter 2010 batch and pivoted during the second week of the program [^3]. Optimizely raised more than $200 million from investors including Andreessen Horowitz, Index Ventures, GV, and Goldman Sachs, scaled to over 1,000 customers including Gap, StubHub, IBM, and The Wall Street Journal, and was acquired by Episerver in 2020; the combined company rebranded as Optimizely in January 2021 [^4]. Siroker had stepped back from the CEO role at Optimizely in 2017 (Jay Larson took over) [^4].

In 2020, Siroker founded Rewind AI with co-founder Brett Bejcek [^5]. Rewind began as a Mac application that recorded and indexed everything a user saw, said, or heard on their computer, making it searchable later. The company raised a seed round of approximately $10M from Andreessen Horowitz (led by David Ulevitch), First Round Capital, and others in November 2022 [^5]. In summer 2023, Rewind raised a $12M Series A at a $350M valuation from New Enterprise Associates, conducted through an unconventional public fundraising process in which Siroker shared the pitch deck and routed investors to rewind.ai/invest; the campaign generated more than 170 preliminary offers [^6][^7]. The company rebranded to Limitless and launched a wearable Pendant device in April 2024, expanding from desktop software to AI hardware [^8].

On December 5, 2025, Meta acquired Limitless to bolster its push into AI wearables. Financial terms were not disclosed [^9]. Siroker also serves as an investing partner at Rebel Fund, a venture fund formed by Y Combinator alumni that invests in YC seed-stage startups [^10].

## Stated Thesis

Siroker has not published a formal angel investing thesis. In public commentary on AI and product building, he has emphasized application-layer value over model wrappers, the importance of transparency with investors, and ruthless prioritization. On thin AI wrappers, he said he believes that pattern "is likely going to go away" and that "the true value is in the app side" [^7]. On founder qualities, he has cited Paul Graham's framing that "the best founders are relentlessly resourceful" [^7].

Siroker's public fundraising experiment for Rewind's Series A also doubles as a public-facing thesis on how investors and founders should relate. He has said that "transparency breeds trust and trust is the most important thing we have," and described the lead investor relationship as "a relationship I'm gonna have for decades" [^7].

- **Sector:** Productivity software, video tooling, analytics, and brain-computer interface — clustering around tools that help individuals or teams capture, retrieve, or act on data. - **Timing:** All four dated investments fall within a three-month window in summer 2021 (May 25 – August 18), suggesting concentrated activity rather than steady deployment.
- **Exits:** Two of his disclosed angel positions have exited — ClearBrain (acquired 2020) and Milk Video (acquired June 2025) [^11].

**Rebel Fund participation:** Through Rebel Fund, Siroker is associated with seed-stage Y Combinator companies more broadly. Rebel Fund has invested in more than 200 YC startups [^10]. Individual deal-level attribution to Siroker via Rebel Fund is not publicly broken out.

**Notable gaps:** Despite Siroker's deep operating experience in marketing software, A/B testing, and (more recently) AI hardware and personal AI, the verified angel portfolio is too thin to determine whether his angel investing tracks his operating expertise. Founders considering him as an angel should treat his time and product judgment as the primary signal, not portfolio breadth.
