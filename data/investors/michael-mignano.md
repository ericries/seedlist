## Background

Michael Mignano is a General Partner at Union Square Ventures (USV), based in the New York metro area. He joined USV in April 2026 after three years as a Partner at Lightspeed Venture Partners [^1] [^2]. Before becoming a venture investor, Mignano co-founded and served as CEO of Anchor, the podcast creation platform he started in 2016 with Nir Zicherman. Anchor was acquired by Spotify in February 2019 in a deal reported at more than $150M; Mignano subsequently led Spotify's talk audio business as Head of Talk (podcasts, video, and live) until departing in May 2022 [^3] [^4].

Mignano studied Computer Science at the University of Delaware. Earlier in his career he worked at Atlantic Records, then moved into product roles at Aviary (acquired by Adobe in 2014) before founding Anchor [^3]. He was named to Fortune's "40 Under 40" list in 2019 [^3].

In October 2024, Mignano and Zicherman reunited to launch Oboe, an AI-powered education startup. Mignano remained a full-time Lightspeed partner while serving on Oboe's board, with Zicherman as CEO [^5]. Mignano writes regularly at his blog "Background Noise" and at migna.no, and is an active essayist on consumer technology, AI, and media [^6] [^7].

## Stated Thesis

At Lightspeed, Mignano publicly described his focus as "the intersection of AI and the creativity supply chain," concentrating on seed and inception-stage product-oriented founders [^1] [^8]. In announcing his move to USV in April 2026, he framed his go-forward approach as thesis-driven rather than capital-led: "to be thesis-driven. That's always been the USV playbook, and I think it's exactly the right one to be running in the years ahead of us" [^7]. He has said he wants to back founders building "something that doesn't automate, but instead obliterates" [^7].

His public writing emphasizes a small set of recurring themes: that algorithmic recommendation has replaced social graphs as the dominant content-distribution model ("The End of Social Media and the Rise of Recommendation Media," July 2022) [^9]; that generative AI is restructuring the "creativity supply chain" across media [^6]; and that founders should embrace a "grow or die" mentality [^10].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 8 verified Lightspeed-era investments plus 1 verified USV investment (9 total), Mignano's investing behavior is sharply concentrated in AI applications and AI infrastructure, with a heavy bias toward consumer-facing creator and productivity tools:

- **Sector mix (9 verified investments):** AI generative media (Suno, Pika) — 2 of 9 (22%); AI productivity / knowledge work (Granola, Macroscope, Lightfield) — 3 of 9 (33%); AI infrastructure / foundation (xAI, Neuralink) — 2 of 9 (22%); AI-content monetization (TollBit) — 1 of 9 (11%); consumer hardware / gaming (Board) — 1 of 9 (11%). All 9 verified investments are AI-adjacent.
- **Stage distribution:** Predominantly seed and Series A. Of the 9 named investments, he led seed at Granola ($4.25M, May 2023), led Series A at TollBit ($24M, Oct 2024), led Series A at Macroscope ($30M, July 2025), led Series A at Board ($20M, June 2026), and was the Lightspeed point person on the Pika Series A ($55M, Nov 2023) and Suno Series B ($125M, May 2024) [^11] [^12] [^13] [^14] [^15] [^16].
- **Geography:** Mix of NYC (TollBit, Board, Anchor heritage), Bay Area (Pika, xAI, Neuralink), Cambridge (Suno), and London (Granola). Notably not US-only — he led a London seed deal within weeks of Granola's founding [^11].
- **Founder profile:** Repeat / product-led founders are a clear pattern. Granola's Pedregal had previously sold Socratic to Google; Board's Brynn Putnam previously founded Mirror (acquired by Lululemon); Pika and Suno are research-heavy founder teams; xAI/Neuralink are Musk vehicles. Of 9 verified investments, at least 4 (44%) have a founder with a prior notable exit or research credential.
- **Decision speed:** The Granola seed closed within two months of the company's founding in March 2023 [^11], consistent with Mignano's stated preference for early conviction.
- **Co-investor patterns:** At Lightspeed, recurring co-investors include Nat Friedman & Daniel Gross (Suno B, Granola B), Founder Collective (Suno), Section 32 (TollBit), Spark Capital and Betaworks (Granola), and Thrive Capital and GV (Macroscope) [^12] [^13] [^14] [^15] [^16].
- **Gap vs. stated thesis:** Mignano's "creativity supply chain" framing understates how much of his actual book is AI productivity software (Granola, Macroscope, Lightfield) rather than pure creative tools. His Lightspeed-era involvement in xAI and Neuralink also reflects a willingness to participate in large foundation-model and frontier-hardware rounds that don't fit the "creativity" framing at all.

Sample size is small (9 confirmed) — this analysis describes patterns rather than statistically reliable percentages.

> "to be thesis-driven. That's always been the USV playbook, and I think it's exactly the right one to be running in the years ahead of us." [^7]
