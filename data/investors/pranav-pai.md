## Background

Pranav Pai is a Founding Partner and Chief Investment Officer at 3one4 Capital, a Bengaluru-based early-stage venture capital firm he co-founded in 2016 with his brother Siddarth Pai [^1][^2][^3]. The firm now manages more than US$550M-850M in AUM across four funds and 90+ portfolio companies [^1][^4]. Pranav earned an M.S. in Electrical Engineering from Stanford University (2011–2013) after a B.E. from R. V. College of Engineering, Bangalore, and previously worked as a Senior Product Manager at EdCast (2013–2016) before starting 3one4 [^1]. He is the son of Mohandas Pai, former CFO of Infosys, and serves on multiple portfolio company boards including Licious, Darwinbox, Jupiter, and Dozee [^1]. He was named to Entrepreneur Magazine India's "35 Under 35" and AIWMI/IAAIF's "40 Under 40 Alternative Investments Professionals" list, and to the GQ List of 25 Most Influential Young Indians 2020-21 [^2].

## Stated Thesis

3one4 Capital publicly positions itself as delivering "transformative capital for the new Indian economy," focusing on early-stage founders solving population-scale problems for India [^5][^2]. Pranav has said his 2016 founding thesis rested on three minority views: that UPI and Jio would enable technology to solve population-scale problems across India, that Indian VC was drowning in "outside-in" investing, and that a fund run by operators rather than finance professionals would make better judgments about what India actually needed [^4]. Recent stated focus areas include "Service as Software" — companies positioned as the destination rather than just the tool — plus export-oriented manufacturing, frontier technology (including sovereign AI capability), and commerce [^4][^6].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on public statements and portfolio disclosures, 3one4's actual activity reflects:

- **Stage**: Pranav has led 70+ seed and early Series A investments over 10 years — "Our best investments are all two guys on a laptop when they come to us. That's how early we go, and we hope we can stay invested all the way to IPO" [^4].
- **Geography**: Overwhelmingly India-focused with select US exposure [^2]. The firm operates from Bengaluru [^1][^2].
- **Fund size discipline**: Deliberately sub-$250M funds; Pranav argues base rates in Indian VC (56 IPOs of 200,000 registered Indian startups over 15 years) don't support $500M+ funds needing $2B returns [^4].
- **Portfolio outcomes**: 3one4 has produced at least 4 unicorns (Licious, Darwinbox, Open, Raise per press coverage), 1 IPO, and 15 acquisitions across 134 companies [^7][^4].
- **Sector breadth**: Portfolio spans consumer (Licious), HR/SaaS (Darwinbox), fintech (Open, Jupiter, BharatPe, Khatabook), health (Dozee, Bugworks), semiconductors (Agnit — gallium nitride), legal tech (Nyayanidhi — litigation OS seeded at $2M), and consumer mobility (Yulu, Spinny) [^7][^4][^8].
- **Sample size caveat**: These are qualitative descriptions from press coverage; the firm has not published a full portfolio breakdown by sector percentage, so specific percentages are not computed.

The delta between stated and inferred: Pranav's public framing centers "transformative capital" and India tech sovereignty; the actual portfolio also includes substantial consumer-DTC (Licious, meat delivery) and HR-SaaS (Darwinbox) that pre-date the "Service as Software" and manufacturing theses.

## In Their Own Words

Pranav Pai on how early 3one4 invests: "Our best investments are all two guys on a laptop when they come to us. That's how early we go, and we hope we can stay invested all the way to IPO" [^4].

Pranav Pai on Licious's brand shift: "Imagine going from black-packet stigma to customers fighting a lottery to put their face on your box. That is a 180-degree inversion" [^4].

Pranav Pai on India's future thesis: "If you work backwards from the question of what India's technological sovereignty depends on, there are a lot of VC-fundable opportunities there" [^4].

Pranav Pai on founder psychology and competitive pressure: "The strongest thing I've seen in a founder is the ability to harness that for the right outcome" [^4].

Pranav Pai on how to judge a fund manager: "To judge me, you look at: has he made money? Has he scored the goals? Has he won the games?" [^4].
