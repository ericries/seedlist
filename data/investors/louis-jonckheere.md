## Background

Louis Jonckheere is a Belgian tech entrepreneur and operator-angel based in Ghent. He co-founded Showpad in 2011 with Pieterjan ("PJ") Bouten and Peter Minne, building it into a global sales enablement platform that reached approximately $100M in annual recurring revenue before being acquired by Vector Capital and Insight Partners in late 2025 [^1][^2]. Through March 2022 he served as Chief Product Officer, leading product, design, and engineering, and was also named President of the company [^1][^3].

Before Showpad, Jonckheere and Bouten co-founded In The Pocket, a Ghent-based mobile development agency, in 2010 — he continues to serve on its board [^1][^4]. Prior to that he led Strategic Partnerships at the Belgian social network Netlog, where he first met Bouten [^1]. He holds a Master's in Commerce and Law from Vlerick Business School, where he now sits on the board [^1].

In late 2025, following the close of the Showpad chapter, Jonckheere joined Aikido Security as General Manager for the US, leading the Ghent-headquartered security platform's North American expansion [^5][^6]. He is also co-founder of Wintercircus, a Ghent event venue and tech-startup hub [^4][^7].

## Stated Thesis

Jonckheere has not published a formal investment thesis. Public signal comes from his portfolio behavior and from his own framing in posts and press: he positions himself as a builder-investor who backs founders he can help operationally, particularly in B2B SaaS and around the Ghent operator-angel network [^4][^7]. On closing Showpad he wrote: "After 14 years, I'm closing the Showpad chapter of my life, following its acquisition by Vector Capital and Insight Partners. … As I close this chapter, I'm ready to share all my lessons - good & bad - from the Showpad journey, and to keep building. Stay tuned!" [^2].

## Inferred Thesis

Based on 17 angel investments tracked by Tracxn and corroborated by individual press releases, the following patterns hold (small sample — describe qualitatively rather than as precise percentages) [^4][^8]:

- **Stage**: Predominantly pre-seed and seed checks, with occasional follow-on into Series A/B (e.g., Aikido Series B participation as both investor and operator) [^4][^6].
- **Check size**: Reported $10K–$50K range, sweet spot ~$25K [^8][^4].
- **Sector concentration**: Enterprise applications (B2B SaaS) dominate, with notable exposure to fintech/spend management (Spendesk), legal tech (Henchman), security (Aikido), construction tech (AproPLAN), AI applications (BetterPic, Eagl, Holmes), and healthcare AI (IntelliProve) [^4][^8][^9][^10].
- **Geography**: Strong Ghent / Belgian SaaS bias, with selective European (Spendesk – Paris) and US exposure [^4][^8].
- **Co-investor patterns**: Recurs alongside other Ghent operator-angels including Lorenz Bogaert (Teamleader / Netlog), Jeroen De Wit (Teamleader), Joris Van Der Gucht (Silverfin / Ravical), and Matthias Geeroms (Lighthouse) — the core "Ghent mafia" of B2B SaaS founders-turned-investors [^9][^10].
- **Outcomes**: 4 reported exits (Henchman acquired 2024, DESelect 2025, AproPLAN 2025, Daltix 2020) and 1 unicorn (Spendesk) per Tracxn [^4].
- **Notable gap vs. stated focus**: Despite a Signal/NFX classification that flags "hardware" and Bay Area exposure, the verifiable portfolio is overwhelmingly software and Europe-centric [^8][^4].

Sample is small — readers should treat the above as descriptive of confirmed investments, not as definitive thesis percentages.
