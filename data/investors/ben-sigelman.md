## Background

Ben Sigelman is an angel investor and General Manager of ServiceNow Cloud Observability, the business unit created when ServiceNow acquired his previous company, Lightstep, in 2021 [^1][^2].

Sigelman began his career at Google, where he co-created **Dapper** (Google's internal distributed tracing system) and worked on **Monarch** (Google's planet-scale metrics system) [^1][^3]. In 2015 he co-founded Lightstep with Ben Cronin and Daniel Spoonhower, building the company into a distributed-tracing and observability platform for microservices architectures [^3][^4]. Lightstep raised funding from Redpoint, Sequoia, Altimeter, and Harrison Metal before being acquired by ServiceNow on June 15, 2021 for undisclosed terms [^4][^5].

Sigelman is also the co-creator of two widely adopted CNCF open-source observability standards: **OpenTracing** and its successor **OpenTelemetry** (OTel) [^1][^3][^6]. He is co-author of the O'Reilly book *Distributed Tracing in Practice* [^7]. He holds a bachelor's degree from Brown University (1999–2003) [^8].

Following the ServiceNow acquisition, Sigelman took the role of General Manager of ServiceNow Cloud Observability and continues to run the observability business inside ServiceNow [^1][^2]. He describes his angel activity as a personal side-line — he has publicly called himself "a tiny angel" in Coval, a voice-AI company [^8].

## Stated Thesis

Self-reported. Sigelman does not publish a formal investment thesis. Public writing, interview appearances, and his angel portfolio are consistent with a narrow, personal-network-driven focus:

- **Observability, distributed tracing, and OpenTelemetry-adjacent infrastructure** — reflecting Sigelman's decade-plus of technical work in the space [^3][^6][^9]
- **Developer tools and enterprise infrastructure for AI-era workloads** — including agent frameworks and voice AI [^8][^10]
- **Small check sizes** — Sigelman has described his angel role in Coval as "a tiny angel" [^8]

He does not describe himself as a professional investor. His day job is running an observability P&L at ServiceNow.

## Inferred Thesis

Sigelman's angel portfolio, per Tracxn's tracking, comprises approximately **6–10 investments** clustered in a narrow band around observability, developer tools, and AI infrastructure [^10][^11]. This is too small a sample for percentage breakdowns; the pattern is qualitative:

**Sector concentration:** Every publicly documented Sigelman angel check maps to either (a) observability/instrumentation directly overlapping his Lightstep/OpenTelemetry expertise (Odigos, Laminar), (b) messaging/data infrastructure adjacent to observability workloads (WarpStream), or (c) AI infrastructure with an operations/agent tooling angle (Coval, Continua) [^9][^10][^11].

**Geographic focus:** United States (Bay Area/Boston) and Israel — the two geographies where his professional network is deepest and where the observability/dev-tools founder pool is concentrated [^10].

**Stage focus:** Seed. All documented checks are at seed stage; no verified Series A+ participation as an angel [^10][^11].

**Co-investor patterns:** Sigelman consistently appears alongside domain-specialist funds (Venture Guides, Firestreak Ventures, Mango Capital, Salesforce Ventures on Odigos; Y Combinator and Atlantic.vc on Laminar; TLV Partners and TQ Ventures on MetalBear) rather than generalist top-tier VCs [^9][^11][^12]. He tends to be a small "expertise" angel added to an institutional round, not a lead check.

**Exit history:** WarpStream, a cloud real-time data streaming platform in his angel portfolio, was acquired on September 9, 2024 [^11].

**Sample size caveat:** With only a handful of publicly documented angel checks, this analysis is qualitative rather than statistical.
