## Background

David Vélez was born in 1981 in Medellín, Colombia, and holds dual Colombian and Costa Rican citizenship [^1]. He completed a bachelor's degree in Management Science & Engineering at Stanford University and an MBA at Stanford Graduate School of Business (2012) [^1].

Prior to founding Nubank, Vélez worked in investment banking at Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs and in growth equity and venture capital at General Atlantic [^1]. In 2011, he joined Sequoia Capital as a partner tasked with establishing the firm's Brazil operations [^1]. Sequoia later shelved the Brazil expansion, citing a local shortage of software engineers [^1].

In 2013, at age 31, Vélez co-founded Nubank in São Paulo with Brazilian co-founder Cristina Junqueira and American co-founder Edward Wible [^1]. His motivation stemmed from personal frustration with Brazilian banking bureaucracy: as a foreigner in Brazil he had found opening a bank account "to be overly bureaucratic and expensive" [^1]. The startup launched a no-fee credit card via mobile app, targeting customers dissatisfied with traditional banks. Nubank secured early backing from Sequoia Capital and Kaszek Ventures, raising approximately 600 million Brazilian reais plus $14.3 million across early rounds [^2].

Nubank crossed 12 million customers in 2019, expanded into Mexico and Colombia in 2021, and went public on the NYSE in December 2021 at a first-day valuation of roughly $45 billion [^1] [^2]. By 2025 the bank reported approximately 131 million customers across three countries, $16.3 billion in revenue and $2.87 billion in net income [^2]. In January 2026, Nubank received conditional U.S. banking approval [^2].

In August 2021, Vélez and his wife Mariel Reyes Milk signed The Giving Pledge, committing to donate the majority of their wealth to philanthropy, primarily focused on Latin American children and young adults [^3]. In 2022 they launched VelezReyes+, a family philanthropic platform [^1]. As of 2025, Forbes ranked Vélez 236th globally with an estimated net worth of $10.7 billion [^1]. In September 2024, the Colombian Congress awarded him the Grand Knight rank of the Simón Bolívar Order of Democracy [^1].
