## Background

Ian Hogarth is a British investor, entrepreneur, and AI policy figure. He studied information engineering at the University of Cambridge (first-class honors), specializing in machine learning during his Master's, and spent a year at Tsinghua University in Beijing studying Mandarin Chinese [^1][^2]. He built an early computer-vision cancer classification system as a student in 2004 [^2].

In 2007 Hogarth co-founded the concert discovery platform Songkick with Michelle You and Pete Smith, as part of Y Combinator's Boston cohort [^1][^3]. Songkick scaled to roughly 12-15 million monthly unique visitors, raised more than $20M in venture capital, and famously sued Ticketmaster for anti-competitive behavior; the suit settled in 2018 for $110M plus a $10M criminal penalty paid by Ticketmaster to the U.S. Department of Justice [^1][^3]. He also co-founded Silicon Milkroundabout in 2010, a startup careers fair in East London [^1].

Hogarth has been an active angel investor since 2014, primarily backing machine-learning companies; he reports investments in 50+ ML startups and 150+ companies overall [^4][^2]. In 2021 he co-founded Plural Platform with Wise co-founder Taavet Hinrikus, former Skype executive Sten Tamkivi, and former Bigpoint CEO Khaled Helioui, where he is a Partner [^3][^1]. Plural launched a €250M entrepreneur-led fund for European tech startups in 2022 [^5].

Since 2018, Hogarth has co-authored the annual State of AI Report with Nathan Benaich, which he describes as "the most widely read, free to access report on progress in AI" [^2][^1]. In April 2023 he authored the widely circulated Financial Times essay "We must slow down the race to God-like AI" [^1]. On 18 June 2023 the UK government appointed him as the first Chair of its Frontier AI Taskforce, which evolved into the AI Safety Institute (now the AI Security Institute) [^6][^1]. He was named to TIME's TIME100 AI list in 2023 and appointed Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) [^7][^1].

## Stated Thesis

On Plural's site, Hogarth describes himself as drawn to "general-purpose technologies" capable of transforming entire industries — specifically machine learning, quantum computing, and materials science — and to founders tackling challenges including climate change and online safety for children [^3]. On his personal site he frames the focus as "frontier technology and science investments" [^2].

His most-cited investing maxim, repeated across interviews, is to "spend a disproportionate amount of time recruiting extraordinary people" [^3].

In his April 2023 FT essay, he publicly argued that the race toward artificial general intelligence carries existential risk and that society — and, by implication, investors — should slow the pace of frontier model development absent stronger safety measures [^1]. This stance is unusual for an active AI investor and is a defining feature of his public persona.

## Inferred Thesis

Based on the verified portfolio entries below (sample size ~12 companies — too small for reliable percentages, so this is qualitative):

- **Deep-tech and frontier-science bias.** Verified investments cluster in quantum computing (Phasecraft, Proxima Fusion's adjacent fusion category), AI infrastructure and applications (Anthropic, Helsing, Kheiron Medical, Oriole Networks), and applied ML across vertical domains (drug discovery, defense, healthcare). This is consistent with the stated "general-purpose technologies" framing rather than a divergence from it.
- **UK / European focus.** Almost every verified investment is in a UK or European company (Phasecraft, Helsing, Kheiron Medical, Oriole Networks, Hopin, Cleo, Songkick alumni network). Plural is explicitly a European fund [^5].
- **Founders with deep technical credentials.** Verified portfolio companies are predominantly founded by PhDs or domain specialists (Phasecraft — quantum researchers; Proxima Fusion — Max Planck spinout; Helsing — ex-Palantir/defense; Kheiron — radiology PhDs).
- **Stage:** Mix of pre-seed/seed angel checks (Anthropic in early rounds, Cleo, Chorus AI) and Plural-led Series A rounds (Oriole Networks £17M Series A in 2024) [^8].
- **Public-policy proximity is part of the deal flow.** Hogarth's roles at AISI and UCL IIPP put him in direct contact with frontier AI labs, regulators, and government AI customers — which appears in his portfolio via defense AI (Helsing) and frontier-model infrastructure (Anthropic, Oriole).

Notable observable gap: he publicly argues for slowing the race to AGI but has invested in Anthropic, a frontier AI lab. He has discussed this tension publicly (see Sifted interview [^4]) rather than hiding it.
