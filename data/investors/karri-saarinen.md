## Background

Karri Saarinen is a Finnish-born designer-turned-founder, currently Co-Founder and CEO of Linear, the issue-tracking and product development tool he started in 2019 with Tuomas Artman (CTO) and Jori Lallo (CPO) [^1]. Before Linear, Saarinen was Principal Designer and co-creator of the design systems team at Airbnb, and earlier the founding designer (and Head of Design) at Coinbase [^2][^3].

Earlier in his career he co-founded Kippt (a Y Combinator-backed bookmarking startup) and the educational nonprofit Rails Girls, and contributed to projects including the Airbnb Cereal typeface and the Lottie animation library [^2]. He relocated from San Francisco to Del Mar, California (north of San Diego) in 2020 [^4].

Linear has raised approximately $134M across four rounds and reached unicorn status in 2025 with an $82M Series C [^5]. Alongside running Linear, Saarinen makes occasional angel investments in design- and craft-driven developer tools and productivity software [^6][^7][^8].

## Stated Thesis

Saarinen does not publish a formal angel-investing thesis. His public writing and interviews focus on what he calls "craft" — the deliberate pursuit of quality as a product strategy. In a Figma essay he argues:

> "Quality isn't binary — it's about continuously refining a product to meet a standard." [^9]

> "For quality, you need a team that views the spec as the baseline, not the finish line." [^9]

> "The best design is opinionated." [^9]

His investments to date map closely to companies that share this aesthetic-and-engineering posture — purpose-built, design-led developer and productivity tools (Raycast, Granola, Foxglove) — but Saarinen has not framed this publicly as a deliberate "thesis" the way professional VCs do [^6][^7][^8].

## Inferred Thesis

**Sample size is small (3 publicly verified angel investments), so percentages are not meaningful — qualitative description only.**

Across the three publicly verified angel checks, every company shares a tightly overlapping profile:

- **Sector:** All three are productivity/developer tools with a strong design and craft orientation. Raycast is a keyboard-driven productivity launcher, Granola is an AI note-taking app, Foxglove builds visualization tools for robotics developers [^6][^7][^8].
- **Stage:** All three checks went into seed or Series A rounds led by established VCs (Accel/Coatue at Raycast, Amplify at Foxglove, NFDG at Granola Series B follow-on) — Saarinen comes in as a small operator-angel alongside other founders [^6][^7][^8].
- **Co-investor pattern:** He repeatedly appears alongside founder-angels in the same craft-driven cohort: Koen Bok (Framer), David Cramer (Sentry), Guillermo Rauch (Vercel), Amjad Masad (Replit), Tobi Lütke (Shopify), Des Traynor (Intercom), Zach Lloyd (Warp), Lenny Rachitsky [^7][^8].
- **Geography:** US- and UK-based companies, distributed/remote-leaning teams (Granola is UK-based) [^6][^8].
- **Founder profile:** Technical and/or design founders building tools they themselves want to use — a pattern that mirrors Linear's own origin story.

**Gap:** Saarinen is not a prolific angel. Aggregators list only 4 disclosed investments through May 2025 [^6]. Founders should expect his involvement to be selective and low-touch rather than a programmatic deployment.

## In Their Own Words

On building Linear and quality as a strategy:

> "We started with quality. Then we learned that people actually noticed, because it's a rare approach — especially for startups." [^9]

On how he thinks about meeting a user's bar:

> "You have to match that level of capability or quality, otherwise people just don't pay attention." [^9]

On product opinion:

> "The best design is opinionated." [^9]

On fundraising philosophy (in a thread about how he approached Linear's rounds):

> "This is what I did with first company. But then with Linear have done opposite. Not starting fundraising until there is already lot of interest. Then only engaging with the few investors that seem a good fit. To this day we never needed to fundraise, in the beginning we could…" [^12]

On Granola, explaining why he invested and uses it:

> "For me, @meetgranola was one of those things that just clicked instantly. It's seamless and very purpose-built. I like that it's a focused app that sits on the side, and the notes don't disappear into some endless pile of miscellaneous information and docs." [^13]

On Carta (after the secondary-share outreach incident in early 2024), a public statement that signaled how he thinks about founder/investor trust:

> "This might be the end of @cartainc as the trusted platform for startups. As a founder it feels kind shitty that Carta, who I trust to manage our cap table, is now doing cold outreach to our angel investors about selling Linear shares to their buyers." [^14]
