## Background

Nithin Kamath (born October 5, 1979) is a Bengaluru-based entrepreneur best known as the founder and CEO of Zerodha, the discount brokerage he co-founded with his younger brother Nikhil Kamath in 2010 [^1]. Before Zerodha, Nithin traded his father's portfolio from age 17, worked as a senior tele-sales associate at a call centre while trading during the day, and operated a sub-broker franchise under the name Kamath Associates from 2004 to 2010 [^1]. He holds a degree from Bangalore Institute of Technology [^1].

Zerodha launched the ₹20-per-trade flat-fee model that displaced India's traditional percentage-based broking structure and grew to roughly 1.5 crore (15M) customers without advertising while keeping headcount at ~1,200 employees [^2]. Nithin received the Forbes India Leadership Award in 2019 and was named among The Economic Times' Top 10 Businessmen in India in 2016 [^1].

In 2016 he launched Rainmatter, the investing arm of Zerodha, originally framed as a fintech incubator and now an "initiative by the same people who brought you Zerodha" backing founders in fintech, climate, health, and media [^3][^4]. Rainmatter is structured as a perennial vehicle with ₹1,000 Cr earmarked for investments, deploys cheques between ₹50 lakh and ₹100 Cr, and explicitly does not take board seats or impose exit timelines [^3][^4]. The separate Rainmatter Foundation has committed $200M in grants for climate and environmental work [^3]. As of April 2026, Rainmatter had deployed approximately ₹1,500 Cr (~$180M) across 160+ startups [^5].

## Stated Thesis

Rainmatter's official site states the thesis directly: "We back Indian founders that are working on helping Indians make better choices with their money and health... We back founders in fintech, climate, health, and media... We invest anywhere between ₹50 lakhs to ₹100 crores" [^3].

Nithin frames Rainmatter as patient capital with no exit mandate. On the official Zerodha "Approaching a decade" retrospective, he wrote: "what is the use of all the money in the world if we keep destroying our health and polluting the one place we can call home?" [^4]. In a 2026 interview he reiterated: "We're not a typical VC. We don't take board seats, and we're not in this for quick exits… our approach has been simple: be patient, back founders for the long term" [^5].

He also publicly describes his evaluation lens as founder-first rather than market-first: "At the end of the day, you're betting on the person. You're betting on the founders of the company" [^6].

## Inferred Thesis

The official Rainmatter portfolio page lists 146 companies grouped as 45 Climate (30.8%), 41 Health & Fitness (28.1%), 37 Fintech (25.3%), 15 Other (10.3%), and 8 Media (5.5%) [^7]. This breakdown — counted from the firm's own portfolio page — is the cleanest data available and matches the stated thesis category set (fintech, climate, health, media) but reveals two patterns the stated thesis under-emphasizes:

- **Climate has overtaken fintech as the largest category.** The stated thesis lists fintech first (and Rainmatter began as a fintech-only fund [^8]), but actual deployment now skews climate-heaviest. The Z-Connect retrospective for 2024 alone shows ₹120 Cr deployed into 15 climate deals — the single largest sector spend of that year [^9].
- **Health & Fitness is nearly as large as fintech.** With 41 portfolio companies, it sits within three of fintech's 37 count, despite less marketing emphasis. The 2024 cohort included 16 healthcare deals [^9].

**Round size: bimodal.** Rainmatter writes both small seed cheques (e.g., ₹6 Cr lead in The Land Bankers, June 2026 [^10]) and large follow-on cheques (e.g., ₹56 Cr Pre-Series A lead in Karo Sambhav, June 2026 [^11]; ₹25 Cr in Agilitas's ₹225 Cr round, June 2026 [^12]). The stated ₹50 lakh–₹100 Cr range plays out in practice as two distinct behaviours: small first cheques and concentrated follow-ons in existing portfolio companies or aligned theses.

**Lead behaviour.** Where data is available in 2024-2026 rounds, Rainmatter frequently leads or anchors: lead investor in Karo Sambhav (Pre-Series A) [^11], lead in The Land Bankers (seed) [^10], primary funder in TruNativ and Boson Whitewater (₹10 Cr each, September 2024) [^13], and first institutional investor in Capitalmind (Series A, August 2025) [^14][^15]. Rainmatter is more often a lead/co-lead than a passive participant.

**Geographic concentration.** Almost exclusively India-headquartered companies, consistent with the stated mission of helping Indians manage money and health [^3].

**Governance pattern (verified, unusual).** Rainmatter explicitly does not take board seats. Nithin wrote on Z-Connect: "We thus avoid taking board seats or being involved in the operations of businesses that we partner with, and instead, we help, mentor, and collaborate" [^16]. The Capitalmind investment was structured with stake capped at 10% per SEBI norms, with no board seat — explicitly to preserve Capitalmind's independence [^15].

Sample size note: the 146-company breakdown is sourced from Rainmatter's own portfolio page; this is the inferred-thesis denominator. The specific 2024-2026 round-level details below are individual verified deals that illustrate the pattern but are a small fraction of total deal flow.
