## About

Stepful is a New York-based healthcare workforce training platform founded in 2021 by Carl Madi, Tressia Hobeika, and Edoardo Serra [^1]. The company runs what it calls "school-as-a-service": short, AI-assisted online training programs (typically four months) that take students with a high school diploma and place them into roles such as medical assistants, pharmacy technicians, phlebotomists, and nursing assistants [^1][^2]. Programs are sold through employer sponsorships with health systems, making them debt-free for trainees [^3].

As of the Series C announcement, Stepful had graduated more than 32,000 healthcare workers and partnered with 35+ major health systems, including Mount Sinai, Ochsner, and Providence [^3][^4]. The company is a Y Combinator alum and went through the accelerator's W21 batch [^1].

## What Investors Say

Vig Chandramouli, Partner at Oak HC/FT, on leading the Series C:

> "Stepful is the only company we have seen that combines online education with a sophisticated AI engine to solve the talent supply problem at scale." [^4][^5]

Jomayra Herrera, Partner at Reach Capital, who has worked with Stepful since its earliest stages, is identified by CEO Carl Madi as a partner who treats the company as her own [^8].

## What Founders Say

Carl Madi, CEO and Co-Founder, on the Series C and Stepful's positioning:

> "Stepful is not just a training platform - we are the foundational infrastructure for the future of the healthcare workforce." [^4]

Madi has also described the company as "both an academic institution and a technology company" centered on employers, with the new funding earmarked to expand hospital partnerships, launch advanced degree programs (including registered nursing and imaging), and accelerate AI capabilities [^5].

On Stepful's earliest investors, Madi has said of Reach Capital:

> "Reach has been with us since day one. They're the kind of investors who say 'we' when they talk about Stepful." [^8]
